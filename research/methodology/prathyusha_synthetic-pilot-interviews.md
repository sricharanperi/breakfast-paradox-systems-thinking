# Critique — third agent's review of both transcripts against the interview guide and existing assumptions

Produced by a third agent role-playing an experienced, skeptical UX researcher, reviewing
the two transcripts above against Guide 1 and the 🟡-flagged assumptions in
`prathyusha_02-stakeholder-map.md`, `prathyusha_03-power-interest-leverage-map.md`, and
`prathyusha_05-system-timeline-bot-map.md`. Guide 1 itself has already been rewritten
per §1 below — see `prathyusha_interview-guide-phase1-halfday.md` for the live version;
this is the record of *why* it changed.

## 1. Weak/leading questions (now fixed in the live guide)

- **"Do you feel students have any real say... or is it one-directional?"** — a false
  binary; both personas converged on near-identical "mostly one-directional" phrasing,
  suggesting the question anchors the answer rather than eliciting it independently.
- **"does it actually change anything for you, or just feel symbolic?"** — same
  binary-anchor problem; both picked "symbolic" almost verbatim.
- **"did a roommate or friend wake you, ask you to come, or go without you?"** — a
  compound multiple-choice probe; both personas just selected from the listed options
  instead of describing freely.
- **Half-hour crowd guess** — not leading in wording, but both personas produced a
  confident, structured answer despite self-flagging near-zero visibility; the question
  invites false precision instead of licensing "I don't know."

## 2. Follow-up probes for real interviews today

- **Companion effect:** "If your roommate/regular breakfast companion were away for a
  week, what do you think would happen to your attendance?" / "Has there been a stretch
  without them — what actually happened?"
- **Crowd guess:** push for direct evidence, not impression: "Have you personally seen
  an item run out — which one, roughly what time, how do you know?"
- **Attendance trend:** "Can you point to a specific week or event when your habits
  changed?" — pin a date, not a vague impression (needed because the two synthetic
  transcripts gave *opposite* trends).
- **Skip Meal vs. Cancel confusion:** ask the respondent to open the app and describe
  the difference live — tests whether this is real UI ambiguity or an invented detail.
- **Mess Cell scale:** "Roughly how many times have you bought/sold/seen a transaction
  in the group in the last month?" — tests the stakeholder map's "Monitor vs. Manage
  Closely" question for that actor.
- **Cost salience:** "How much money do you think you've spent this semester on
  breakfasts you didn't eat?" — tests whether concretizing the number changes the
  "doesn't feel like a big deal" framing.
- **Lever skepticism:** "If breakfast started an hour later, do you think that would
  change anything for you?" — directly tests Transcript 2's claim that timing isn't the
  real driver.

## 3. Cross-check against 🟡 assumptions — hypotheses to verify, not findings

- **Friend/roommate influence** (stakeholder map): both personas strongly affirm it. IF
  real respondents say similarly, that supports the 🟡 flag — but convergence between
  two AI-generated transcripts proves nothing on its own.
- **"Students have no real say"** (power-interest map): both synthetic answers
  converged — but per §1, that may be the question leading, not the truth. A real
  answer matching it should still be treated cautiously.
- **BOT map §A curve (8-8:30 peak):** both personas guessed a shape nearly identical to
  the existing hypothesis table — likely because the reasoning ("closest to 8:30 class")
  is generic, not independent evidence. Convergence here shouldn't raise confidence; a
  *different* real answer would be the more informative outcome.
- **Bakul/catering-split hypothesis:** untouched by Guide 1 — no signal either way,
  still fully dependent on tomorrow's Role C/D interview.
- **Academic Office "sleeping stakeholder":** both personas gave hazy, uncertain recall
  of anyone raising timing changes. IF real students are similarly vague, that supports
  the "no real channel" story.

## 4. New angles the synthetic run surfaced

- **Attendance reportedly *increasing*, not decreasing, over the semester**
  (Transcript 1) — directly contradicts the "Breakfast Paradox" framing itself. Worth
  testing explicitly today; if several real Role-A respondents report flat/rising
  attendance, the core problem framing may need revisiting in Phase 2.
- **Batch weekly registration** — both personas register for the whole week in one
  Sunday sitting, decoupling the registration decision from the morning-of attendance
  decision. Not previously flagged anywhere in the docs; if common, this mechanically
  explains a lot of the no-show pattern on its own (people aren't deciding daily whether
  to eat — they're deciding weekly whether to register, then separately deciding daily
  whether to walk over).
- **"It's not a system-design thing, it's willpower" (Transcript 2)** — directly
  challenges whether shifting mess/class hours (the Academic Office lever) would fix
  anything at all. Worth testing as a real possibility today, not dismissing.
