## 1. Kadamba (vendor: Prism)

### 1.1 Governance and command chain. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
**CFS → CDS (head: Raju) → contractor Prism → operations manager → manager → supervisor → service staff.**
- Prism is Kadamba's outsourced third-party vendor. It wins the contract by tender bid, registers as a vendor, and runs **all** operations at Kadamba.
- **Name conflict to flag (D-24):** earlier research (braindump #2, the CFS Chair interview) names the **CDS Chair as Giri**. This account names **Raju as the CDS head**. These may be two different roles (e.g. an administrative chair vs an operational head) or one of the names may be wrong. Do not merge or pick one; flag it as open.
- The supervisor constantly checks the counters and tells the kitchen how much to cook.

### 1.2 How quantity is decided. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- The **first batch is 70% of registrations**, planned separately for veg and non-veg (example: 400 non-veg + 300 veg registrations). This upgrades Brief #1's *(unclear)* 70% to an observer-verified operator statement.
- **Portions are recorded per dish in grams.** Idli is 4 per person (120 g of flour/batter makes 4 idlis; the account says "wheat flour", which is probably a slip for idli batter); sambar is 100 g per person. Poha, pulao and similar dishes have their own gram standards.
- **Items that "float"** (people take more than the standard): bonda, puri, vada, idli.
- **In-service top-up loop:** staff watch how much of each item is left at what time and tell the chef how many more portions to cook. The judgement combines registrations with turnout so far. The team's own illustration: if a 50% first batch runs out in the first 45 minutes, a quarter of the diners have eaten half the food, so the remainder is cooked against the observed flow.
- **Arrival pattern as the operator describes it:** about 100 in the opening phase, about 100 through the middle, and **about 150 in the last 10 minutes**. "For any meal the highest flow is in the last 10 minutes." This matches the shape of the April scan records (26.5% of Kadamba breakfast scans fall in 9:15–9:30; see Brief #1). The records are the measurement; this account is the operator's rule of thumb.
- **Biryani lunch is cooked at 100%** of registrations, not 70%. Standard serving is 2 pieces at 150–200 g per piece, with unlimited rice. When some diners get 3 pieces, later diners run short. A head chef comes in for biryani days. (This is lunch, not breakfast; relevant as the contrast case where full preparation is the norm.)
- **Kadamba cooks on registrations, full stop:** "it doesn't care if people don't want to eat because it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it." **Conflict to flag:** earlier project findings (confirmed by the user on 2026-09-13) say Skip Meal declarations do reach the kitchen for same-week prep. Possible reconciliation: Skip Meal may adjust the *registration count* the operator sees, while the operator's attitude is that the count, whatever it is, is the obligation. Needs one direct question.

### 1.3 Extra mouths fed from the same food. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- About **30 extra portions** are cooked for people outside the student registration: roughly **30–50 people**, e.g. night-shift security who do not want to leave to eat.
- **About 50% of Kadamba's internal staff also eat at the mess.**
- Together these form a substantial, uncounted sink for first-batch surplus (candidate link to the "5–10% wastage" figure in §1.5).

### 1.4 Food safety and quality sequence. Confirmed (observed) for the display plate and tasting; single-sourced for the rest
Cook → **cooking temperature checked** → **sample retained 72 hours** (to test the food if anyone falls ill, including to tell whether the illness came from outside food) → **display plate at the entrance** for every meal, carefully wrapped, so diners see the full menu before entering → **serving temperature measured per item** → **tasting** by the facility team together with 2 people from Prism → corrections ("repairs") → service.
- **QC inspects weekly.**
- **Water:** TDS is read daily for both cooking and drinking water, and any failure triggers a full check.
- **Team observer's quality judgement (the team's own assessment, not the operator's):** Kadamba's main quality problem is that food is **cooked ahead, reheated repeatedly, and transferred between vessels** for display and service, so it degrades fast. This is a candidate mechanism linking the 70%-plus-top-up model to quality complaints: the surplus from each batch is held and reheated rather than discarded.

### 1.5 Waste. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- **About 5–10% daily wastage** (operator figure; the base is not stated, i.e. per meal or per day, and whether by weight of cooked food).
- **Tension to validate (important for Phase 3):** a 70% first batch against 37% recorded breakfast turnout (Kadamba, April) would leave a large surplus if nothing else happened. The 5–10% figure is compatible only if (a) most of the surplus is absorbed by the extra mouths (§1.3), by reheating into later service (§1.4), or by top-ups being smaller than the first batch implies, or (b) the waste percentage is measured on a different base (all meals, or plate waste only). Brief #1's disposal route still stands: dustbin → garbage contractor, no composting.

### 1.6 Timing and procurement. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- **Breakfast:** the chef arrives at **5:30 am** and cooks batch items (sambar, upma). **Live items (dosa etc.) are cooked in front of diners from 7:00** so they are hot.
- **Procurement tiers:** dry goods (rice, pally/groundnut, putna, masala, ghee, podi) are sourced **one week ahead**; perishables (milk, fruit, meat, eggs, most vegetables) **the day before**; some vegetables **2–3 days ahead**.

### 1.7 Registration from the student side. Confirmed (observed as a student) + confirmed against earlier records
- Registration is **mandatory and automatic**. Students are registered whether or not they are on campus, including over holidays, and **the only way out is 5 cancellations per month** (per meal type, made at least 4 days ahead, per the CDS poster already on record). Beyond those, "we don't have any opportunity to nullify it."
- The Skip Meal toggle (on record from the portal screenshots) tells the kitchen not to prepare but does **not** remove the charge. The team member's account ("no opportunity to nullify") describes the charge, which is consistent.
- **Late arrivals:** service closes at 9:30. When the front door closes, people come in through the **back door** after 9:30 and demand food, and the mess cannot refuse them. Last-minute registrations are described as "a pain."

### 1.8 People
- **Nazir:** main veg chef. A head chef joins for biryani days.

## 2. Yuktahaar (vendor: ABC Hospitality Services)

### 2.1 Organisation. Confirmed (observed) + single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- **Vegetarian only**, with Jain service alongside regular (the registers split "regular + Jain").
- The vendor runs a **project management team of two people** (team account). **Reconciliation with Brief #1:** the site head's recording mentioned a five-person group ("three from this team and two…"). Most consistent reading: **two vendor project managers plus about three site staff** involved in project management work. Use "two project managers" in deliverables.
- **No hierarchy, everyone does every job:** there is no dedicated storekeeper-only role, and cross-checking is done by peers.
- **Ajita** is the in-charge of the mess, keeps the accounts, and **keeps the Wastage Register**. Her records are cross-checked by other staff. (The speaker first said "Sylaja" and corrected it to Ajita. The recording's "Adit Amma" is very likely the same person: *amma* is a Telugu honorific. Candidate, not merged.) **Bhavani** cross-verifies and writes the grocery register (recording; and her signature is on the photographed Fridge 3 cleaning checklist as "M. Bhavani").
- **Kitchen teams:** tasting, cutting, cleaning (plus the store function and the coordinator).
- **No formal leave policy:** anyone who needs leave raises it in the daily meeting and colleagues agree to absorb the load.
- **Team building:** one project manager took all staff to his farmhouse for training on what good quality looks like, how to think and what responsibilities to take. Staff describe a family-like culture (e.g. colleagues advising a staff member to buy a shirt as a gift for a visiting relative).
- **Change management:** when waste weighing started, staff feared being blamed for mistakes. The managers deliberately made it blame-free, discussed it daily, and staff themselves later reported that waste had fallen (Brief #1: started in August, and by December staff said waste had reduced).

### 2.3 The Yuktahaar planning and forecasting cycle. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified) + confirmed (records)
1. **Daily meetings** (the first half of the day for tomorrow's breakfast and lunch, the second half for snacks and dinner). Everyone discusses the day's positives, negatives and discrepancies, including excess dirt or stones found while cleaning raw materials (reported to the store; they hold an expected grams-of-waste-per-raw-item norm, and exceeding it means complaining to the supplier or changing supplier).
2. **Turnout estimate, set before service:** the menu repeats (weeks 1 & 3, and 2 & 4), so they look up the matching week's run-out times and quantities in the Wastage Book, the month's turnout pattern, and any holidays, special weeks or campus events. Registrations are read as regular + Jain + cash meals.
3. **Turnout re-estimate, during service (new in this account):** **the first half hour of service is used as a sample** to project how many will come for the rest of the meal, which then drives top-up cooking.
4. **Issue:** ingredient list per dish (kg) → the store pulls, **segregates and cleans** (raw materials are bought the week before from many local shops and godowns, so stones and debris are expected) → issued to the kitchen.
5. **Cook:** breakfast cooking starts **5:30 am, ready by 7:30**. Mostly one batch; poha in two. Rotis are handmade with no oil (diners add ghee), and **two roti counters open at peak**.
6. **Reuse:** leftover idli becomes idli upma or idli poha for lunch; batter goes back into the fridge; surplus milk becomes curd.
7. **Record:** the Wastage Book, grocery balance, vegetable balance and utilities, closing the loop back to step 2.

**Operator's turnout beliefs vs records:** breakfast "max 50%" (operator claim, repeated in this account) vs **17.7–40.2%, mean about 28% in their own September register** (confirmed, records). Lunch "> 70%" is confirmed by the register (56–89%, 15 of 20 days ≥ 70%). Paneer dinner "> 90%" is operator claim only (no dinner records seen).

### 2.4 Quality, cost and data stance. Single-sourced (operator, observer-verified)
- Rock (pink) salt only and cold-pressed oils, **"for the previous six months"** (team account; Brief #1's recording said since the operator took over last year, which is compatible if the change came some months after takeover). The operator visits the mills to check how oil and other inputs are made.
- **The project management goal is explicit:** best food quality, less waste, an optimised workflow, lower operating cost, standardisation and **higher profit**. Waste reduction is a cost lever for the vendor, not only a quality one.
- **ERP decision:** they tried **Zoho ERP** to streamline groceries and predict rush. They judged that paying "lakhs of rupees" for prediction made no sense when the data already sits in their books, weighing the cost of the tool against their own data and against turnout. They chose to stay "very close to the data." A second constraint: **staff are not digitally inclined**, and there are not enough digitisation staff. Office staff digitise later.

## 4. New or sharpened mechanisms for Phases 2 and 3 (candidates)
- **Two different forecasting philosophies side by side:**
  - *Kadamba:* a fixed ratio to registrations plus reactive top-up, with no learning record beyond per-dish grams.
  - *Yuktahaar:* matching-week learning from its own books, plus an in-service projection from the first half hour.

  The contrast is the project's cleanest natural comparison. Yuktahaar's first-half-hour sample is still vulnerable to the closing crest, where about a quarter of diners arrive in the last 15 minutes (April records).
- **Surplus-to-reheat quality chain at Kadamba:** over-preparation → held and reheated food → quality degradation → (candidate) more skipping and complaints. The closing link to skipping is **not** evidenced; it is a chain, not a loop (D-40).
- **Surplus absorbed by extra mouths:** 30–50 outside diners plus half the staff eat from the same food. This is an absorption mechanism in the D-45 sense: an intervention that cut first-batch surplus would take food from these people unless it plans for them explicitly.
- **Vendor incentive:** Yuktahaar's goal statement ties waste reduction to profit, while Kadamba's "we are paid for the registration" ties preparation to registrations. The same billing basis produces opposite operator stances depending on management culture. This is a candidate reason why operator culture is itself a leverage point.
- **Blame-free measurement as a precondition:** Yuktahaar's waste measurement only worked once it was made blame-free. This is directly relevant to any intervention that introduces new measurement.
- **Automatic registration without an off-switch (the student side):** mandatory auto-registration with a cap of 5 cancellations → the booking is a default, not a choice → confirms the "standing booking" root cause from the student's own vantage point.
