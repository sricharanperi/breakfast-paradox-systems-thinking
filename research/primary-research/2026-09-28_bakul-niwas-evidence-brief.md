## 1. What the recordings establish (OBSERVED = stated by the operator)

### 1.1 Identity and supply chain. Single-sourced (operator, team synthesis)
- The mess on recordings 54/55 **is Bakul Niwas**. Operator: **Vijayalakshmi Caterers**. This resolves the long-open candidate "which mess does Vijayalakshmi serve" for Bakul. **Palash is not mentioned**; that Palash is served by the same operator/kitchen stays a candidate (earlier sources: Bakul and Palash share one off-site kitchen until the Felicity facility opens, ~Dec 2026).
- Food is cooked at the operator's **Hafizpet kitchen, about 8 km from Bakul**. Travel about **40 minutes normally, about 20 minutes early in the morning** (little traffic).
- Food travels in insulated **Cambro-type boxes**; stated holding range about **60-72 °C**. At the mess the boxes sit in a **hot-water / steam setup** to keep temperature.
- **Monthly payment is based on the number of plates served** (see conflict C5; this is the D-54 hinge).

### 1.2 How the first batch is set. Single-sourced
- The mess does **not** cook for 100% of registrations. For 100 registrations it may cook for about **70-80%**; **the exact share depends on the item**.
- The manager sets it from **historical attendance patterns, reviewed week by week** (illustration given: if Monday historically sees ~70% attendance, cook ~80% of registrations that day). Whether this history is written down or held in the manager's head is **not stated** (open, Q-B7).

### 1.3 In-service top-up. Single-sourced
- About **every 30 minutes** the manager checks food remaining, registrations and expected remaining demand.
- If more is needed he **phones the Hafizpet kitchen** with an approximate quantity; the kitchen cooks it and it is transported. Top-up lead time is **not stated** in this source (Q-B4).
- Operating model: registrations → historical attendance → initial batch → observe consumption → request an additional batch.

### 1.4 The operator's goal. Single-sourced (explicit operating principle)
- **"There should not be a shortage of food, even if some wastage occurs."** Priority: no shortage over minimum waste.
- Even on **weekends, when breakfast attendance is "very low"**, the manager keeps food for about **70% of registered students**.

### 1.5 Breakfast turnout and the manager's explanations. Single-sourced (estimate, no records)
- **At most about 60%** of registered students come to breakfast (manager's estimate).
- The manager offers two reasons: **(a) students may not like the taste/menu; (b) students may not wake up or may be reluctant to come.** These are his hypotheses (H1, H2), not student evidence.

### 1.6 Item- and menu-dependent demand. Single-sourced
- **Pav bhaji** cited as an item that brings more students to breakfast.
- **Muesli and Chocos** are consumed readily; quantities like 1-2 kg or 3-4 kg "go easily".

### 1.7 Breakfast timing at Bakul. Single-sourced
- Main rush about **8:00-8:30**. About **10-15 people arrive in the last 10 minutes**.
- The manager aims to still have food for **about 50 people around 9:20**.

### 1.8 Menu planning. Single-sourced (the manager's view)
- **Students select the menu combinations**, and the manager thinks some are inappropriate: the problem is **pairings**, not individual dishes. Examples: kachori should go with chole; Raj Kachori is listed with chana where he would pair aloo curry.
- Students (in his account) reason from **balanced diet, protein, carbohydrate and individual preference**; he reasons from **traditional/regional combinations, taste and what people actually eat**, citing **~20 years' experience with North Indian food**. He believes North and South Indian patterns differ (curry/roti/rice balance).
- He mentioned a target of about **30-40 g of protein per meal** (who set it and how it is computed are unknown, Q-B10).

### 1.9 Early preparation and texture. Single-sourced
- For labour-heavy items such as **aloo paratha**, preparation may start **around 3 AM**, targeting arrival at Bakul **around 7 AM**.
- Hot parathas in the insulated box produce steam that **softens them**: food arrives hot but less pleasant to eat. The system preserves **temperature** well and can degrade **texture**.

## 4. Interpretations (candidate; may be used in prose only with the candidate label)

- **I1. Bakul's first batch exceeds breakfast turnout by design.** On the manager's own numbers the first batch (70-80%) and the weekend floor (~70%) sit above the highest breakfast turnout he expects (~60%). If Bakul's real turnout resembles the recorded halls (28-37%), the gap is larger. No records exist to size it.
- **I2. The 9:20 reserve is a structural consequence of the transport delay.** A top-up needs a phone call, cooking and a 20-40 minute drive. Late in the window a top-up cannot arrive before close, so the only hedge against late arrivals is food already held. The reserve (~50) is several times the late arrivals the manager describes (10-15 in the last 10 minutes). The difference is a shortage-insurance premium.
- **I3. The shortage floor is the kitchen's answer to loudness.** A run-out is visible and routes a complaint (CDS team → manager → MD); a surplus is silent. The operator's stated principle is the kitchen-level form of the system's "only reacts to what is loud" condition.
- **I4. If Bakul is paid on plates served, the operator bears the cost of over-cooking and accepts it,** which implies it judges a shortage costlier than waste (complaint, penalty or contract risk; which of these is unknown). It also implies that lowering registrations (LP2) would not cut Bakul's revenue, so Bakul's rational position on a booking-exit change is likely neutral or favourable (to be marked **simulated** in Activity 10). Held behind C5.
- **I5. Holding food hot for hours is a quality cost of both early dispatch and the late reserve.** The food kept for late arrivals is the food held longest, so late diners at Bakul likely get the softest paratha. Any lever that holds more food longer (a bigger reserve, earlier dispatch) trades waste or shortage against texture.
- **I6. Bakul's manager holds two kinds of expertise the menu process does not use:** consumption by item (what goes, what is left) and pairing knowledge. Whether his pairing view matches Bakul residents' preferences is untested (H4); it must not be treated as student truth.
