                    JEE coaching phenomenon
                    Systems Problem Statement
                    The JEE preparation system has evolved into a self-reinforcing ecosystem in

Leverage in Systems Thinking
Leverage is the idea that a small, well-chosen change in a system can produce a
disproportionately large and lasting impact.
In simple terms:
Leverage means finding the right place to intervene in a system—not simply applying more
effort.

A system may have hundreds of visible problems. We can spend enormous resources fixing
symptoms, yet little changes. Systems Thinking asks:
“Where can we make a change that fundamentally influences the behaviour of the whole system?”
That place is called a leverage point.

Leverage in Systems Thinking

The Basic Idea
Imagine you are trying to improve a system. You have two choices:

Option A: Apply more effort                                      Option B: Find leverage
   Add more people                                                  Change an important relationship
   Spend more money                                                 Improve the flow of information
   Create more processes                                            Modify incentives
   Fix problems repeatedly                                          Change a rule
                                                                    Redefine the goal
The second approach may require less effort but produce greater systemic change.
A simple principle: Not all interventions are equally powerful. Some changes affect only a small
part of the system.

       Six Leverage Points
       Leverage Point 6: Change the Mental Model
       This may be the deepest intervention.

       We need to challenge the equation:
       JEE Rank = Intelligence = Future Success = Personal Worth
       A healthier mental model is:
       JEE is one selection mechanism for one set of educational pathways—not a
       measure of human potential or future success.
       This doesn't require reducing the aspiration for excellence.
       It means decoupling excellence from a single examination outcome.

       But Every Intervention Can Create a New Problem
       Suppose we reduce the importance of one examination. What might happen?
       Coaching institutes may shift to:
           Olympiads
           Interviews
           Portfolio preparation
           School marks
           New entrance examinations
       In other words:
       The coaching industry may adapt.
       Therefore, simply changing the examination may not solve the system.
       This is why we need to anticipate second-order effects.

       Design the Future System Rather Than "Destroy
       Coaching"

       A better design objective would be:
       Create a high-quality, equitable JEE preparation ecosystem
       in which coaching is an option rather than a perceived
       necessity.
       That is a much more achievable systems goal.
           We don't necessarily eliminate coaching.
           We change the conditions that create excessive
           dependence on it.
