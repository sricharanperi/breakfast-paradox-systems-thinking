# What changed and why

The first Phase 2 documents were written before the operator visits and the Kadamba scan records. Each change below is stated once here and applied everywhere in the rebuilt documents.

| First submission said | Now |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Registration is a weekly or monthly standing booking | An automatic default for every student, with five cancellations per meal type per month, each at least four days ahead |
| The vendor forecast locks four days ahead; the four-day lock is the bottleneck | The four-day close applies to registration. Kitchens buy on their own cadences and set quantities the day before. The binding constraint is the cooked first batch |
| The operators are one actor that cooks to a four-day forecast | Three operators, each applying its own ratio to the closed count: a fixed 70% at Kadamba, 70 to 80% by item with a floor near 70% at Bakul, and a book-based lookup at Yuktāhār |
| Bakul's kitchen is near the airport and its ratio was raised from 70 to 80% | Hafizpet, about 8 km, 20 to 40 minutes by road. The raise is withdrawn: the share varies by item and day and is adjusted weekly |
| Bakul is a fixed-ratio kitchen like Kadamba | A third regime between Kadamba and Yuktāhār |
| Composting absorbs the surplus | Kadamba does not compost; its waste goes to a garbage contractor |
| 20 to 30 staff eat leftovers at Kadamba | About 30 extra portions for 30 to 50 outside diners, plus about half of Kadamba's staff |
| Kadamba waste of 16 and 23 kg | Withdrawn. The operator reports 5 to 10% daily on an unstated base |
| Yuktāhār has a five-person project team | Two project managers, with about three site staff sharing the work |
| Prism is Kadamba's floor team | Prism is the vendor that holds the tender and runs all Kadamba operations |
| All three operators describe a closing rush, and Bakul peaks at 8:30 to 9:00 | The crest is recorded at Kadamba and described at Yuktāhār. Bakul's manager places his rush at 8:00 to 8:30 |
| Nothing in the kitchen reacts in real time; no one tracks waste | Supervisors, projections and phoned top-ups react all morning, upward only. Yuktāhār weighs waste per item and meal, and no route carries it to a rule owner |
| Kadamba 51 to 79% and Bakul and Palash 11 to 31% turnout by cuisine | Registrations as a share of capacity from one portal snapshot, not attendance |
| The loops were named R1 and B1 to B4, six of them closed | One scheme, L1 to L17 plus L3b. Three close with every link evidenced (L4, L11, L12), five are candidates, and L6 is a calendar switch, not a loop |
| The gap is about 30% | 62.9% at Kadamba in April and about 72% at Yuktāhār in September, the figure of about 30% fits lunch |
