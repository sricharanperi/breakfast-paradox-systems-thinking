## Headline

The deliverable's biggest structural blind spot: it treats "Vendors" as one undifferentiated, low-interest actor who "produces to the forecast," and it places the system's data with the Mess Office. The operator interviews overturn all three points. The mess operator is the **data-rich, authority-poor** actor. It holds the only per-item, per-weekday record of registered vs eaten vs wasted. It sets the single most consequential parameter in the system: how much of each registration actually gets cooked (Yuktahar ~50% belief, Kadamba 70% first batch, Vijayalakshmi 70 to 80%). Yet it has no authority over the registration cut-off, cancellation rules, billing or menu, and no route by which its data reaches anyone who does. That is a new central power finding. It matters as much as the existing "student controls the input but bears no cost" finding, and it changes which leverage points Phase 3 should target.

## Phase 3 implications (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

1. **Information-flow leverage point (Meadows LP6), the strongest candidate:** a route from the operator's existing records (Wastage Book, weekday attendance register) and the CDS scan data to whoever owns the registration cut-off, cancellation cap and billing rule. The data already exists. The missing element is the flow, not a new measurement.
2. **Parameter leverage point (LP12, fast, low-cost):** recalibrate the first-batch conversion ratio per mess, per weekday and per meal against actual records (Kadamba breakfast 70% → nearer 37 to 42% by weekday, Sunday 27%). This sits inside the operator's existing authority, so it needs no rule change. It is a quick win, but it reinforces the D-45 absorption, because the gap becomes even less visible upstream.
3. **Rule leverage point (LP5):** the mismatch between the student's T-4 cut-off and the operator's T-1 perishables and same-day top-up. Operators themselves question the 4-day cut-off. That makes it a lever with operator-side support, which is rare.
4. **Incentive leverage point (LP5/6), contingent:** the contract payment basis (per registered vs per served plate) decides whether operators gain or lose from the gap. Any intervention recommendation must first resolve G9. Do not design around an assumed payment basis.
5. **Roles leverage point:** give the operator a formal voice in the menu and cut-off (currently consulted-but-not-deciding), and create a formal calendar feed (exams, events, holidays) from the institute to operators, replacing "only if someone mentions it in the meeting."
6. **Intervention point: cross-mess comparability.** Operators misjudge each other (Vijayalakshmi's "Kadamba gets 90 to 95%" vs 37%) and their own figures (Yuktahar 50% vs 28%). A shared, per-mess, breakfast-specific dashboard is a candidate intervention point. Evidence limits apply: records cover Kadamba breakfast April and Yuktahar September only.
7. **Design principle: build on the operator's existing practice, do not replace it with software.** Yuktahar dropped Zoho ("prediction will be based on our data only... I want to remain close to the data"), and its waste reduction came from routine and staff buy-in (August → December). Interventions should augment hand-kept records and meetings, not impose an ERP.
8. **Design principle: make the data visible to the people who hold it, at meal granularity.** Holding data did not stop the Yuktahar head misjudging breakfast by nearly 2 times. Any feedback artefact must show breakfast separately from lunch and dinner.
9. **Design principle (D-45 constraint, reconfirmed):** do not remove absorption mechanisms that benefit real people (staff eating leftovers, the late-door reopening, friends eating in place, leftover reuse). Surface the gap through the records that already exist instead.
10. **Design principle: route changes through actors with both interest and some authority.** The operator + student coalition over the cut-off is the first evidenced shared interest. Academic administration remains power-without-interest, so interventions that need it should ask for low-cost information (the calendar feed), not timetable change.
11. **Caution for Phase 3:** all Kadamba and Vijayalakshmi figures (70%, ₹60/₹80, 5 cancellations, 16/23 kg) are noisy machine translation. Do not headline them in the intervention map until the audio is checked.
