## A. Summary

The pass-1 revision absorbed Brief #1 well, but it now carries **seven places that Brief #2's correction table directly contradicts** (P0): the Kadamba staff-meal figure "20 to 30", the extra-portion figure "30 to 40 for paying and unregistered diners", the "five-person project management team" at Yuktāhār, the claim that "each operator runs its own hierarchy" and the matrix cell that has operators managing staff "through supervisor, manager, operations manager, director" (Yuktāhār has no hierarchy), the "Prism team ... company name still to be confirmed" framing, the Kadamba 70% rule still tagged as plain single-sourced, and Yuktāhār's forecasting described without the first-half-hour in-service projection.

The larger gap is **structural**: Brief #2 supplies two complete operator organisations the map does not show. On the Kadamba side the full command chain CFS → CDS (head: Raju) → Prism → operations manager → manager → supervisor → service staff, with Nazir as main veg chef, a biryani head chef, a facility tasting team plus two Prism tasters, weekly QC, and a sizeable population of outside diners and staff eating from the same food. On the Yuktāhār side a flat, peer-checked organisation under ABC Hospitality Services with two project managers, Ajita (in-charge, accounts, Wastage Register), Bhavani (grocery cross-check), a coordinator who runs the attendance register and wastage board, a store function without a dedicated keeper, tasting, cutting and cleaning teams, and a supplier base of local shops, godowns and mills. Each brings new relationships, dependencies, conflicts and informal roles that Activity 2's four methods ask for.

Three new D-24 identity conflicts must be **flagged, not merged**: Raju (CDS head) vs Giri (CDS Chair); Ajita vs the recording's "Adit Amma" (and the male "site head" the deliverable describes); and the Kadamba "facility team" vs the map's Facilities/Estate team, CDS site team and faculty QC visitors. One process conflict must also be flagged: Kadamba "cooks on registrations, full stop" vs the 2026-09-13 finding that Skip Meal declarations reach the kitchen.

No withdrawn Kadamba kg waste figures (16 / 23 / 100 kg) and no withdrawn arrival figures (200-300 / 300-400) appear in the deliverable; those need no removal. The deliverable carries no Kadamba waste figure at all, so the operator's "5 to 10% daily" and its tension with the 70% vs 37% gap are missing (P1).

## D. Phase 3 implications

- **Surplus absorbers must be planned for.** 30-50 outside diners plus about half of Kadamba's staff eat from the same food. Any intervention that trims the first batch takes food from them unless it budgets their portions explicitly (D-45, D-49). They are a stakeholder in the intervention, not only a waste sink.
- **Operator culture is a leverage point.** The same billing basis gives Kadamba "we are paid for the registration" and Yuktāhār "waste is cost". Levers that assume operators want less waste hold for Yuktāhār and not demonstrably for Kadamba; D-54's hold on payment-basis levers remains, and Kadamba's statement is a candidate pointer to payment on registrations.
- **Blame-free measurement is a design principle.** Yuktāhār's waste tracking only worked once the project managers made it blame-free and discussed it daily; any new measurement at Kadamba (or by CDS) should copy that.
- **The two forecasting regimes give a natural comparison and a transfer candidate.** Yuktāhār's matching-week lookup plus first-half-hour projection vs Kadamba's fixed 70% plus supervisor top-up. Both are vulnerable to the closing crest (about a quarter of diners in the last 15 minutes, records), so a transfer must include a late-crest correction.
- **Low-tech, book-native interventions fit.** Yuktāhār dropped Zoho for cost against data already held and staff not being digitally inclined. A paper-first or book-to-sheet design is more likely to be adopted than a new app.
- **Governance entry point is uncertain.** The Kadamba chain runs CFS → CDS → Prism; who at CDS owns the rule (Raju or Giri) must be settled before a governance-side intervention names an owner.
- **Student-side rule is concrete.** Automatic registration with 5 cancellations per meal type per month, 4 days ahead, and no way to nullify the charge is the standing-booking root cause seen from the student's side; relaxing it is an intervention on the Mess Office/CDS rule, not on students.
- **Late-door service is an absorber the operator cannot refuse.** Interventions on the closing crest must account for back-door entry after 9:30.
- **Food-safety chain constrains reuse.** Kadamba's reheat-and-transfer practice (team judgement) means surplus is recycled into later service at a quality cost; a cut in over-preparation may improve quality as a side benefit (candidate, unevidenced closing link).
