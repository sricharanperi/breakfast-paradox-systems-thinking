## A. Summary

The Stakeholder Map was built entirely from the student side plus the institutional/governance side. It has **no operator-side interior at all**: the three "vendors" are treated as one homogeneous commercial actor labelled by cuisine, and "serving/counter staff" and "kitchen/cooking staff" are the only operator roles. The September recordings and registers now give (a) the operators' names and three very different planning regimes, (b) a full operator-internal role structure and reporting chain, (c) suppliers and a waste-exit actor, (d) the CDS-side "site team" and QC visitors, and (e) record-level attendance data held by CDS itself.

Five existing claims are now **contradicted** (P0): who places food orders; that vendors expect the four-day forecast to approximate demand; that the four-day lead time caps same-day responsiveness; that no direct vendor–student relationship exists; and the Palash-cooks-for-Bakul arrangement. Several "not established" cells can now be filled, and a new, central conflict (menu ownership vs. waste visibility) is missing entirely.

## B. Contradictions (P0)

### B1. "The Mess Office places food orders on a four-day lead time" (Findings §, para 2) and "Vendors depend on the Mess Office for a registration forecast fixed four days ahead of service ... to procure" (Interests table, Dependencies §)
- **Wrong:** procurement is done by each operator, not the Mess Office, and it is not on a four-day cadence. The T-4 lock applies to *student registration*; raw procurement is mixed.
- **Evidence:** Yuktahar groceries bought fortnightly ("every 15 days", stock register), vegetables 2-3x/week, milk daily; per-meal issue list written the evening before by the operator's own storekeeper/coordinator meeting. **confirmed (records) + confirmed (operator, clear audio)** [brief §2.1; Synthesis §2.1, §2.6]. Vijayalakshmi runs its own supply chain (paneer brand switching Amul, Heritage, Milky Mist). **single-sourced** [IIIT Road 55 05:13-05:42].
- **Proposed change:** Mess Office (CDS) role = registration rule owner and registration-count transmitter to operators (T-4, confirmed by CFS Chair in braindump #2 §5), not purchaser. Operators own procurement and cooking quantities. Rewrite the vendor "Need" cells and the Dependencies paragraph accordingly.

### B2. Vendor "Expectation: the four-day forecast approximates real demand" (Interests table, all three vendor rows)
- **Wrong:** no operator treats registrations as demand. Each applies its own conversion factor and tops up live.
- **Evidence:** Yuktahar believes breakfast turnout is ~50% and plans from the matching-week Wastage Book + weekday attendance register, **confirmed (operator, clear audio)**; its own register shows 17.7-40.2%, mean ~28%, **confirmed (records)** [csv/02_Attendance_Breakfast.csv]. - **Proposed change:** vendor Expectation = "registrations overstate demand; a fixed discount plus live top-up covers the difference". Add that each operator's discount is miscalibrated upward against the records (Kadamba 70% vs 37%; Yuktahar 50% vs 28%): the operators' mental model is itself a stakeholder attribute.

### B3. "The four-day procurement lead time itself ... a fixed constraint that caps how responsive the entire system can be to any same-day signal" (Findings, last para)

## G. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)
1. **Information-flow leverage (Meadows: information flows):** the gap is measured twice already (CDS scan records; operator wastage and attendance registers) but neither measurement reaches the rule owner of registration and billing, and the two do not meet each other. An intervention that routes existing data between existing actors (CDS to operator weekday/item turnout; operator waste to committee) needs no new measurement.
2. **Parameter recalibration as a low-hanging intervention point:** operators' first-batch ratios (50/70/80%) are about double real breakfast turnout (28-37%). Feeding record-based weekday turnout to operators is a parameter change with immediate waste impact, but it shifts risk onto the live top-up loop, so reserve and re-cook capacity (10-30 min) must be preserved.
3. **Rule leverage: the T-4 registration cut-off** is not required by raw procurement (perishables T-1, groceries fortnightly). The owner is CDS. A shorter cut-off or a T-1 confirm/skip is technically feasible from the operator side; the constraint is billing/rule design, not supply.
4. **Role / structure leverage: menu governance.** Give operators a formal input seat in menu decisions (they hold item-level waste data; Vijayalakshmi manager asks for it), paired with committee ownership. Precedent: the biweekly rotation already passed through the Council/Parliament pathway.
5. **Incentive leverage: vendor payment basis.** If operators are paid on registrations, they have no stake in closing the gap; if on served plates, they do. The Phase 3 intervention map should hold this as a gated option until the contract terms are confirmed.
6. **Calendar feed as an intervention point:** events, exams, holidays reach the kitchen only by chance. A formal feed from academic/event calendars to the operator's daily planning meeting connects the map's clearest sleeping stakeholder (Academic administration) to the actor who can act on it the same day.
7. **Design principle: augment local practice, do not replace it.** Yuktahar rejected Zoho and ERP to "remain close to the data"; staff are not computer savvy; registers are paper. Interventions should fit paper registers and the twice-daily meeting, not require new software.
8. **Design principle: non-blaming measurement.** Yuktahar's waste reduction came from a routine and staff buy-in over August-December, with cross-checking "not to find fault". Interventions that expose waste data must be framed as improvement, not surveillance, or staff will resist.
9. **Design principle: preserve absorption mechanisms (D-45).** Staff meals from leftovers, reopening the door for late-comers and leftover reuse (idli to upma, batter, milk to curd) help real people; interventions must not remove them to create visibility.
10. **Positive deviant for scaling:** Yuktahar's documented feedback loop is an in-system exemplar; a peer-transfer intervention (operator to operator) is more plausible than an institution-designed tool.
11. **Closing-rush intervention point:** 26.5% of breakfast scans in the last 15 minutes, driving both shortage and just-in-case over-preparation, plus the 8:30 class overlap. Levers sit with Academic administration (timetable) and CDS (service window), not with operators.
12. **Waste exit as a measurement point:** the garbage contractor is the single point where all waste leaves; weighing there would give an institution-level waste signal without burdening operators.
