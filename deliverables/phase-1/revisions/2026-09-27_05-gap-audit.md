## 0. Headline

The deliverable was written when the project had no record-level data, so every behaviour-over-time claim is recall-based, and the file says so repeatedly ("No records-based ... data exists yet", "not a measurement", "any numeric trend over time for any variable" is not claimed). All three of those statements are now false. The project now holds:

1. **30 days of Kadamba breakfast records** (April 2026, 32,162 registrations, 11,941 timestamped scans). This gives a real daily BOT series, a weekday cycle, and a measured intra-morning arrival curve.
2. **20 days of Yuktahar breakfast and lunch registers** (September 2026, photographed). This gives a second mess and a breakfast-vs-lunch comparison by day.
3. **Operator-side history** (Yuktahar takeover "last year", waste tracking from August, staff-reported waste fall by December; Kadamba LPG shortage response and dinner-sambar removal; Vijayalakshmi prep ratio 70% -> 80%, paneer supplier churn, new manager).

The revision should turn the deliverable from a recall-only BOT into a **record-anchored BOT at three timescales (within morning, across the week, across the month) plus an operator-side history timeline**, while keeping the individual-level "no single trend" caution, now reframed as "heterogeneous individuals sum to a stable aggregate with a strong weekday cycle".

## 3. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

1. **Leverage on information flows (Meadows: information flows / delays), not on procurement.** The gap is already measured by operators every day (wastage book, weekday register, live scans). It never reaches registration or billing policy. An intervention that routes weekday-specific conversion rates (Kadamba 27-42%) into prep ratios and into the registration default is higher-leverage than new tracking.
2. **The prep-ratio rule is a parameter-level intervention point with a known baseline.** Replacing a fixed 70/80% of registrations with a weekday-specific ratio from the previous weeks' records would remove most of the ~1.9x breakfast over-preparation. Low leverage in Meadows terms but fast and measurable; the Yuktahar "cut to 30% next week" statement shows operators are willing.
3. **The registration default is the stronger leverage point (rules).** Registration is flat all week, including Sunday, so it is a standing booking, not a daily decision. Sunday breakfast (27% Kadamba, 18-24% Yuktahar, with Sunday lunch registrations already falling while breakfast does not) is the obvious pilot slice for an opt-in or shorter-lock breakfast registration.
4. **Delay mismatch as an intervention point:** registration locks at T-4 while perishables are bought T-1 and the eat/skip decision is made T-0. Shortening the breakfast lock toward T-1 aligns the rule with the real procurement horizon. Contract terms (still open) bound this.
5. **Timing leverage at the closing crest:** 26.5% of attendance arrives in 9:15-9:30 and ~9% after close. Staged cooking or a second batch timed to 9:10-9:15, rather than more total food, addresses both late run-outs and early over-preparation. Design principle: design for the arrival curve, not the daily total.
6. **Design principle: calibrate to records, not beliefs.** All three operators' beliefs diverge from records (50% vs 28%; 300-400 vs ~134 by 8:30; "Kadamba 90-95%" vs 37%). Any intervention should display the operator's own numbers back to them.
7. **Design principle: build on the operator's existing routine and expect a ~4-month adoption curve.** The only documented behaviour change (Yuktahar, Aug -> Dec) came from a paper routine, staff buy-in and a "proposal, not mandate" culture; software (Zoho) was dropped. Interventions should extend existing books, not replace them, and should be evaluated over months, not weeks.
8. **Design principle: target patterns, not "students".** Individual trajectories diverge; the aggregate is stable with a weekday cycle, and veg breakfast (32%) differs from non-veg (49%). Interventions should target slices (Sunday, veg breakfast, the late crest), not assume a single population trend.
9. **Evaluation baseline now exists.** Kadamba April (37.1%, weekday profile, arrival curve) and Yuktahar September (~28% breakfast, 56-89% lunch) are before-state BOT baselines for any Phase 4 prototype or scenario model.
10. **Absorption layer must be revised before interventions are chosen:** Kadamba surplus goes to the bin and to staff meals, not composting. A waste-exit intervention (composting/redistribution) is a separate, lower-leverage point that treats symptoms.
11. **Existing precedent:** the LPG/holiday non-compulsory registration (B4) is already a tested intervention; its dates and outcomes should be recovered from the operators' Wastage Books (archive goes back at least to June 2026) to give Phase 3 a real before/after.
