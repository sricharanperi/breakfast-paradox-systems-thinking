## A. Contradictions (P0)

### A1. "The kitchen side is not visible from any source gathered" (Overview, Conduct Interviews and Observations "System visibility" paragraph, Outstanding Data Collection)
- **What is wrong:** the document asserts that everything from "registration reaches the kitchen" onward (how quantity is decided, when cooking starts, portioning, what happens to unclaimed food, whether data reaches a decision-maker) is unseen, and promises it "will be collected and submitted later".
- **New evidence:** three operators interviewed on site (Yuktahar site head, ABC Hospitality Services: **OP-CLEAR**; Kadamba "Prism team" floor manager: **OP-NOISY**; Vijayalakshmi Caterers owner + manager: **OP-NOISY**), 26 photographed Yuktahar registers (**REC**), and the Kadamba kitchen walkthrough already in braindump #2 §12 (**CFS**). Brief §3 row 1: "Largely closed for operations."
- **Proposed change:** rewrite Overview and the visibility paragraph to say the trace now covers both halves, with the remaining unseen parts narrowed to: live observation of waste disposal, contract terms, lunch/dinner records beyond Yuktahar, the Bakul/Palash kitchen confirmed by name. Replace "stops at the kitchen door" framing everywhere.
- **Priority:** P0.

### A2. "Four-day lead time on which the Mess Office places its order with each vendor ... nothing lets same-day registration, cancellation, or Skip Meal activity change what is actually cooked, since the order is already fixed four days out" (Track Exceptions > Dependencies)
- **What is wrong:** conflates the registration cut-off with procurement and with cooking, and asserts cooking is fixed four days out. Contradicted three ways:
  1. T-4 is the **registration** cut-off (Kadamba: "registration closes about 4 days in advance, weekly compulsory", K 12:05–12:25, **OP-NOISY**). Procurement is mixed: groceries **fortnightly** (Yuktahar stock register, **REC** + **OP-CLEAR**), vegetables **2–3×/week** (**REC**/**OP-CLEAR**; Kadamba says veg lasts 2–3 days), meat/eggs/milk/paneer **T-1** at Kadamba (K 13:14–14:36, **OP-NOISY**), milk daily at Yuktahar.
  2. Cooking quantity is set **the day before** in a planning meeting (Yuktahar twice-daily meeting [00:11–01:15], **OP-CLEAR**; Kadamba "decided the day before from the weekly menu", K 00:00–00:26, **OP-NOISY**), and then **adjusted live on the day**: Kadamba keeps raw material ready and cooks more in 10–15 min on counter request while watching live scan counts (K 01:02–02:39); Vijayalakshmi tops up from the off-site kitchen within 30 min after 30-min checks (54 03:32–04:46); Yuktahar re-cooks by run-out time rule.
  3. MASTER_CONTEXT 2026-09-13 user correction (already logged, never propagated here): Skip Meal declarations **do** reach the kitchen for same-week prep forecasting; T-4 is a sourcing lock distinct from prep.
- **Proposed change:** replace the dependency sentence with a two-(actually three-)stage pipeline: registration locked T-4 → raw procurement on mixed cadences (fortnightly / 2–3×wk / T-1) → cooked quantity fixed T-1 as a ratio of registrations → live top-up on the day. State the brief's refinement: the binding constraint on waste is **cooked food prepared at a fixed ratio to registrations**, not raw procurement; raw surplus is recoverable (fortnightly stock, FIFO/FEFO per braindump #2 §12), cooked surplus is not (3–4 h usable life, K 06:48, **OP-NOISY**).
- **Priority:** P0.

### A3. Key Finding 5: "only around 30% of registered students actually attend ... the mess still struggles to serve even that smaller group well ... under-provisioning ... unverified respondent impression"
- **What is wrong / now changed:** two parts.
  - The attendance figure is **no longer unverified**. Kadamba breakfast April 2026: 32,162 registrations, 11,941 availed = **37.1%** (veg 32.4%, non-veg 48.5%) (**REC**). Yuktahar breakfast Sept 1–20: **17.7%–40.2%**, mean ~28% on 13 legible days (**REC**, `csv/02_Attendance_Breakfast.csv`). The respondents' "~30% attend" was approximately right for breakfast. Note a wording trap: MASTER_CONTEXT §9 and the brief both phrase this item as a "~30% *gap*", and the brief then says the 30% "was a lunch-like figure". The deliverable itself says ~30% *attend*, which the records support for breakfast (gap ~63% Kadamba, ~72% Yuktahar). The revision and the master context should resolve this wording consistently: **attendance ~28–37%, gap ~63–72% at breakfast**.
  - The **aggregate under-provisioning** hypothesis is contradicted: operators prepare against 70% (Kadamba first batch), 70–80% (Vijayalakshmi) or a believed 50% (Yuktahar) of registrations, against actual ~28–37%. Kadamba first batch alone is ~1.9× actual turnout (brief §2.2, **CAND** from REC + OP-NOISY). What students experience as "struggles to serve" is **item-level run-outs at the closing surge**, not aggregate shortage: 26.5% of scans fall in 9:15–9:30, climbing to ~9 scans/min at 9:29 (**REC**); re-cook is refused when run-out happens late (Yuktahar rule, **OP-CLEAR**); specific equipment failures (broken toaster, **OP-NOISY**).
- **Proposed change:** rewrite Finding 5 as: breakfast attendance ~28–37% is record-confirmed; the service shortfall students report coexists with structural over-preparation and is a timing/item-mix failure concentrated in the last 15 minutes, not a quantity shortfall. This is one of the most important reframes the deliverable can carry forward.
- **Priority:** P0.

### A4. "Mess closing at 9:30 ... designed to run until 9:30" treated as the actual service boundary (Formal rules table; divergence table row "Item availability at close")
- **What is wrong:** records show the formal 9:30 close is not the operating boundary. Kadamba April: service closes ~9:35–9:45 by last scan, latest scan 10:07 (3 Apr), 9:57 (7 Apr) (**REC**, `05_Service_Window_Daily.csv`). The last-scan data independently shows breakfast scans past 9:30 on most days (**REC**), so a de facto grace period at breakfast is supported regardless.
- **Proposed change:** add a formal-vs-actual row "Closing time: formal 9:30 entry cut-off; actual last scans 9:35–9:45, occasionally ~10:00; door reopened informally for late-comers". Keep "rationale for 9:30 unknown" (still true).
- **Priority:** P0 (a formal-vs-informal claim the records directly overturn).

## F. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

1. **The leverage is at the conversion factor, not at procurement.** Cooked quantity = registrations × private ratio (50% / 70% / 70–80%) set T-1. Raw procurement surplus is recoverable; cooked surplus is not. Intervention point: the T-1 planning decision and the ratio itself (Meadows: parameters, but fed by information flows). The earlier T-4 procurement lock is the wrong target.
2. **Information flow is the highest-value leverage, and the data already exists.** Yuktahar measures registered vs ate by category, per item waste and run-out time; Kadamba has live scan counts; CDS holds a per-scan export. None of it routes into registration rules, billing, or across messes. Candidate intervention: close the loop from operator records (and CDS scan data) to (a) each operator's ratio by weekday and item, and (b) the institution's registration/billing policy. Design principle: "build on the operator's own records rather than replacing them" (Zoho was dropped; "remain close to the data").
3. **Calibrate by weekday and item, not by a single ratio.** Records give weekday turnout (Tue 42% → Sun 27%) and veg/non-veg difference (32% vs 49%); operators give item popularity (puri/idli/vada vs uttapam/set dosa/upma). Intervention point: weekday × item conversion tables; menu-committee feedback on chronic-waste items.
4. **Shorten the feedback delay.** The Wastage Book is read two weeks later (matching menu day). A weekly or daily review of the same data is a delay-reduction lever.
5. **The closing surge is a stock-and-flow timing problem.** 26.5% of scans in the last 15 minutes causes both late run-outs (no re-cook after a cut-off) and "just in case" over-cooking. Intervention points: staged cooking with a late small batch sized from live scan rate (Kadamba already holds raw stock ready for 10–15 min cooking); a formalised grace period instead of ad-hoc door reopening; the second-counter rule. Design principle: design for the arrival curve that exists, not the 7:30–9:30 window as written.
6. **Event and calendar information has no formal channel.** Exams, hackathons, holidays reach the kitchen only by chance in the meeting. Intervention point: an institute-to-operator calendar feed (rules / information flow).
7. **Registration is a standing booking, not a demand signal** (flat registrations, weekday-varying turnout). Changes to registration rules (cut-off, default, cancellation cap, Skip Meal refund) remain the structural lever, but operators have no say over them. Design principle: give the party who sees the waste a route to the party who sets the rules.
8. **Respect the absorption mechanisms (D-45).** Staff eating surplus, leftover reuse, late-comer grace are locally beneficial. Do not design interventions that work by removing them. Note the composting absorption is contested (Kadamba says none), so waste-exit interventions (composting, measured disposal) are a genuine open intervention point, not a solved one.
9. **Culture and roles matter as much as tools.** Yuktahar's waste reduction came from routine, paired non-punitive cross-checking and staff buy-in over ~4 months. Design principle: interventions framed as "a proposal, come back to me if there's a better option" and non-punitive measurement; role change (a data-owning coordinator) is a candidate intervention point.
10. **Incentive structure:** operators are paid monthly per plate with deductions (OP-NOISY, unclear). Whether waste reduction benefits or costs the operator depends on the contract, still unknown; this remains the most consequential unknown for any incentive-based intervention (MASTER_CONTEXT §9 L6/contract item).
11. **Exception handling as design input:** QR-failure hand logging (data gap), LPG shortage (shock-adaptive mode, buy outside), equipment failure, supplier quality. The shock-adaptive behaviour (cut and top up) shows the system can run leaner; this strengthens the L6/B4 shock-adaptive-model intervention idea.
12. **Cross-mess learning is an untapped leverage point.** Three operators run three different balancing loops of very different quality (documented vs rule-of-thumb vs off-site lag). A shared practice standard or cross-mess benchmark is an intervention point on roles/information flows that needs no rule change from the institution.
