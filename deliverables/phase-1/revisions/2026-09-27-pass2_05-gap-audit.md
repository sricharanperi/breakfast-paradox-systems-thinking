## 3. Phase 3 implications

1. **The in-service projection is biased by the crest.** Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour, but on the measured curve that window holds about 15% of diners and the last half hour about 40%. Any "sample early, cook the rest" intervention needs a crest-shaped conversion, not a linear one. The measured Kadamba curve is a ready-made correction factor (candidate transfer; test with Yuktāhār timestamps).
2. **Blame-free measurement is a precondition, with a delay of about four months.** Yuktāhār's weighing worked only once it was made blame-free and backed by daily discussion and team training, and staff reported results after about four months. Any intervention that adds measurement should be framed blame-free and judged on a timescale of months, not weeks.
3. **Extra mouths are a named absorber (D-45/D-49).** 30-50 outside diners plus about half the Kadamba staff eat from the same food. Cutting the first batch without planning for them shifts hunger onto night security and staff.
4. **Plan veg and non-veg as separate levers.** The first batch is planned per type, and turnout differs sharply (32.4% vs 48.5%). A type-specific ratio is a cheaper lever than a global one.
5. **Automatic registration without an off-switch confirms the standing-booking root cause (D-50).** The four-day lead mainly protects dry goods that are bought a week ahead anyway, while perishables are bought the day before. A shorter cancellation lead for perishable-heavy meals is supported by the procurement tiers.
6. **Vendor incentive and D-54.** Kadamba's "we are paid for it, so we cook for registrations" is a further hint that payment follows registrations (candidate). This moves toward, but does not settle, the registered-vs-served question that holds back LP-C and LP-D.
7. **Low-tech fit.** Zoho was dropped on cost and staff digital skills. Interventions should work with paper books (the two-book reconciliation, the wastage board) rather than require new software at the kitchen.
8. **Quality as a co-benefit.** If surplus is reheated and transferred, cutting the first batch may improve quality as well as waste (candidate chain; the link to skipping is not evidenced, D-40).
9. **An informal close sustains the late crest.** Back-door service after 9:30 keeps late arrival workable. An intervention on the crest has to decide explicitly what happens at the back door.
10. **Do not set a Kadamba waste target until the 5-10% base is known.** The tension with 70% vs 37% means the current figure cannot serve as a baseline.
11. **The Skip Meal conflict decides one lever.** If Kadamba ignores Skip Meal, promoting Skip Meal does nothing to cooking there. Resolve the conflict before ranking any Skip Meal-based lever.
