## 0. Headline findings

1. **The brief has no attendance number anywhere.** Its only quantitative "uptake" claim (Kadamba 51-79% vs North Indian lines 11-36.5%) is a *registration-fill-vs-capacity* figure from one portal screenshot (2026-09-09), but the row text calls it "registered breakfast attendance". We now have record-level turnout: Kadamba breakfast April 2026 **37.1% availed / 62.9% no-show** (32,162 registrations), Yuktahar breakfast Sept 2026 **17.7-40.2%, mean ~28%**, against Yuktahar lunch **56-89%**. This is the single largest upgrade available to the brief.
2. **Three claims are contradicted by operator evidence (P0):** (a) "vendors prepare food against a four-day-old figure; nothing lets same-day activity change what is cooked", (b) the problem framing's "the meal is still cooked", (c) Bakul's veg food "cooked at Palash and transported" (already corrected by braindump #2 on 2026-09-13 and never reconciled into this file; now reinforced by the Vijayalakshmi off-site kitchen, candidate).
3. **The boundary stops at the "vendor" box.** The kitchen side is now an evidenced subsystem: three named operators, a day-before planning meeting, a first-batch conversion ratio (70% / 70-80% / wastage-book lookup), live top-up, leftover reuse, suppliers on different cadences, an off-site kitchen with a 30-40 min lag, and a waste exit through a garbage contractor.
4. **Purpose is stated only as intent.** D-3 (2026-09-05) decided to split Purpose into *true purpose / actual mechanism / gap*; the current file does not do this. The new evidence now makes the "actual mechanism" describable with operator- and record-level evidence, and the institution's own stated purpose (CDS poster: "minimizing food wastage through advance meal planning") is available but not used.
5. **The binding constraint is misidentified.** The brief treats a T-4 *food order* as the constraint. Evidence shows T-4 is the *registration* cut-off; procurement is mixed (groceries fortnightly, vegetables 2-3x/week, perishables T-1); the binding constraint on waste is **cooked food prepared at a fixed ratio to registrations with a 3-4 h usable life**.

## 5. Implications for Phase 3 (Activity 9: leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

1. **Information-flow leverage point (high):** the registration-to-turnout conversion is already measured (CDS scan export; Yuktahar weekday register and wastage book) but each operator guesses its own factor and even the best-documented one misremembers it (50% vs 28%). Routing measured, weekday-specific breakfast turnout back to each operator's day-before planning is a Meadows "information flows" intervention that needs no new data collection and removes no absorption mechanism (consistent with D-45).
2. **Rules leverage point:** the T-4 weekly-compulsory registration cut-off is not required by the kitchen's own supply chain (perishables T-1, groceries fortnightly). Shortening the cut-off or separating breakfast from the weekly block is a rule change the operators themselves questioned. Intervention point: the registration rule owner (Mess Office/CDS), not the operator.
3. **Breakfast-specific scope:** lunch at the same mess runs 56-89%; any intervention should be scoped to breakfast rather than redesigning registration for all meals. Design principle: *change the breakfast rule, leave the meal that works alone.*
4. **Timing leverage point:** 40% of breakfast scans fall in the last 30 minutes. Interventions at the closing surge (staged cooking late in the window, a grab-and-go counter, alignment with the 8:30 class start) act on both shortage and waste at once. Constraint for design: cooked food lives 3-4 h, so staging cooking later is feasible; off-site halls have a ~30 min top-up lag, so the principle must hold for both on-site and off-site kitchens.
5. **Incentive/contract leverage point:** whether operators are paid on registered or availed plates determines who benefits from over-registration. Treat this as the gating question for any billing-side intervention (it replaces, and is more specific than, the earlier "contract terms" P0).
6. **Roles leverage point:** operators control quantities but have no say in menu or registration; students/committee choose the menu but do not see item-level waste. A design principle: *whoever sees the waste should have a route to whoever sets the menu.* Intervention point: the menu-selection step on the two-week cycle, which is already structured for item-level lookup.
7. **Process design principle from Yuktahar's own change:** its waste reduction came from routine, paired cross-checking "not to find fault", and staff buy-in over ~4 months, and it rejected software (Zoho) to "remain close to the data". Interventions should augment the operator's existing records rather than replace them with a new system.
8. **Correct the absorption layer before designing against it:** at Kadamba surplus is absorbed by staff meals, late-comer top-ups and the bin, not composting. D-45's corollary (do not remove absorption mechanisms) still holds, but the list of mechanisms Phase 3 protects must be updated.
9. **Evidence-limit principle:** records exist for Kadamba breakfast (April) and Yuktahar (September) only. Any intervention claim about Bakul/Palash or about lunch/dinner is assumption until records are obtained; the CDS export route shows those records probably exist and are obtainable.
