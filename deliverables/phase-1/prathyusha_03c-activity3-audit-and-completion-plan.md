## PART D — Independent Research Pass Results (2026-09-06)

**Headline finding: a real, previously-unknown governance body was found, verified
directly from source (not a search snippet) — the Student Parliament's mess-related
elected roles.** This is the single most valuable outcome of this entire research pass
and materially changes the Stakeholder Map, not just the Power Analysis.

### 1. Wayback Machine — confirmed dead end, not a retrieval failure

Both target Ping articles ("The Mess-y Situation," "Cleaning up the Mess?") have **no
archive.org snapshot at all** — confirmed via the Wayback "available" API directly, not
inferred. Wayback simply never crawled pingiiit.org during the relevant window. This
path is closed; everything about those two articles' content remains 🟡
search-snippet-derived, not full-text-verified, and should stay tagged that way
permanently unless the site becomes fetchable some other way.

### 2. "Mess Council" — likely institution confusion, not a real separate IIIT-H body

A real "Mess Monitoring Council (MMC)" exists at `gymkhana.iith.ac.in` — but that
belongs to **IIT Hyderabad**, a completely different institution from **IIIT
Hyderabad** (the one this project studies), frequently confused because of the
near-identical name. A domain-restricted search of `iiit.ac.in` specifically excluding
IIT-H found **no dedicated "Mess Council" page or reference anywhere on IIIT-H's own
domain**. One earlier, non-restricted search result that described a "Mess Council
with per-mess sub-councils" is flagged as unreliable — it reads like a search engine
blending IIT-H's real MMC into an IIIT-H-framed answer.

**What this means for your Q9 answer:** "goes to mess council where there will be
people to take care of" most plausibly refers to either (a) IIT-H's council, mentioned
in error, or (b) an informal/colloquial way of referring to what verified evidence
below shows is actually called the **Mess Committee** (see finding 3) — not a
confirmed, separate, real body. This isn't a contradiction of what you said, just a
naming clarification worth double-checking directly if you get the chance, rather than
this document continuing to carry "Mess Council" as an open, unresolved entity.

### 3. The Student Parliament's mess-related roles — ✅ verified directly from source

Fetched directly (not via search snippet) from `clubs.iiit.ac.in/student-bodies/
parliament` — this domain is not blocked, unlike pingiiit.org. IIIT-H's Student
Parliament (elected via Single Transferable Vote, ~1 representative per 50 students)
currently has these named, elected mess-related roles:
- **Mess Secretary**
- **Mess Deputy Secretary (Yuktahar)**
- **Mess Deputy Secretary (Kadamba)**
- **Mess Deputy Secretary (Palash)**
- Historically: **North Mess Secretary** (2023-09 to 2024-08) — this independently
  corroborates the earlier "North Mess" naming puzzle from public-source research,
  now confirmed from a second, different, directly-fetched source.
- An event listing confirms Parliament representatives hold a direct standing meeting
  format with a **"Mess Warden"** ("FSIS with Mess Warden," 2026-05-07) — a title
  distinct from, but likely the same person/office as, the "Warden" already in this
  project's stakeholder map.

**This resolves the "who is the student representative on the Mess Committee"
question from last round far more precisely than a bare "yes."** It isn't one
undifferentiated seat — it's a Mess Secretary plus a dedicated Deputy Secretary per
mess, all separately elected. **This stakeholder was not in the Activity 2 Stakeholder
Map and should be — see the update applied below.**

**A significant disambiguation, still not fully resolved:** search-derived context
(not raw-source-verified this time) suggests there may be **two different things both
called "Mess Committee"** — (1) a Parliament-internal committee made of MPs, which
negotiates/pushes back toward the administration, and (2) the faculty-chaired,
institute-level policy body (Prof. Girish, per the Ping articles). Whether these are
the same body with mixed student/faculty membership, or two separate committees
sharing a name, could not be determined. This is now the sharpest remaining ambiguity
in the whole governance structure — sharper than the original Committee/Office split.

### 4. Two new precedents found (via three additional Ping articles)

- **"Let Them Eat Frogs" (Nov 2024, pingiiit.org)** — a frog was found in Kadamba's
  chicken biryani; the Mess Office **unilaterally cancelled non-veg meals at Kadamba
  without officially communicating the real reason**, sparking debate across multiple
  mail threads. This is a *cleaner, more specific* precedent of unilateral, opaque
  Mess Office decision-making than the Dec 2024 cancellation-policy episode, and it
  predates it by about a month — suggesting a pattern, not an isolated instance.
- **"We Are So Cooked" (~Jan 2025, pingiiit.org)** — direct continuation of the Dec
  2024 policy story: confirms the new 4-day cancellation window, criticizes the
  rollout timing, and reports the new IMS portal migration was buggy (at least two
  students reported being able to register for the same meal multiple times) — a
  concrete, if secondhand, data point on the Portal/IT quality question this project
  has flagged as entirely unknown.
- **"Water Mess" (2023, pingiiit.org)** — a different domain (drinking water, not
  food) but the same institution and a structurally identical pattern: complaints via
  WhatsApp/email went unaddressed for months despite Parliament requesting water
  testing, until 38+ confirmed typhoid cases forced reactive action. **Useful as a
  comparative precedent**: it shows a recurring institutional pattern of
  complaint → ignored → crisis → reactive fix, evidenced twice now (water, mess), not
  once — this strengthens rather than merely adds to the "feedback exists, output
  unconfirmed" finding elsewhere in this brief.
