\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Phase 2 found that the Breakfast Paradox is a stable equilibrium, held in place by three confirmed structures: an automatic registration default with a narrow exit, billing at the booking and not at the meal, and a gap that the scan record and the operators\textquotesingle{} books both measure but that reaches nobody who sets the rules. On the kitchen side, registrations turn into a cooked first batch that any kitchen can top up and none can shrink. Activity 9 asks where this system can change. It delivers the Leverage-Point / System Intervention Map and the Design Principles, and hands seven principles and fourteen intervention points to Activity 10.

Claims keep the project\textquotesingle s labels: confirmed (read off a record or seen on site), single-sourced (one account), candidate (an inference) and assumed. Depth is counted on Meadows\textquotesingle{} twelve places, with place 1 the deepest and place 12 the shallowest. Bakul has no records of any kind, so no Bakul effect is sized anywhere in this document.

\section{1 Activity 9: Define Design Principles and Intervention Opportunities}\label{activity-9-define-design-principles-and-intervention-opportunities}

Four methods run in turn: leverage points, read against archetypes and ranked; changes to rules, roles, incentives, information flows and processes; the design objective set against the case for the status quo; and fourteen intervention points. The map and the principles close the activity.

\subsection{1.1 Identify Leverage Points}\label{identify-leverage-points}

A lever is ruled out if it removes a mechanism that protects someone: resale, reuse of leftovers, the meals of 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba\textquotesingle s staff, the walk-in buffer, late and back-door service. The over-cooked first batch, repeated reheating and the bin protect no one and are fair targets. Feasibility is judged for a student team, which can propose, pilot and evidence a change but cannot enact a rule. High means a prototype or pilot with an actor already engaged, medium an evidenced proposal to an existing forum, low a proposal only, either because the holder is out of reach or because the lever rests on a fact nobody knows yet.

Rules (place 5) are used only where a rule itself changes. Place 9, on delays, would mean flattening the closing crest, which no lever attempts. Places 11 and 12 appear only as settings inside a deeper lever: cutting Kadamba\textquotesingle s 70\% ratio by decree would move the cost from surplus into run-outs at the crest, and the cancellation cap, raised from five to ten during a gas shortage and back at five now (single-sourced), changes only how many exits the default allows.

\textbf{LP1: Build the missing routes.} Of Kadamba\textquotesingle s 32,162 breakfast registrations in April 2026, 37.1\% were eaten. Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s September breakfast ran at 17.7 to 40.2\% against 56 to 89\% for lunch (confirmed). No route from any record to any rule owner exists, and Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s own books disagree on 3 September, 77 eaten against 110. The fix is a standing breakfast view, the ledger, of booked, cooked, eaten and left over per hall, weekday and line, sent to the rule owner and back to the operators under one agreed definition of "ate". It also carries a weekly note of exams, events and holidays into the day-before planning meeting, which at Yuktāhār now arrives by word of mouth. It has to be blame-free and accept paper, because Yuktāhār has already dropped a commercial ERP system once (single-sourced). Delivery waits on a receiver, since two different people are on record as head of CDS. A bill line showing breakfasts paid for and not eaten is a student-facing variant, but without a way to respond it would only confirm that the charge cannot be avoided.

\textbf{LP2: Change the breakfast default and its exit.} Registrations hold at about 1,070 every weekday while turnout runs from 42.1\% on Tuesdays to 27.3\% on Sundays (confirmed). The four-day lead protects only dry goods, since Kadamba buys perishables the day before and Yuktāhār takes milk daily, and the Kadamba team has already pushed back on the cut-off (single-sourced). A student and operator coalition for a shorter lead is therefore the first shared interest on record (candidate). The lever is the default and its exit, not the cap. The options, lighter to heavier, are a one- or two-day lead, an evening-before confirm-or-skip that lowers the count, Sunday opt-in, and no registration while off campus. A lower count shrinks Kadamba\textquotesingle s first batch only if it is the count the kitchen reads, and whether Skip Meal already lowers it is an open conflict. A skip must never cost a hall slot: one Kadamba respondent books the whole month because the hall fills up (L3b, candidate). The payment basis is unconfirmed for every operator. The Bakul manager says plates served and Kadamba\textquotesingle s remark points to registrations (both single-sourced), so Bakul\textquotesingle s position on LP2 is likely neutral or favourable if its account holds (candidate). If resale falls, it falls because the need has gone. No absorber is removed.

\textbf{LP3: Operator goal: surplus is a cost.} The purpose would move from "cook for every booking" to "feed whoever comes, at least cost" (place 3), with a numeric target inside L11 (place 8). Under the same rules the operators state different purposes. Yuktāhār names quality, less waste, lower cost, standardisation and profit. Kadamba cooks to registrations "because it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it". The Bakul manager\textquotesingle s rule is "there should not be a shortage of food, even if some wastage occurs" (all single-sourced), and at Bakul a run-out sends a complaint to the managing director while surplus stays silent, a kitchen-level case of a system that reacts only to what is loud (candidate). The fix is a wastage board, a target and a purpose stated by the operations manager, with the target anchored to the best recorded performance (Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s rate or the institution\textquotesingle s own goal), never last month\textquotesingle s average, which drifts down. Smaller portions are the cheapest cut, so every target carries grams per plate, taste and run-outs (P7). A cost-per-line case can go to Prism\textquotesingle s operations manager, who has not been interviewed.

\textbf{LP4: A standing operator forum that runs its own trials.} The operators already plan differently, and what separates them is practice more than rules: Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s matching-week lookup (books photographed, confirmed), Kadamba\textquotesingle s fixed ratio, Bakul\textquotesingle s weekly judgement above a floor (both single-sourced). Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s waste fell between August and December once weighing was made blame-free (single-sourced, with no before-and-after figures). Kadamba keeps gram standards but no learning record, and the Bakul operator believes Kadamba fills 90 to 95\% where the records say 37\% at breakfast (confirmed). The fix is a forum convened by CDS and hosted by Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s two project managers, where each hall runs its own trial and the ledger shows the results. Swapping books alone is place 6, while a forum with the power to use the ledger to choose what works is place 4. What has to transfer is the preconditions: blame-free weighing, daily discussion and a purpose that treats waste as cost. Kadamba\textquotesingle s layered chain adds a reprimand risk.

\textbf{LP5: Record-calibrated first batch with a daily learning record.} L11 closes at Yuktāhār, is a candidate instance bounded by a floor at Bakul, and is absent at Kadamba. Creating it at Kadamba is place 6 and strengthening it where it runs is place 8. Kadamba\textquotesingle s 70\% becomes 100\% for biryani lunch (single-sourced), so the ratio follows expectation and no record. April turnout was 32.4\% on the veg line and 48.5\% on non-veg (confirmed), so the batch is about 2.2 times what was eaten on veg and about 1.4 times on non-veg (candidate arithmetic). The fix sets the batch as the gram standard times expected turnout per hall, weekday and line, plus a named allowance for outside diners and staff and a float for bonda, puri, vada and idli. It starts on the veg line with a one-page paper record read the next day. A 70\% batch against about 398 eaten implies roughly 350 surplus portions (candidate), at odds with Kadamba\textquotesingle s stated 5 to 10\% waste, so the lever cannot yet be sized. Bakul\textquotesingle s manager puts turnout at most around 60\%, under his floor of about 70\% (single-sourced), and the floor stays a named constraint until item-level records test it. LP5 is a quick fix, paired with LP1 against the L17 risk and with LP6 against run-outs.

\textbf{LP6: Crest-aware top-up and a staged late batch.} At Kadamba 26.5\% of April scans land between 9:15 and 9:30, rising to about nine a minute by 9:29, and 9.2\%, about 37 a day, arrive at or after 9:30, the latest at 10:07 (confirmed). The operator\textquotesingle s estimate of about 150 diners in the last ten minutes overstates this and may drive the hedge (candidate). Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour, when only 15.3\% of Kadamba\textquotesingle s scans have arrived. The fix has three parts: a weekday arrival card telling the supervisor at 8:00 what share of the day has come (place 6); a late batch started just after 9:00 on that basis (place 10, because it changes when stock is created); and a grace period after 9:30, always paired with the late batch. The share seen by 8:00 runs from 13.5\% on Sundays to 22.5\% on Wednesdays, and the share after 9:15 from about 32\% (Tuesdays, Wednesdays) to about 46\% (Sundays), so one flat factor would misread Sunday and Wednesday by about a quarter (confirmed; used as factors, candidate). Nothing here transfers to Bakul, where the manager puts his rush at 8:00 to 8:30 and aims to have food for about 50 at 9:20 (single-sourced). A Bakul top-up needs a phone call, cooking at Hafizpet and a drive of about 8 km, roughly 20 minutes if called early and 40 otherwise, and the end-to-end lead time has never been measured. There the lever is the size of the held reserve, a hedge and not an absorber (candidate), and the latest call that can still land, both set from measured data. LP6 is part of the quick fix.

\textbf{LP7: A cost trigger for attention, carried on the oversight line.} Menu fatigue was fixed through the Student Council, the Parliament and the CDS Committee (confirmed, L4), but the far larger breakfast gap never entered that pathway. The facility team tastes every Kadamba meal with two Prism staff (confirmed, observed) and never checks quantity. The fix is a standing threshold, such as cooked-against-eaten above a set level for several weeks, that sends the case to the Committee as complaints already do. It is anchored to the stated goal, lunch turnout or Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s rate, never the hall\textquotesingle s own past, and it reports run-outs alongside surplus. The cheapest carrier is one extra line at the tasting. Because an inspector recording quantity can look like an audit, a breach triggers a review and never a deduction.

\textbf{LP8: Operator voice in the menu.} Kadamba\textquotesingle s uttapam, set dosa and upma waste most while puri, bonda, vada and idli move well (single-sourced), yet students or the committee choose the menu, not the kitchen. The Bakul manager wants menu input, knows what sells (pav bhaji) and which pairings he thinks work (kachori with chole), but his view that students would eat more of familiar pairings is untested with residents (single-sourced). The fix is a standing operator slot at each two-week menu cycle to report item waste and run-outs. This builds the missing route L16, the quiet counterpart of L4. Operator input is read with consumption records and checked against residents\textquotesingle{} views: a voice, never authority.

\textbf{LP9: A named owner of booked, cooked and eaten (held).} Decision rights are split at least five ways, across registration, billing, menu, cooked quantity and the academic calendar (confirmed), and whoever sets registration and billing bears none of the cost they produce. A named owner, not a committee, would put the consequence where the decision is made and inherit LP1. Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s Wastage Register keeper is a lighter version. The lever is held until the head of CDS is identified.

\textbf{LP10: What a booking costs: billing and payment basis (held).} Attendance-tracked billing ran during a gas shortage and some holiday and fest periods, and it worked (confirmed). Operators are paid monthly as a lump sum on plates, with deductions (single-sourced, noisy audio), but no contract has been seen and the basis may differ by operator. The lever is held because a billing change made blind to the payment terms could push the operator incentive the wrong way, and student reliance on resale income needs checking first.

\textbf{LP11: Surface and test the governing mental models.} The beliefs holding the default, the fixed ratio and the unrouted gap in place sit at the base of the Iceberg chains, and nobody can change them for someone else. Eight meet a record that does not fit. The four Bakul beliefs meet none yet, so a test record is named for each.

Each session starts from the ledger, settles disagreements by the record, pairs advocacy with inquiry and points at specific anomalies such as 50 against 28. At Bakul, which has no record, one has to be built first. Effort goes to the actors already open to the data, and holders of tested views are made visible.

\textbf{LP12: Change the goal the system actually seeks.} The CDS poster gives the institution\textquotesingle s goal as minimising food wastage through advance meal planning (confirmed). The goal it actually pursues is the registration count: students are billed on it and Kadamba cooks on it (candidate reading). The stated goal\textquotesingle s measure is recorded and read by no one. The fix makes booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, per hall and line, the real indicators, read with quality and run-outs, because a wastage goal alone could be met by running short. CFS and CDS carry this by restating their own goal and asking for its indicators. LP12 is the move from the registration count to cooked-against-eaten, and the other levers make that move possible.

\subsubsection{Identify System Archetypes}\label{identify-system-archetypes}

\subsection{1.3 Develop Design Principles}\label{develop-design-principles}

The objective is a breakfast system where registration matches each student\textquotesingle s intent, the kitchen cooks to expected eaters, and the cost of any gap reaches whoever sets the rules. It does not mean ending registration, resale or all waste. Registration gives the planning count, resale recovers money, and some surplus is the price of never running out. Over-booking and over-cooking should become noticed exceptions. The guiding idea from the archetypes is to change the architecture instead of competing at the kitchen layer.

The status quo persists because it pays each actor. The levers keep what each actor values (the seat, the resale, the absorbers, run-out protection) and change what drives over-booking.

\subsection{1.6 Deliverable 2: Design Principles}\label{deliverable-2-design-principles}

Seven principles follow from the leverage points, archetypes and evidence, and every Activity 10 concept must pass all seven.

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio,alt={The seven principles, with what each rules out.}]{Assets/a9-principles.png}
\caption{The seven principles, with what each rules out.}
\end{figure}

\textbf{P1: Route before you measure, and route on paper.} The scan export, Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s books and Kadamba\textquotesingle s own stated waste share already measure the gap, so connect them to the decisions that need them before adding any instrument. Yuktāhār dropped a commercial ERP over cost and staff readiness (single-sourced), so routes start from a book page and use existing channels: the daily meeting, the tasting, the menu cycle.

\textbf{P2: Make measurement blame-free, and judge it over months.} No recorded figure may justify a deduction against the staff who recorded it. Yuktāhār\textquotesingle s weighing worked only once it was blame-free and discussed daily, and waste took from August to December to fall (single-sourced). One run-out after a smaller batch, judged over a week, would bring the hedge straight back.

\textbf{P3: Change the default, the goal and the belief, not the people.} Treat every actor as rational given its incentives and information. Students are registered by a default they never chose, operators run the only working balancing loops (L11, L12), and the institution states one goal and pursues another. Nudges and campaigns aimed at students, blaming operators, and changing the cancellation count while the default stays untouched are ruled out.

\textbf{P4: Act on the first batch the day before, and on the morning only through the top-up.} Morning levers work through smaller, later top-ups or, where a top-up cannot land in time, a sized reserve. No lever may cut waste by raising shortage risk, and scope stays per hall, weekday, line and, where records allow, item. Kadamba fixes the batch three and a half to four hours before the crest, Bakul can start cooking around 3 AM (single-sourced), and arrival curves and line turnouts differ widely. One hall\textquotesingle s rule is not carried to another before that hall has records.

\textbf{P5: Feed the absorbers by plan, name them as symptomatic outlets, and target only the over-cooked batch, repeated reheating and the bin.} A concept that shrinks surplus must name who eats it and budget their meals. Thirty to fifty outside diners and about half of Kadamba\textquotesingle s staff already eat this food (single-sourced), and calling their meals "not wasted" would hide the gap again, the shifting-the-burden trap in miniature. Restricting resale, closing the back door and cutting the batch without an allowance are ruled out.

\textbf{P6: Measure success as cooked against eaten, run-outs and food quality, not turnout.} Success is the cooked-eaten gap, run-outs, food quality as eaten (holding time and texture as well as temperature and taste) and whether the cost reaches a rule owner. Falling waste with rising run-outs does not count. Some skipping is legitimate (confirmed across interviews), and Bakul\textquotesingle s food, held at about 60 to 72 °C, keeps its temperature while aloo paratha softens (single-sourced). No turnout targets, no waste cuts sized before Kadamba\textquotesingle s 5 to 10\% base is known, and no temperature-only quality checks.

\textbf{P7: Measures are diagnostic and plural, never a single target.} Measures are for learning and never for reward or penalty. Cooked-against-eaten always comes with grams per plate, taste and run-outs, because a kitchen held to one figure can meet it by cutting portions or letting the crest run short, and a vendor rewarded on it can meet it through the rate. The targets in LP3 and LP7 serve review and are not to be gamed. A single headline target, any bonus, deduction or ranking tied to one ledger figure, and waste figures reported alone are ruled out.
