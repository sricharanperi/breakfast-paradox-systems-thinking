Scenario Model and Impact Evaluation
The model explores how the system might respond; it doesn’t predict. Every output is
projected, meaning arithmetic run on real records under stated assumptions. It replays
Kadamba’s April 2026 breakfast records (confirmed, 32,162 registration rows with scan times),
comparing what each rule would have cooked against what was actually eaten, in the real
order of arrivals. Kitchen forecasts use only earlier records, so the backtest covers 23 out-ofsample days, 8 to 30 April; student-side scenarios use the whole month. No food cost or waste
weight is used; money here is simply the posted rate times counted uneaten registrations.

Three limits apply throughout. The records cover one hall, one meal and one month, and rest
on the operator’s own ratio. Kadamba’s stated waste of 5 to 10 percent (base unknown) only
fits a surplus of several hundred portions if most of it is eaten by others, reheated or never
actually cooked, so surplus here means portions cooked beyond what registered students ate,
not waste in the strict sense. And demand is censored: on a run-out day, “eaten” is a floor, so
an unadjusted forecast would drift downward over time, which is exactly what the ledger’s runout rule is there to answer.
