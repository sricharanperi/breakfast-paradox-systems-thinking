Every waste indicator is read alongside the run-out indicator for the same hall and line. Some
results look better before they look worse (a bad crest can bring the hedge straight back
months later), and others look worse before better (supervisor workload in Stage 2, the charge
staying at until week 10), so each expected shape is written down on the ledger in advance.

Who Must Act
Of thirteen actions in total, the student team can complete two entirely on its own: building the
ledger, cards and sheet, and running the co-design tests. It can convene or support four more
that others have to decide: the joint and shared readings (CDS as convenor), taking Yuktāhār’s
routine to Kadamba, the calibrated and 9:00 batches (Prism’s supervisor decides), and
Yuktāhār’s re-anchoring. It can carry six forward as evidenced proposals: the tasting line
(facility team, parent body unknown), a cost threshold (Student Council, Parliament, CDS
Committee), the stated goal (CFS and CDS), the Sunday exit (rule owner, Mess Office or CDS,
unconfirmed), the billing or payment basis (only once the plate-basis question is answered),
and an owner of booked, cooked and eaten (CFS and CDS). And it must complete one thing
outright: the handover, by week 20.

Before and After
Read as a range, first-batch surplus falls from about 254 to 471 portions a day down to about
69 to 120, over roughly four months, and the weekly uneaten charge falls from about ₹2.47
lakh to ₹2.27 to ₹2.37 lakh with the Sunday skip in place, and to ₹1.23 to ₹1.85 lakh if the
every-day skip follows (all projected). The kitchen change is large and the student-side change
stays small until the rule actually moves, which is really just the diagnosis restated as a system
response. Kitchen levers reduce surplus and run-outs; only a rule moves the booking itself;
and the design keeps both in view at once, so the first is never mistaken for the second.
