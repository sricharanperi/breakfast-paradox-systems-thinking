The baseline cooks far ahead of demand (veg runs about 525 a day for 237 eaten). The ratio
cut fails on both counts: 150 surplus still remains (138 on veg), and non-veg still runs short on
7 of 23 days, since its turnout reaches 59 percent on busy days. One at ratio simply can’t
serve two lines 16 points apart. A calibrated single batch cuts surplus down to 60, but it just
moves the cost into the crest instead, 16.8 short a day (13.7 after 9:15), and on 26 April the
veg line would have run out at 9:24, 45 short: L14 running in reverse. With the late batch
added, 274 fewer portions get cooked, leaving 91 surplus and only 2.6 short, which the
ordinary top-up covers; the effective ratio becomes an output ranging from 29 percent (Sunday
veg) to 67 percent (Friday non-veg). Across true ratios of 60 to 80 percent, the reduction runs
165 to 382 portions a day: about 105 to 382 net of an allowance of up to 60, and about 214 to
274 at 70 percent.
