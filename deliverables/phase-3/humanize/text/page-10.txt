The widest rule change costs the kitchen the least, since the four-day lead only protects dry
goods anyway. The key incentive nding here is conditional. Where payment is on registered
plates, cutting surplus lowers cost while revenue holds. That route is open right now, though it
closes if the rate gets renegotiated and turns harmful if it’s achieved through smaller portions.
Where payment is on served plates, the gain is bigger, but so is the pressure to serve more,
and if the Bakul manager’s account holds, Bakul is already absorbing the cost of uneaten
portions and accepting it to avoid shortage (candidate). P7’s paired measures guard against
both cases.

The Natural Comparison
Kadamba and Yuktāhār share registration, billing and, as far as is known, tendered plate
payment. Yuktāhār plans from records, re-cooks by run-out time and weighs blame-free on
paper (books con rmed, practice single-sourced); Kadamba cooks a fixed ratio instead. So a
learning practice is clearly possible inside the current rules, but the comparison can’t prove the
cause, because the two halls differ in five other ways.
