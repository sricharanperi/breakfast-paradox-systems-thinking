P4: Act on the first batch the day before, and on the morning only through the top-up.
First-batch levers act at the day-before plan; morning levers act only through smaller, later topups or, where one can’t land in time, a sized reserve. None of this may cut waste by raising
shortage risk, and scope stays per hall, weekday, line and, where records allow, item. At
Kadamba the batch is fixed three and a half to four hours before the crest, and at Bakul
cooking can start around 3 AM (single-sourced), while arrival curves and line turnouts (32.4
against 48.5 percent) differ substantially. This rules out same-morning signals aimed at the first
batch, shortening safety steps, a single ratio for everything, cutting waste through run-outs, or
moving one hall’s rule onto another before it has its own records.
P5: Feed the absorbers by plan, name them as symptomatic outlets, and target only the
over-cooked batch, repeated reheating and the bin. Any concept that shrinks surplus has to
name who eats it and budget their meals, and the ledger should record absorbers as outlets of
the gap, never as solutions to it. Thirty to fifty outside diners and about half of Kadamba’s staff
already eat from this same food (single-sourced), yet counting their meals as “not wasted”
would just hide the gap again, the shifting-the-burden trap in miniature. This rules out
restricting resale, closing the back door, cutting the batch without an allowance, or reporting
absorbed surplus as a success.
P6: Measure success as cooked against eaten, run-outs and food quality, not turnout.
Success means the cooked-eaten gap, run-outs, food quality as actually eaten (holding time
and texture, not just temperature and taste), and whether the cost reaches a rule owner at all;
waste falling while run-outs rise is not success. Some skipping is entirely legitimate (confirmed
across interviews), and Bakul’s food, held at about 60 to 72°C, keeps its temperature even as
aloo paratha softens (single-sourced). This rules out turnout targets, attendance drives, waste
cuts sized before Kadamba’s own 5 to 10 percent base is even known, and quality checks that
read temperature alone.
P7: Measures are diagnostic and plural, never a single target. Every measure serves
learning, not reward or penalty, and cooked-against-eaten always has to be paired with grams
served per plate, taste and quality, and run-outs. A kitchen held to just one gure can meet it
by cutting portions or letting the crest run short, and a vendor rewarded on it can meet it
through the rate; paired measures close off both of those routes, and the targets in LP3 and
LP7 exist to serve review, not to be gamed. This rules out a single headline target, any bonus,
deduction or ranking tied to one ledger gure, and waste gures reported alone.
