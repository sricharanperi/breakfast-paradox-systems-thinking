LP6: Crest-aware top-up and a staged late batch. At Kadamba, 26.5 percent of April scans
land between 9:15 and 9:30, rising to about nine a minute by 9:29, and 9.2 percent, about 37 a
day, arrive at or after 9:30, the latest at 10:07 (confirmed). The operator’s own estimate of
about 150 diners in the last ten minutes (single-sourced) overstates this, and is a candidate
driver of why the hedge exists. Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour (single-sourced), when
only 15.3 percent of Kadamba’s scans have even arrived (confirmed). The fix has three parts:
a weekday arrival card telling the supervisor at 8:00 what share of the day has already shown
up (place 6); a late batch started shortly after 9:00 based on it (place 10, since it changes
when stock gets created); and a grace period after 9:30, always paired with the late batch.
Place 9 isn’t claimed here. The share seen by 8:00 runs from 13.5 percent on Sundays up to
22.5 on Wednesdays, and the share after 9:15 runs from about 32 percent (Tuesdays,
Wednesdays) to about 46 (Sundays) (confirmed; used as factors, candidate), so a single at
factor would misread Sunday and Wednesday by about a quarter. None of this transfers to
Bakul. The manager places his rush at 8:00 to 8:30 and aims to have food ready for about 50
diners at 9:20 (single-sourced), and a top-up there needs a phone call, cooking at the Ha zpet
kitchen and a drive of about 8 km, roughly 20 minutes if called early and 40 otherwise, with the
end-to-end lead time never actually measured. The Bakul version of this lever is really about
the size of that held reserve and the latest call that can still land, set from measured data. The
reserve is a hedge, not an absorber (candidate). LP6 is part of the quick fix.
