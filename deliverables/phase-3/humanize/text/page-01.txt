The Breakfast Paradox: Leverage
Points, Intervention Design and
Recommendation
Team Invictus
This report covers Phases 3 and 4 of the Breakfast Paradox project: why IIIT Hyderabad
students get billed for breakfasts most of them never eat, and what could change that. Activity
9 works out where and how the system can be changed, producing the Leverage-Point /
System Intervention Map and the Design Principles. Activity 10 takes it further. It designs and
tests interventions against the system as a whole rather than any one stakeholder’s interest,
and delivers the intervention concepts and prototypes, the scenario model and evaluation
framework, and the recommended intervention.
We start from the Phase 2 reframe. The Breakfast Paradox isn’t a broken system; it’s a stable
equilibrium, and three structures hold it there. Registration is an automatic default with a
narrow way out: five cancellations per meal type per month, each made at least four days
ahead, with no way to undo the charge beyond that (confirmed). Billing fires at the moment of
booking, not at the moment of eating (confirmed). And the gap between the two is measured
but never routed anywhere. The CDS scan record and the operators’ own books both capture
it, but neither reaches whoever sets the registration, billing or menu rules (confirmed).
Underneath the kitchen side sits one intermediate mechanism: turning registrations into a
cooked first batch. Kadamba cooks a fixed 70 percent of registrations per line (single-sourced).
Bakul (Vijayalakshmi Caterers) cooks 70 to 80 percent by item, a gure its manager sets each
week from his read of past attendance, never letting it drop below about 70 percent even on
the quietest weekends (single-sourced). Yuktāhār plans from its own books. Every kitchen can
top up a batch, but none will shrink one. Whatever the gap produces gets absorbed long
before it becomes a signal anyone notices: through resale, top-ups, reuse, the meals of
outside diners and staff, late service, holding and reheating, and nally the bin. Each actor is
responding rationally to the incentives and information it has; the outcome nobody wants is
what happens when those responses connect. The design opportunity, then, is to give the
system a way to notice its own cost, by routing the records that already exist to the people who
set the first batch and the rules, and by testing the beliefs that keep the working goal fixed on
the registration count.

Claims here carry the project’s con dence vocabulary. Confirmed means read directly off a

posted rates) or seen on site. Single-sourced means it rests on one account: the Kadamba
operator’s gures are first-hand, so the numbers themselves aren’t in doubt, but they remain
his account of his own practice; the Bakul manager’s gures come from a team member’s
synthesis of an interview with him. There are no Bakul records of any kind, so no Bakul effect
is sized anywhere in this report. Candidate means an inference built by combining sources,
