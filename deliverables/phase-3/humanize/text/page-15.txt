1.6 Deliverable 2: Design Principles
Seven principles follow from the leverage points, the archetypes and the evidence. Each one is
a test that any intervention concept in Activity 10 has to pass.
P1: Route before you measure, and route on paper. Connect the records that already exist
to the decisions that need them, before adding any new instrument. The gap is already
measured, in the CDS export, in Yuktāhār’s books, in Kadamba’s own stated waste share, and
Yuktāhār already dropped a commercial ERP system over cost and staff readiness (singlesourced), so routes should start from a book page and travel on channels that already exist:
the daily meeting, the tasting, the menu cycle. This rules out new apps, sensors or forecasting
software as a first step, surveys that just re-measure what records already hold, and any
design that needs operators to go digital.
P2: Make measurement blame-free, and judge it over months. No gure an intervention
records should ever become grounds for a deduction against the staff who recorded it, and
results should be judged over months, not days. Yuktāhār’s weighing only worked once it was
made blame-free and discussed daily, and the fall in waste took from August to December
(single-sourced); one run-out after a smaller batch, judged over just a week, would be enough
to bring the hedge straight back. This rules out quantity checks framed as audits, linking waste
to deductions, and one-week trials.

P3: Change the default, the goal and the belief, not the people. Aim at the default on the
student side, the goal on the operator and institution side, and the beliefs holding both in place,
treating every actor as responding rationally to its own incentives and information. Students
are registered by a default they never chose and hold the least power of anyone in the system;
operators run the only balancing loops that actually work (L11, L12); the institution states one
goal and pursues another. This rules out nudges and campaigns aimed at students, any logic
of “students should just register more accurately,” blaming operators, or changing the
cancellation count while leaving the default untouched.
