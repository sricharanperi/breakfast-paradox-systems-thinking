LP5: Record-calibrated first batch with a daily learning record. L11 closes at Yuktāhār,
runs as a candidate instance bounded by a floor at Bakul, and is simply absent at Kadamba.
Creating it at Kadamba sits at place 6; strengthening it where it already runs sits at place 8.
Kadamba cooks 70 percent of registrations per line, but biryani lunch runs at 100 percent
(single-sourced), so the ratio is following expectation, not a record. April turnout came in at
32.4 percent veg and 48.5 percent non-veg (confirmed), meaning the batch is roughly 2.2
times what was eaten on the veg line and about 1.4 times on non-veg (candidate arithmetic).
The fix sets the batch as the gram standard multiplied by expected turnout per hall, weekday
and line, plus a named allowance for outside diners and staff and afloat for bonda, puri, vada
and idli, starting on the veg line, with a one-page daily paper record read the next day.
Kadamba already cooks about 30 extra portions for outside diners, and about half its staff eat
there too (single-sourced), so their meals and the raw reserve need to be budgeted in. The
lever can’t yet be sized precisely: a 70 percent batch against about 398 eaten implies roughly
350 surplus portions (candidate), which sits oddly against Kadamba’s own stated 5 to 10
percent waste gure. At Bakul, which cooks 70 to 80 percent by item and keeps food for about
70 percent even on very low weekends against a turnout the manager puts at most around 60
percent (single-sourced), item-level cooked-and-eaten gures would let us check his weekly
adjustment; his floor stays a named constraint regardless. It is a quick fix, paired with LP1
against the L17 risk and with LP6 against run-outs.
