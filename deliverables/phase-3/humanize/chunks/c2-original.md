## 1.3 Develop Design Principles

The objective is a breakfast system where registration matches each student’s actual intent, the kitchen cooks to expected eaters, and the cost of any gap reaches the people who set the rules. It doesn’t mean eliminating registration, resale or all waste. Registration gives the planning count, resale recovers money, and some surplus is simply the price of never running out. The aim is to make over-booking and over-cooking exceptions someone notices, not the norm. The guiding idea follows straight from the archetype reading: change the architecture, don’t compete at the kitchen layer.

The status quo persists because it pays each actor something.

*Table 7. The case for the status quo*

| Actor | What it gives | Cost of change | Cost of not changing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | No decision; a guaranteed seat; resale income | A nightly decision; less resale | Paying for uneaten breakfasts (63 percent of Kadamba’s April registrations); reheated food |
| Prism (Kadamba) | Revenue possibly on registered plates; few run-outs | Possible revenue fall; a daily record | Food cooked that no one eats; reheating |
| Bakul operator | No shortage, secured by a floor and reserve (single-sourced) | A check on the manager’s judgement | Food held hot for hours, softening items such as aloo paratha |
| CDS | Low workload | A standing ledger; portal changes | A stated goal it cannot show it meets |
| Staff and outside diners | Food from surplus | Meals must be budgeted | Meals stay a by-product |
| CFS | A predictable budget | A budget varying with consumption | Spending on food nobody eats |

The levers keep what matters to each actor, the seat, the resale, the absorbers, run-out protection, while changing what actually drives over-booking. CDS and the Mess Office, who gain the most from low workload, own the rules the fundamental fix touches, so LP1, LP11 and LP12 need to reach them before any rule proposal does. The principles themselves make up Deliverable 2, below.

## 1.6 Deliverable 2: Design Principles

Seven principles follow from the leverage points, the archetypes and the evidence. Each one is a test that any intervention concept in Activity 10 has to pass.

**P1: Route before you measure, and route on paper.** Connect the records that already exist to the decisions that need them, before adding any new instrument. The gap is already measured, in the CDS export, in Yuktāhār’s books, in Kadamba’s own stated waste share, and Yuktāhār already dropped a commercial ERP system over cost and staff readiness (single-sourced), so routes should start from a book page and travel on channels that already exist: the daily meeting, the tasting, the menu cycle. This rules out new apps, sensors or forecasting software as a first step, surveys that just re-measure what records already hold, and any design that needs operators to go digital.

**P2: Make measurement blame-free, and judge it over months.** No figure an intervention records should ever become grounds for a deduction against the staff who recorded it, and results should be judged over months, not days. Yuktāhār’s weighing only worked once it was made blame-free and discussed daily, and the fall in waste took from August to December (single-sourced); one run-out after a smaller batch, judged over just a week, would be enough to bring the hedge straight back. This rules out quantity checks framed as audits, linking waste to deductions, and one-week trials.

**P3: Change the default, the goal and the belief, not the people.** Aim at the default on the student side, the goal on the operator and institution side, and the beliefs holding both in place, treating every actor as responding rationally to its own incentives and information. Students are registered by a default they never chose and hold the least power of anyone in the system; operators run the only balancing loops that actually work (L11, L12); the institution states one goal and pursues another. This rules out nudges and campaigns aimed at students, any logic of “students should just register more accurately,” blaming operators, or changing the cancellation count while leaving the default untouched.

**P4: Act on the first batch the day before, and on the morning only through the top-up.** First-batch levers act at the day-before plan; morning levers act only through smaller, later top-ups or, where one can’t land in time, a sized reserve. None of this may cut waste by raising shortage risk, and scope stays per hall, weekday, line and, where records allow, item. At Kadamba the batch is fixed three and a half to four hours before the crest, and at Bakul cooking can start around 3 AM (single-sourced), while arrival curves and line turnouts (32.4 against 48.5 percent) differ substantially. This rules out same-morning signals aimed at the first batch, shortening safety steps, a single ratio for everything, cutting waste through run-outs, or moving one hall’s rule onto another before it has its own records.

**P5: Feed the absorbers by plan, name them as symptomatic outlets, and target only the over-cooked batch, repeated reheating and the bin.** Any concept that shrinks surplus has to name who eats it and budget their meals, and the ledger should record absorbers as outlets of the gap, never as solutions to it. Thirty to fifty outside diners and about half of Kadamba’s staff already eat from this same food (single-sourced), yet counting their meals as “not wasted” would just hide the gap again, the shifting-the-burden trap in miniature. This rules out restricting resale, closing the back door, cutting the batch without an allowance, or reporting absorbed surplus as a success.

**P6: Measure success as cooked against eaten, run-outs and food quality, not turnout.** Success means the cooked-eaten gap, run-outs, food quality as actually eaten (holding time and texture, not just temperature and taste), and whether the cost reaches a rule owner at all; waste falling while run-outs rise is not success. Some skipping is entirely legitimate (confirmed across interviews), and Bakul’s food, held at about 60 to 72°C, keeps its temperature even as aloo paratha softens (single-sourced). This rules out turnout targets, attendance drives, waste cuts sized before Kadamba’s own 5 to 10 percent base is even known, and quality checks that read temperature alone.

**P7: Measures are diagnostic and plural, never a single target.** Every measure serves learning, not reward or penalty, and cooked-against-eaten always has to be paired with grams served per plate, taste and quality, and run-outs. A kitchen held to just one figure can meet it by cutting portions or letting the crest run short, and a vendor rewarded on it can meet it through the rate; paired measures close off both of those routes, and the targets in LP3 and LP7 exist to serve review, not to be gamed. This rules out a single headline target, any bonus, deduction or ranking tied to one ledger figure, and waste figures reported alone.
