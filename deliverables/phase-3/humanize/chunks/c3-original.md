# 2 Activity 10: Design, Test and Evaluate Interventions

Activity 10 develops and assesses interventions that improve system-level outcomes rather than optimising for any one stakeholder’s interest. Its three deliverables are the intervention concepts and prototypes (Deliverable 3, the first two sections below), the scenario model and evaluation framework (Deliverable 4, from the scenario model through the unintended consequences), and the final system design recommendation (Deliverable 5, after the refinement). The shifting-the-burden reading from Activity 9 sets the order throughout. The kitchen levers (LP5 and LP6) are the quick fix: they save food and buy time without touching why too many plates get booked and cooked in the first place. The route, the default and its exit, the goal, and the beliefs (LP1, LP2, LP12 and LP11) are the fundamental fix, so the recommendation runs them on fixed dates regardless of what the kitchen pilot shows.

## 2.1 Generate Alternative Interventions (Deliverable 3: Intervention Concepts)

These concepts target the three confirmed structures holding the equilibrium in place (registration as a standing default with a narrow exit, billing at the booking, and a gap that’s measured but unrouted), plus the step where registrations turn into a first batch. Kadamba cooks a fixed 70 percent of registrations per line; Bakul (Vijayalakshmi Caterers) cooks 70 to 80 percent by item, set weekly by its manager’s judgement and never below about 70 percent (both single-sourced); Yuktāhār plans from its own books. Every kitchen can top up a batch but none will shrink one. Concept G is a deliberate foil, carried through every test so that its failure gets shown rather than just asserted.

**A: The Breakfast Ledger.** Each week, the CDS scan export and the operators’ books become one sheet per hall, by weekday and line, including the money charged for uneaten breakfasts. That charge line matters because it stops a falling surplus from being read as “problem solved”: kitchen levers shrink surplus while the charge stays flat until a rule actually moves. A threshold on cooked-against-eaten, anchored outside the hall’s own past, alerts the escalation forum alongside the existing complaint trigger, and a weekly note of exams and events flows down to planning. A routes L10, adds a second trigger to L4, counters L17, and brings L7’s exam dips to the kitchen (P1, P2, P6, P7).

**B: The Kitchen Learning Kit.** Kadamba’s fixed ratio becomes a day-before plan card built from expected eaters, with a named allowance for the 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba’s staff, plus a daily paper sheet read the next day. An operator forum, convened by CDS and hosted by Yuktāhār’s project managers, carries over the preconditions behind Yuktāhār’s Wastage Book: blame-free weighing, daily discussion, and surplus treated as cost. At Bakul, whose manager already adjusts weekly by item, the kit works more as a check than a new rule, with the floor kept in place until a record shows how often a smaller batch would actually run short. Nothing there is sized yet. B creates L11 at Kadamba (a candidate second instance at Bakul), shortens L13’s kitchen half, and reaches L8 through cost (P1 to P7).

**C: The Crest Plan.** At 9:00 the supervisor projects the day from a weekday arrival card and starts a late batch that lands by about 9:15, before the crest, so the first batch only needs to cover the earlier diners. A grace period after 9:30 covers late-batch food only. All of this rests on Kadamba’s 10 to 15 minute on-site top-up. Bakul’s top-up needs a phone call, cooking at the Hafizpet kitchen and a 20 to 40 minute drive (single-sourced), so its hedge is about 50 portions held at 9:20 (candidate), and its version of C is really about sizing that reserve and the latest call that can still land. C gives L12 sight of the crest, removes L14’s reason to hedge, and bounds L15 (P4, P5, P6).

**D: The Sunday Exit.** Sunday, the lowest-turnout day at both recorded halls, is first drafted as opt-in: charged only if confirmed by 8 pm Saturday. A lighter variant keeps the default and adds a skip, open until the same hour, that removes the charge and lowers the kitchen’s count. Other days keep the four-day lead and the cap of five cancellations, and no skip may ever cost a hall place. D acts on L13’s booking end, on L2 (Skip Meal fails because a skip currently returns nothing) and on L1 (P3, P4, P5). Under opt-in, a student who forgets to confirm simply pays the walk-in rate.

**E: The Operator Menu Slot.** At each two-week menu cycle, operators bring the items that wasted most (uttapam, set dosa and upma at Kadamba, single-sourced) and those that ran out. The Bakul manager’s view that pairings are the real problem (single-sourced) sets the guard here: operator input gets read alongside consumption data and residents’ actual wishes, as a voice, never as authority. E supplies L16’s missing link, which only becomes a loop once it’s actually used (P1, P6).

**F: Billed at Consumption (held).** Breakfast gets charged on the scan, exactly as already done during an LPG shortage and some fest periods (confirmed), acting on L1, L2, L6 and L8 (P3, P5, subject to a check on resale income). It stays held because the operator payment basis is unconfirmed: the Bakul manager reports plates served (single-sourced), while the Kadamba operator says it cooks on registrations and is paid for that. The hold lifts only once each operator’s basis is confirmed.

**G: Ratio Cut to 50 Percent (foil).** Kadamba’s first batch drops from 70 to 50 percent by instruction, with no record, late batch or allowance attached. It changes no loop, and it breaks both P4 and P5.

**H: The Shared Reading.** Joint sessions around the ledger ask each belief holder what leads to their figure, and what would change it: the Yuktāhār site head’s “about 50 percent” set against his own register at about 28, the Kadamba supervisor’s crest prediction, the registration side’s reasons for the automatic default. The Bakul manager’s beliefs wait on a Bakul record. CFS and CDS are asked to carry the goal already on the CDS poster, minimising food wastage through advance planning, as two indicators, booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, read alongside P7’s quality measures. H acts on the mental models beneath every chain and on the working goal behind L10, L13 and L17 (P2, P3, P6, P7).
