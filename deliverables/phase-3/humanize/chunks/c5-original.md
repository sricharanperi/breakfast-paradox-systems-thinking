## 2.4 Test Interventions Against System Objectives (Deliverable 4: Evaluation Framework)

The objectives come from the reframe, and belong to the system rather than any one stakeholder: notice its own cost, cut the first batch without trading waste for run-outs, make no one worse off, and be usable. Failing any of four gates makes a concept ineligible, whatever its score.

**G1, absorbers kept:** resale, reuse, outside-diner and staff meals, walk-ins, late service and outside venues all survive, and only the over-cooked batch, reheating and the bin are targeted (P5). **G2, safety kept:** every food-safety step stays intact (P4). **G3, aimed at structure:** it neither asks students to behave better nor blames operators, and it isn’t gated on an unknown that could reverse it (P3; LP10 held). **G4, quick fix paired:** any kitchen lever comes with a route, default, goal or belief lever landing on a date independent of kitchen performance.

### Interventions Across the Iceberg

At the level of events, the daily sheet records batches, run-outs and leftovers, and marks run-out days as censored, flagged by run-out time per line. Of thirteen actions in total, the student team can complete two entirely on its own: building the ledger, cards and sheet, and running the co-design tests. ### Before and After

Read as a range, first-batch surplus falls from about 254 to 471 portions a day down to about 69 to 120, over roughly four months, and the weekly uneaten charge falls from about ₹2.47 lakh to ₹2.27 to ₹2.37 lakh with the Sunday skip in place, and to ₹1.23 to ₹1.85 lakh if the every-day skip follows (all projected). The kitchen change is large and the student-side change stays small until the rule actually moves, which is really just the diagnosis restated as a system response. Kitchen levers reduce surplus and run-outs; only a rule moves the booking itself; and the design keeps both in view at once, so the first is never mistaken for the second.

# 3 Key Findings

1. The system is missing routes, not measurement. The enabling lever sits at place 6 of 12: routing records that already exist to the kitchen and to the rule owners, and a student team can prototype it right now as a paper ledger.
2. The loops form a shifting-the-burden structure. The kitchen levers are the quick fix, and the booking, goal, belief and route levers are the fundamental fix, so the recommendation runs the fundamental fix on fixed dates, whatever the kitchen pilot shows.
3. The deepest levers are the beliefs that let the registration count stand in for demand (place 2) and the working goal the system actually seeks (place 3). The deepest rule lever that isn’t gated on an unknown is the breakfast default and its exit (place 5), provided no skip ever costs a student a hall slot.
4. Cutting Kadamba’s ratio to 50 percent fails on both counts, leaving about 150 surplus portions a day and starting non-veg run-outs. A calibrated first batch on its own just moves the cost into the crest; add a 9:00 late batch, and first-batch surplus falls from about 254 to 471 portions a day to about 69 to 120, with a residue the ordinary top-up covers (all projected). The design fails loud when eaters exceed the forecast, so the raw reserve and the stop rules are essential parts of it, not add-ons.
5. Kitchen levers leave the student cost completely untouched: ₹10.6 lakh was charged in April alone for uneaten Kadamba breakfasts (projected). Only a rule that actually returns something moves that number. A Sunday skip is unlikely to run the crest short below about two-thirds uptake on non-veg, so it can go on a fixed date now, while the every-day skip waits on its own decision rule (projected).
6. Kadamba and Yuktāhār show that a learning practice is possible under the very same rules, but the two halls differ in five other ways and still leave a similar breakfast gap, so the cause of that difference stays a candidate reading, not a conclusion. Targets are anchored outside each hall’s own past and paired with portion, quality and run-out measures, and no lever may cut waste by raising shortage risk.
7. Every modelled figure in this report is Kadamba’s. Bakul, the third regime, has no records at all. It needs its own scans, a measured top-up lead time and an item-level record before any lever can be sized there. Sunday breakfast on Kadamba’s veg line is the first test slice.
