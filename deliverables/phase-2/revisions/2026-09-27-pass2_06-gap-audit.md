## E. Phase 3 implications

1. **LP-E (first-batch calibration) cannot be designed as a pure cut.** 30-50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff eat from the same food. Per D-45/D-49 any calibration must provision for them explicitly (e.g. a counted allocation), or it takes food from people the current surplus quietly feeds.
2. **An internal benchmark exists.** Yuktāhār's book system plus first-half-hour projection is a working, paper-based forecasting practice on the same campus under the same registration rules. Transfer to Kadamba is a candidate intervention, but EB2 shows its preconditions: blame-free measurement (resistance at first), daily meetings, peer cross-checking, and a culture that treats waste as cost. Kadamba's layered chain and "paid for registrations" stance may block it; operator culture and incentive are candidate leverage points.
3. **Any new measurement must be blame-free and paper-first.** Yuktāhār dropped an ERP on cost and staff digital readiness. Interventions that assume digital tools on the kitchen side should be tested against this.
4. **The first-half-hour projection needs a crest correction.** About a quarter of Kadamba diners arrive in the last 15 minutes and about 20% in the last 10. Any in-service forecast rule (LP for live signalling) should weight late arrivals, not extrapolate the opening rate.
5. **The four-day registration lock is not needed for anything that is cooked.** Only dry goods are bought more than four days ahead. This strengthens the case for shortening the cancellation lead time or the lock (rule-level lever), while the plate basis still holds back billing levers (D-54). Kadamba's "paid for it" remark is a hint, not an answer.
6. **The safety sequence fixes the first batch before 7:30.** Levers that act on the first batch must act the day before or earlier (registration count, calibration ratio), not in the morning. Levers that act during service work through top-up (smaller, more frequent batches).
7. **Quality is a second outcome of over-preparation.** If the reheat chain holds, smaller, more frequent batches reduce both waste and quality complaints at Kadamba. This gives a quality-side reason operators and students could share, but it needs direct observation first.
8. **The display plate is an existing daily touchpoint** at the entrance that could carry more than the menu (for example, expected crowd or leftovers), with no new infrastructure.
9. **Registration is an automatic default with a narrow exit.** Levers on the student side should target the default and the exit (cancellation count, lead time, a real nullify option) rather than persuasion.
10. **Evidence gaps to add to `00-phase3-input-brief.md` §5:** base of the 5-10% waste figure; extra-diner count and entitlement; reheating observation; back-door entry counts; Skip Meal conflict; CDS head identity.
