## 0. Headline verdict

The deliverable was built when the project had no operator-side evidence and no records. Its whole diagnosis sits on the student/registration side (L1, L2, L5, L7) and the governance side (L4, L6, L10), with the kitchen treated as a black box. The new evidence opens that box, and the most important feedback in the system turns out to live inside it:

1. **Two new balancing loops with evidenced closure** now exist, both kitchen-side: the Yuktahar waste-learning loop (days-to-weeks) and the counter-monitoring/top-up loop (minutes, all three operators). Under D-40 these are the best-evidenced loops in the whole project after L4, and the deliverable has neither.
2. **The core causal chain of the Breakfast Paradox is missing**: registration as a standing booking (flat ~1,070/day, turnout 27-42% by weekday) feeding a fixed private conversion ratio (70% / 70-80% / believed 50%) into a cooked first batch that exceeds turnout. It is a chain, not a loop (no route back to registration), which is itself the diagnostic point.
3. **The closing rush is a two-sided failure** (shortage and surplus on the same morning) created by a delay (top-up lag) meeting a late crest; this produces a candidate "just in case" ratchet (Vijayalakshmi 70% to 80%).
4. **Four P0 contradictions**: composting (L9), "no source measures waste or tracks attendance" (cross-loop synthesis), "no channel exists to notice silent predictable skipping" (L5 / synthesis), and L8's "confirmed severed by design" (vendor plate basis is unknown).
5. **No delays are marked anywhere** in the deliverable. T-4 registration close, T-1 quantity planning, 10-15 min / 30 min top-up, 2-week matching-day lookup, and August-to-December culture change are all now evidenced.
6. **Framework wording (D-7)**: section headers do not echo the Activity 7 methods ("Identify reinforcing and balancing feedback loops", "Analyse unintended consequences", "Identify structural causes and systemic tensions"). There is no explicit Unintended Consequences section even though the new evidence supplies several.

## 7. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)
1. **Highest-leverage point is an information flow, not a new measurement (Meadows: information flows).** The gap is already measured daily in the CDS scans and the operators' registers; the missing element is a route from those measurements to the registration/billing rule owner. Intervention point: a routine that puts per-hall, per-weekday breakfast turnout in front of whoever sets the registration rule.
2. **Rules leverage at the booking, not the procurement.** G-07 shows the weekly block + 4-day close makes registration a standing booking. Changing registration granularity (daily or T-1 opt-out for breakfast, aligned with the T-1 perishables cadence operators already use) acts at the head of the core chain. Interventions targeting the T-4 procurement lock miss, because the binding constraint is the cooked first batch.
3. **Parameter leverage that is cheap and immediate: calibrate the conversion ratio to recorded turnout by weekday and item.** The Yuktahar loop (G-05) is an in-house, proven design pattern; Kadamba and the off-site kitchen lack it. Design principle: calibrate to turnout, not registration; start from records that already exist.
4. **Delay leverage at the close.** The closing crest meets a 10-30 min top-up lag. Options: smaller first batch plus faster or staged top-up for items that hold; pre-positioned raw material (Kadamba already does); for the off-site kitchen, a transport-aware buffer rule. Design principle: make shortage at close cheap to fix so operators need not hedge with surplus (break the G-08 ratchet).
5. **Balance loud and silent signals.** Shortages generate complaints; surplus generates nothing. Design principle: give surplus a voice equal to shortage (e.g. item-level waste reported with the same visibility as complaints), and route item-level waste to the menu owner (closes the absent G-10 loop, building on the menu-rotation channel that already closes L4).
6. **Build on the operator's own practice and culture.** Yuktahar's success came from routine and staff buy-in over four months, paper-first, and an explicit rejection of imposed software ("I want to remain close to the data"). Design principle: operator-owned, paper-compatible, cross-checked "not to find fault"; do not impose a dashboard the kitchen did not ask for.
7. **Formalise the events feed.** Events, exams and holidays reach the kitchen only if someone mentions them. A formal institute-to-kitchen calendar feed is a small, low-conflict intervention point.
8. **Respect D-45 but separate absorption routes by who benefits.** Staff meals and reuse are locally beneficial absorbers (do not remove them); the bin is not, and Kadamba has no composting, so a downstream diversion is a legitimate secondary intervention, not a manufactured-visibility one.
9. **Target breakfast and Sunday specifically.** Lunch works under the same booking system; Sunday breakfast is worst (27% Kadamba, ~18-24% Yuktahar). Interventions can be scoped narrowly, which lowers institutional resistance.
10. **Check the vendor's plate basis before designing any billing intervention.** If vendors are paid per registered plate, a billing change shifts vendor revenue and will meet vendor resistance (candidate vendor-interest loop); if per served plate, vendors are natural allies for attendance-tracked registration.
11. **The shock-adaptive precedent (L6) plus the June low-registration pages** remain the evidence that attendance-tracked registration is feasible here; the new operator evidence shows kitchens already handle same-day demand via live scans and top-up, lowering the feasibility risk of a lighter registration commitment.
