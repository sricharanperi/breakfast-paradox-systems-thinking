## 0. Headline verdict

Pass 1's core move (measured but unrouted; the cooked first batch as the point of no return; four intervention points; the absorber constraint) survives and is **strengthened** by Brief #2. Yuktāhār now supplies a much fuller proof that a learning loop can run inside the current registration and billing rules. Five things are wrong or stale:

1. **P0, correction table:** the absorber constraint (§5, line 88) still says "20 to 30 cooking and security staff" eat Kadamba surplus. Brief #2 replaces this with **30 to 50 outside people (about 30 extra portions, e.g. night security) plus about half of Kadamba's internal staff**.
2. **P0, correction table:** §8 (line 142) says the Kadamba conversion ratio and "all waste weights" are "single-sourced from noisy recordings". The Kadamba 70% is now **observer-verified**, and the kg waste figures are **withdrawn**, not merely noisy. The Kadamba waste figure is now "about 5 to 10% daily, base unstated", and it is in open tension with 70% prep against 37% turnout.
3. **P0, correction table:** the rule is described throughout as "a standing booking closed about four days ahead" (lines 22, 34, 64, 124, 140). Brief #2 (confirmed) says registration is **automatic and mandatory**, the only exit is **5 cancellations per meal type per month, each at least 4 days ahead**, and there is **no way to nullify the charge**. Revised Phase 1 now calls it "a default rather than a choice". The rule-level lever therefore concerns the **default and its exit**, not how far ahead a student books.
4. **P0/P1, flags the document must carry:** (a) Kadamba says it **cooks on registrations, full stop**. This conflicts with the earlier "Skip Meal reaches the kitchen" finding. The deliverable already calls that finding unresolved (line 102), but it does not yet carry Kadamba's statement. (b) Brief #2 says students are auto-registered **"including over holidays"**. This is in tension with the deliverable's claim (line 62) that attendance-tracked billing ran "during holiday periods" (D-32 precedent). Flag it; do not resolve it.
5. **P1, missing:** operator stance and culture as part of the opportunity. Under the same billing basis, Kadamba treats the registration count as its paid obligation, while Yuktāhār treats waste reduction as a profit lever. Blame-free measurement was the precondition for Yuktāhār's loop. The ERP was dropped because the books already hold the data and staff are not digitally inclined. The pre-service food-safety sequence fixes the first batch before any turnout signal. The deliverable's claim that "the kitchen side can move first" (line 110) and its framing of the operator as wanting "a voice and a better input" (line 48) both need qualifying for Kadamba.

## 5. Phase 3 implications (Activity 9: leverage points; rules, roles, incentives, information flows, processes; design principles; intervention points)

- **Existence proof inside the current rules.** Yuktāhār runs a before-service estimate (matching week, month, events) and an in-service projection (first half hour) from its own books, under the same auto-registration and billing as Kadamba. So LP-E-type first-batch calibration is achievable without any rule change. Phase 3 should treat Yuktāhār's book system as the reference process, and not invent a new one.
- **Calibration can use Kadamba's existing standards.** The per-dish gram standards and separate veg/non-veg planning mean calibration can replace "70% of registrations" with "gram standard × expected turnout per line, per weekday". Floating items (bonda, puri, vada, idli) need their own margin. Veg turnout of 32.4% vs non-veg 48.5% says the lines need different factors.
- **Absorber check (D-45/D-49) must be recalculated.** The Phase 3 input brief (`deliverables/phase-3/00-phase3-input-brief.md` lines 99, 245) still says "20-30 staff". Any first-batch reduction must explicitly provision the 30-50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff. Held-and-reheated surplus is an absorber with a quality cost, not one to protect.
- **New design principle: blame-free measurement.** Any new measurement or routing (LP-A routing, the cost-triggered escalation) must be designed so kitchen staff are not penalised by what they record. Yuktāhār's loop only worked after this was made explicit.
- **New design principle: stay close to the data, low-tech first.** Build on paper books and daily meetings; do not assume ERP adoption, since the best-run operator rejected it on cost and staff grounds.
- **Timing constraint sharpened.** The first batch is cooked from 5:30 and passes a safety sequence (temperature, 72 h sample, display plate, serving temperature, tasting) before service. Calibration inputs must land by the evening before or at the planning meeting. Live-cooked items (dosa from 7:00) are an existing just-in-time pattern that could be extended. Safety steps are not tradeable.
- **Rule lever restated.** The lever is the automatic default and its capped exit (5 per meal type per month, 4 days ahead, no nullification), plus what triggers the bill. It is not a "standing booking" a student chooses. Candidate interventions include changing the default or widening the exit for breakfast only. They stay gated on the plate basis (D-54).
- **Incentive and culture as leverage.** The same billing basis produces "cook to the count, it's our obligation" (Kadamba) and "cut waste, it's profit" (Yuktāhār). Phase 3 should list operator incentive and culture (training, blame-free norms, a waste-share target) as a lever class under "incentives" and "roles". It should also recognise that first-batch calibration at Kadamba may not self-start without an incentive change.
- **Oversight gap as a roles lever.** The facility team, weekly QC and TDS checks cover safety and taste every meal but not quantity. Extending an existing oversight actor's remit to cooked vs eaten is a candidate roles intervention that needs no new body.
- **Forecast method caveat.** Any in-service projection (Yuktāhār's first half hour, Kadamba's counter watching) underestimates the closing crest (26.5% of scans in the last 15 min). Phase 3 designs that rely on in-service sampling must protect late-window service, including back-door arrivals after 9:30.
- **Evidence limits to carry.** One visit per mess. The team's synthesis is not a verbatim operator transcript. Operator self-presentation bias applies. Kadamba's 5-10% has no base. Raju/Giri, the Skip Meal conflict and the holiday regime each need one direct confirmation before Phase 3 relies on them.
