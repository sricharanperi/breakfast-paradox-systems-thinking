## 0. Summary verdict

The register was built when the project's only evidence was six student interviews, a self-account and one CFS Chair conversation. It now has a whole new evidence class (records: 32,162 scan rows plus photographed operator registers) and a whole new stakeholder class (three catering operators), neither of which it registers. Four of its claims are now contradicted (P0), most of the rest can be upgraded or need scoping, and its "What Remains Unvalidated" list is half resolved and half missing the new open questions. Structurally, the register needs two new sections (a source/provenance register and an operator testimony register) and its tension list needs re-deriving against the revised Phase 1 three-layer story (booking layer, kitchen conversion layer, governance layer).

Headers: the existing section headers are project-invented ("Assumptions Carried From Phase 1...", "Stakeholder Testimony Register", "Tensions, Evidenced", "What Remains Unvalidated"). Per D-7 they should be re-anchored to the framework's own Activity 8 method wording, which this register serves: "Challenge Initial Assumptions", "Compare Stakeholder Perspectives", "Identify Contradictions and Tensions", "Validate the Reframing with Evidence".

- **Proposed change:** status becomes "Revised, then left open. Cuisine is a candidate influence on booking only; attendance by hall exists for Kadamba and Yuktahar only; Bakul and Palash share one kitchen and have no records." Remove "three independently operated" and "share only one variable".

### G-02 (P0, contradicts_existing) Section 2 "Confirmed absence of a stakeholder's voice": "Faculty and early-shift staff ... do not appear in ... any registration data this project has access to"
- Yuktahar's September attendance register has an F/S (faculty/staff) column: breakfast 0-2 per day, lunch 0-3 per day (REC, `csv/02`, `csv/01`). - **Proposed change:** replace with "Faculty and staff do appear in the Yuktahar register, in small daily counts; operator staff also eat leftover food at Kadamba." This also narrows MASTER_CONTEXT §9's assumed "whether faculty or early-shift staff consume mess food" item.

### G-03 (P0, contradicts_existing) Section 3, "Administrative simplicity against real variation": "one average-based portioning method applied across four structurally different kitchens"
- There is no single portioning method. Each operator converts registrations to a cooked first batch with its own private rule: Yuktahar uses a twice-daily meeting, the matching-week Wastage Book (weeks 1&3 / 2&4 menu cycle) and a weekday attendance register, with a run-out-time re-cook rule (OP-CLEAR + REC); Kadamba cooks about 70% of registrations first, top-up in 10-15 min (OP-NOISY); Vijayalakshmi cooks 70%, now 80%, top-up in ~30 min from off-site (OP-NOISY). Every ratio sits above recorded turnout (Kadamba 37.1% REC; Yuktahar ~28% REC). And there are three kitchens, not four (Bakul/Palash shared).
- The run-out of idli/puri/bhatura cited as "the direct cost of uniformity" is now better explained by the late crest (26.5% of Kadamba scans in 9:15-9:30, rate climbing to ~9/min/day at 9:29, REC) against a first batch that cannot be shrunk and a top-up with lag. Kadamba names puri, idli, vada, bonda as items that "move" (OP-NOISY), consistent with run-outs.
- **Proposed change:** rewrite the tension as "One registration rule against three private conversion rules": the rule is uniform (standing booking, one billing basis) but the conversion from booking to cooked food is fragmented, private and uncalibrated. Evidence: the three ratios vs the two turnout records.

### G-04 (P0, contradicts_existing) Section 3, "Procurement certainty against demand-matching accuracy" and Section 2 governance facts: "The standing model gives the vendor a number it can plan four days out"; "that same four-day figure is what gets communicated to the vendor"; "Weekly stock assessment"
- The four-day lock applies to registration, not procurement (Phase 1 revised, D-32 style correction). Operators read the count from the portal and set quantities the day before: Yuktahar's first-half meeting fixes tomorrow's breakfast (OP-CLEAR); Kadamba decides the day before (OP-NOISY). Procurement is mixed: Yuktahar groceries fortnightly, vegetables 2-3x/week, milk daily (OP-CLEAR + REC stock register); Kadamba meat, eggs, milk, paneer T-1, groceries weekly (OP-NOISY). The binding constraint is the cooked first batch (3-4 h cooked-food life, OP-NOISY), not raw procurement; raw surplus is largely recoverable.
- The CFS Chair's statement is still a real statement and should stay as testimony, but the register must add the operator-side account that qualifies it and must not use it as the basis of the tension.
- **Proposed change:** keep the tension (the institution has run both models) but restate its first side as "a registration count fixed four days ahead that each operator can plan against", and add the operators' own challenge: the Kadamba team questioned why registration closes four days ahead when perishables are ordered one day ahead (OP-NOISY). Reword "weekly stock assessment" to "the CFS Chair describes weekly stock assessment; operators describe fortnightly to daily procurement by item class".

### G-05 (P0, contradicts_existing; carried from sibling deliverable) Missing assumption row: "Composting absorbs the physical surplus"

## 8. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)
1. **Information-flow leverage (Meadows #6):** the gap is already measured twice (CDS scans, operator registers) and routed nowhere. The highest-evidence intervention point is a route from existing records to the rule owner and back to operators, not new measurement. Design principle: "Route the data that already exists before collecting more."
2. **Parameter-level quick win with a known baseline:** replace fixed 70/80% first-batch ratios with weekday-specific ratios from prior weeks' records (Kadamba Sun 27% vs Tue 42%). Low leverage but fast and measurable; Yuktahar already shows the method (weekday register + Wastage Book).
3. **Rule-level leverage:** the standing weekly booking plus billing at registration is the structure producing flat registrations; the four-day close is challenged by operators themselves. Intervention: shorter close or daily opt-in for breakfast only (lunch works, so scope to breakfast).
4. **Timing intervention at the late crest:** 40% of scans in the last 30 minutes; staged/late second batch, grab-and-go counter at 9:15-9:30. Constraint: cooked food lives 3-4 h; off-site kitchens have a 30-min lag.
5. **Role/authority leverage:** give operators (data-rich, authority-poor) a formal channel into menu and registration-rule decisions; the menu-item waste list (uttapam, set dosa, upma) is ready input.
6. **Contract/incentive leverage is still the biggest unknown:** whether plates are paid as registered or as served determines whether operators gain or lose from any attendance-based model. Phase 3 must treat this as a gating assumption, not a design choice.
7. **Mental-model intervention:** operators' beliefs (50%, 90-95%) are wrong against their own data. Feedback displays that show operators their own turnout vs ratio are a cheap, high-evidence move. Yuktahar's August-to-December change shows buy-in, not software, drives change (Zoho dropped). Principle: "Build on operators' own records and routines; no imposed software."
