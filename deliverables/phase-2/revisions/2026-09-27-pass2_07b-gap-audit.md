## 0. Headline verdict

The pass-1 revision made 07b the authoritative loop scheme and it is structurally sound: the D-40 closure discipline holds and none of the three closed loops (L4, L11, L12) is broken by Brief #2. The problems are of three kinds.

1. **Corrected numbers and terms still in the text (P0).** L9 still says "20 to 30 cooking and security staff"; L9 and L14 still use "30 to 40 extra portions for walk-in and paying diners" (and L14 uses it as a crest hedge, which Brief #2 says it is not: it is food for outside people); L1, L13 and Structural Causes still describe registration as "a standing weekly booking" / "taken in weekly blocks" rather than the confirmed automatic, mandatory default with an exit of 5 cancellations per meal type per month at least 4 days ahead and no way to nullify the charge; the Overview still says every Kadamba statement comes from noisy recordings.

## 3. Phase 3 implications

1. **Two kitchen correction paths, two different leverage points.** Kadamba's supervisor-reactive top-up and Yuktāhār's matching-week learning plus first-half-hour projection are the project's cleanest natural comparison. First-batch calibration (LP-E) should be designed as "install L11 at Kadamba", using gram standards Kadamba already keeps, rather than as a new ratio.
2. **The projection must be crest-aware.** A first-half-hour sample underestimates a morning where 26.5% of diners arrive in the last 15 minutes (illustratively about 40% short on the Kadamba curve). Any in-service forecasting intervention should use a weekday arrival-shape multiplier from the CDS scans, not a flat extrapolation.
3. **Top-up cannot rescue the close.** With 10 to 15 minutes of cook time against a final 10 to 15 minute surge, the only in-service fixes are a pre-positioned raw reserve timed for about 9:10 or spreading the crest (arrival-side levers), not faster top-up.
4. **Absorbers have beneficiaries.** 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff eat from the surplus. Any lever that cuts first-batch surplus must plan their meals explicitly (D-45/D-49, design principle P5), or it will be resisted and could harm people it never counted.
5. **Blame-free measurement is a design precondition.** Any intervention that introduces new measurement (waste weighing at Kadamba, routing operator books to the rule owner under LP-A) must copy Yuktāhār's blame-free framing, peer cross-checks and daily discussion, or the measurement link will be gamed.
6. **Operator culture and incentive are a leverage point.** The same arrangement yields "cook to the count because we are paid for it" at Kadamba and "waste is lost profit" at Yuktāhār. Pending the plate basis (D-54 hold), a lever that lets the vendor keep part of the saving from lower waste may point the right way at Kadamba.
7. **Quality lever via L18.** A smaller first batch with more frequent, smaller top-ups would cut held-and-reheated food, a quality gain as well as a waste one. L18's closure (quality → skipping) is untested, so this is a hypothesis to test in a pilot, not a claim.
8. **Oversight covers taste and safety, not quantity.** Existing channels (facility tasting with Prism staff, weekly QC, daily TDS) are working routes into the kitchen; adding a quantity check (first batch vs registered vs yesterday's turnout) to that existing routine is a low-cost intervention point.
9. **Student-side delay.** The T-4 cancellation lead with no nullify option is the rule that decouples intent from the bill; shortening it or adding a same-day "not eating" signal that the operator actually cooks against (resolving the Skip Meal conflict first) remain the upstream levers.
10. **Paper records limit routing.** Yuktāhār dropped a paid ERP; any data-routing intervention must work from paper books plus office digitisation, or use the CDS scan export the institution already holds.
