## 0. Headline verdict

Pass 1 rebuilt this document around EB1. It now carries the kitchen layer, the measured-but-unrouted framing and the D-51 loop scheme. But it was written before EB2, and it still reads EB1's Kadamba numbers as noisy-audio claims. Against EB2 it has four kinds of problem:

1. **Numbers and people EB2 corrects or withdraws (P0).** "Twenty to thirty Kadamba cooking and security staff" appears three times (lines 237, 350, 372). It must become 30 to 50 outside diners plus about half of Kadamba's staff. "Kadamba records waste too, though the quoted weights are unclear" (line 238) points at the withdrawn kg figures and omits the 5-10% figure. "Thirty to forty extra portions for walk in and paying diners" (lines 154, 388) needs reconciling with EB2's "about 30 extra portions for 30 to 50 outside people". The Overview's confidence note (line 12) says every Kadamba operator statement is noisy Telugu audio, which EB2 explicitly upgrades.
2. **Mechanisms EB2 contradicts (P0).** The document treats every kitchen as converting registrations "by its own private rule" or ratio, and lists "a 50 percent belief at Yuktāhār" as Yuktāhār's ratio (lines 16, 271, 394). EB2 §4 and Phase 1 Key Finding 3 (revised) say there are **two forecasting logics**. Kadamba uses a fixed 70% ratio with reactive top-up and no learning record. Yuktāhār has no fixed ratio: it uses a matching-week lookup, month turnout, events, and a first-half-hour projection. L11's claim that "the turnout percentage is recorded but does not drive the ratio" (lines 243, 284) is also contradicted by EB2 §2.3, steps 2 and 3. Registration is still called "a standing weekly booking" in "weekly blocks" (lines 40, 47, 263, 297, 322, 344, 394). EB2 §1.7 and §3 describe it as **automatic and mandatory**, with an exit of 5 cancellations per meal type per month made at least 4 days ahead, and no way to nullify the charge.
3. **Conflicts that must be flagged, not resolved (P0/P1).** Skip Meal is said to reach the kitchen (lines 51, 300, 326), but Kadamba cooks on registrations regardless. Raju (named as CDS head) and Giri (named as CDS Chair) must not be merged (line 370, D-24). The team's "some sort of registration done for outside" diners puts the claim that they are "not counted anywhere" (line 372) in doubt. The Yuktāhār "about 50 percent" belief (EB1) now reads as "max 50%" in EB2, which may be a ceiling rather than an average.
4. **Iceberg content EB2 can now supply (P1).** New events: the day waste weighing began and staff feared blame; back-door entry after 9:30. New structures: auto-registration, gram standards with floating items, veg and non-veg planned separately, the food-safety sequence that fixes the first batch before any turnout signal, extra diners as a sink, the flat blame-free organisation, and the book system with its two-book rule. New mental models: Kadamba's "responsibility, we are paid for it", Yuktāhār's "stay close to the data" and "waste is profit", and the student's "no opportunity to nullify it". New chains: surplus leads to reheating, which leads to degraded quality (an open chain, D-40); and the same billing basis produces opposite operator stances. The analysis also never states the **5-10% waste vs 70% / 37% tension**, which the brief requires to be stated explicitly and not resolved by assumption.

The root causes (D-50) survive the Kadamba vs Yuktāhār re-test, with one refinement. The intermediate kitchen mechanism is not "an uncalibrated ratio in every kitchen". It is "a fixed ratio where the operator has no learning record and treats registrations as the obligation (Kadamba), against a learning system that still starts from an unrouted booking and misses the closing crest (Yuktāhār)". Both halls show the same breakfast gap: 37% at Kadamba and about 28% at Yuktāhār. That is the strongest evidence that the root causes sit upstream of the kitchen: two opposite kitchen philosophies, one unchanged gap.

## 2. Phase 3 implications

1. **The natural experiment strengthens LP-A and LP-B.** Two opposite kitchen philosophies under one booking rule produce the same breakfast gap (37% vs about 28%). Kitchen-side levers (LP-E calibration) reduce waste and run-outs; only booking and routing levers can move turnout. Rank accordingly.
2. **LP-B (T-1 confirm/skip) must be framed against auto-registration.** The current rule is an automatic default with an exit of 5 per meal type per month, 4 days ahead, and no nullify. The intervention is an opt-in, or a cheaper exit from a default, not a tweak to "weekly blocks". Sunday pilots remain the right test.
3. **The D-45 absorber constraint is now sized.** Any first-batch calibration (LP-E) must plan explicitly for the 30 to 50 outside diners (about 30 portions) and about half of Kadamba's staff, who eat from the same food. Cutting surplus without provisioning them makes real people worse off.
4. **Blame-free measurement is a design principle,** not a detail. Yuktāhār's weighing worked only after management made it blame-free and discussed it daily. Any intervention that adds measurement or routes kitchen data upward (LP-A) must protect operator staff from blame, or data quality will collapse.
5. **Operator culture and incentives are a lever.** The same billing basis gives Kadamba an obligation stance and Yuktāhār a profit stance. The payment-basis levers stay held back (D-54), but vendor management practice (flat, book-based, profit-linked waste goals) can be transferred or specified in tenders as a candidate lever.
6. **The first-half-hour projection misses the crest.** An intervention that feeds the April arrival curve (or live scans) into top-up timing would fix the fast correction's blind spot. The Kadamba operator already believes in the last-10-minute peak, so adoption resistance should be low.
7. **Oversight covers safety and taste, not quantity.** A quantity or waste check could be added to an existing, working routine (weekly QC, facility tasting) rather than a new body. Low-cost intervention point.
8. **The food-safety sequence constrains any staged-cooking intervention.** Each batch must pass temperature, sample, display and tasting checks, so staged cooking needs a lighter check for top-up batches, or it will not fit the 7:30 start.
9. **Reheating and quality is a candidate secondary benefit** of first-batch calibration: less surplus means less held food and better quality. Do not claim it until the reheat chain is observed.
10. **Resolve the 5-10% tension before sizing any waste-reduction benefit** for Kadamba. Without a base, no before and after comparison is possible.
11. **The Skip Meal conflict decides whether Skip Meal is a usable lever.** If Kadamba ignores it, making Skip Meal refundable changes billing but not cooking at Kadamba.
12. **The ERP lesson:** Yuktāhār dropped an ERP because its books already hold the data and staff are not digital. Phase 3 interventions should build on paper books and existing records, not on new software for operators.
