Each is P0. More importantly the map is missing a whole subsystem: the **operator layer**, three named operators each converting registrations into a cooked first batch with a private ratio, one of which (Yuktahar) runs a documented balancing loop. The operator is the data-rich, authority-poor actor, and the map currently draws the kitchen as a box that receives a four-day-old number.

### G6-01 · "Actors, Process..." para 4 · Four-day lock described as a procurement/ordering lock
- **Current text:** "Registration happens monthly, four days ahead of time ... That four day lock feeds the vendor's ordering, so nothing about a specific morning can change what gets bought."
- **New evidence:**
  - Registration closes about 4 days ahead in weekly blocks ("weekly compulsory") [RS K 12:05-12:25, K 13:14-14:36]; single-sourced (operator, noisy audio). Phase 1 revised to "weekly blocks" (G-03 to G-08).

  - Quantities are set the day before by the operator (Yuktahar twice-daily meeting [RS 00:11-01:15]; Kadamba "decided the day before" [RS K 00:00-00:26]).
  - EB §3: "the T-4 lock applies to *registration* ... the binding constraint on waste is cooked food prepared at a fixed ratio to registrations, not raw procurement."
- **Proposed change:** Rewrite as: registration closes about four days ahead in weekly blocks and is a standing booking; operators read the count from the portal and set tomorrow's issue quantity the day before; raw procurement runs on its own cadences (fortnightly/2-3x week/daily/T-1); the binding constraint is the cooked first batch, which can be topped up but not shrunk. Also resolve "monthly" vs "weekly" (see G6-30). State the revision explicitly in the text, per D-32 (revise the framing, do not footnote it).
- **Priority:** P0

## G. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

**Leverage points surfaced (Meadows ordering, weakest to strongest)**
- *Parameters:* per-mess, per-weekday first-batch ratio calibrated to recorded turnout (Kadamba 70% vs 37%; Sunday 27%). Cheap and fast, but weak alone because the late crest and top-up lag remain.
- *Delays:* shorten top-up lag (off-site 30 min + transport); align registration close (T-4) with perishable ordering (T-1) that operators already run; feed the events/exam calendar to operators ahead of the meeting.
- *Balancing feedback loops:* spread the Yuktahar L11 practice (matching-week Wastage Book, run-out time rule) to Kadamba and the off-site operator, which currently run open-loop rules of thumb.
- *Information flows (strongest practical lever):* route the gap that is already measured twice (CDS scans + operator registers) to the registration/billing rule owner and back to the operator (e.g., each operator sees its CDS scan-based turnout by weekday; the rule owner sees operator waste). No new measurement needed; the missing element is a route, not a sensor.
- *Rules:* registration granularity (standing weekly booking -> daily or opt-out for breakfast only, since lunch works); billing on scans (L6 precedent); walk-in/late-comer rules around the 9:30 crest.
- *Self-organisation / roles:* give operators a formal voice in menu choice and cut-off rules (data-rich, authority-poor actor); give the committee per-item waste data.
- *Goals / paradigm:* the institution's stated objective ("minimising food wastage through advance meal planning") assumes registration is a forecast; the evidence says it is a standing booking. An intervention that treats registration as a booking and turnout as the forecast target changes the paradigm.

**Intervention points on the map**
1. Portal -> operator: send turnout-by-weekday alongside registrations (information flow).
2. Operator records -> CDS/Mess Committee: periodic gap and per-item waste report (missing link G6-13).
3. First-batch sizing rule per mess (parameter) plus a documented top-up protocol keyed to the arrival curve (e.g., top-up trigger at 9:00-9:10 before the 9:15 crest).
4. Closing crest: service-design options (staggered close, second counter trigger, pre-9:15 signal) to flatten or anticipate the last 15 minutes.
5. Registration rule for breakfast only (weekly standing booking vs daily confirmation / opt-out).
6. Events calendar feed into the operators' planning meeting.
7. Waste exit: standard waste measurement at all messes and a disposal route (compost) the institution can see.
8. Menu feedback: route per-item waste (uttapam, set dosa, upma) to the menu owner.

**Design principles suggested by the revised map**
- Calibrate to turnout, not registration; treat registration as a booking signal.
- Route existing data before building new measurement ("remain close to the data"; Zoho was dropped, so prefer low-tech register-compatible flows over software).
- Design for upward flexibility (fast top-up) rather than precise forecasting, because the batch cannot be shrunk once cooked.
- Give authority where the information already is (operator voice on cut-off, menu, ratios) and information where the authority is (rule owners see the gap).
- One principle, three kitchens: interventions must work for a documented-loop operator, a rule-of-thumb operator, and an off-site operator with transport lag.
- Breakfast-specific: do not disturb lunch/dinner, which already work.
- Non-punitive monitoring (Yuktahar "not to find fault" buy-in) so operators do not hide waste.
- Respect legitimate non-attendance (D-33): the target is calibration, not 100% turnout.
- Make the surplus visible before it is absorbed (reuse, staff meals, bin are locally rational sinks).
