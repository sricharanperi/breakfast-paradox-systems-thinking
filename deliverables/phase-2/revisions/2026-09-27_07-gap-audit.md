## 0. Headline verdict

The deliverable was built when the project had no kitchen-side evidence and no attendance records. Its diagnosis is therefore almost entirely about the **student and governance layers** (billing, cancellation, resale, escalation). The new evidence adds a whole **kitchen conversion layer** the analysis does not contain: each operator converts a flat standing booking into a first batch using a private ratio above real turnout, tops up upward only, cannot shrink a cooked batch, and absorbs the surplus through reuse, staff meals and the bin. The gap is measured locally twice (CDS scans, operator registers) but routed to no one who owns registration rules.

Five claims are now **contradicted** (P0): the "Kadamba served 51-79% / Bakul 11-31%" event (it is registration fill from one portal snapshot); the four-day **order/procurement** lock; the "weekly stock check cannot catch a same-day swing / no way to react" structure and Chain 2 root cause; composting as a confirmed absorber and loop (L9) plus the claim that waste "never shows up as a number"; and the claim that the CFS Chair holds "the single most precise piece of information" in the system. The Chain 4 explanation for reversion (vendor's four-day lead time) loses its main premise and must be downgraded.

The framework also asks for **reinforcing and balancing** loops; section 3 does not label polarity and has almost no balancing loops. The new evidence supplies three well-evidenced balancing loops (in-meal top-up, Yuktahar's inter-week Wastage Book calibration, Yuktahar's staff waste-reduction routine) and one candidate "fix that fails" (ratio raised 70% to 80% at the off-site operator).

## 5. Implications for Phase 3 (leverage points, intervention points, design principles)

1. **Highest-leverage point is an information flow, not a new rule (Meadows #6).** The gap is already measured by CDS (scans) and by operators (registers). An intervention that routes existing measurements to the registration/billing rule owner, and routes CDS scan-based weekday turnout back to each kitchen, costs little and needs no new data collection.
2. **Calibrate the conversion layer.** Each kitchen's first-batch ratio (70%, 70-80%, "50%") sits above recorded turnout (37%, ~28%). A weekday-and-item-specific ratio fed from records is an intervention point inside the operator's existing authority, and Yuktāhār's own Wastage Book shows the practice is feasible and staff-accepted.
3. **Design for the late crest, not the average.** 26.5% of scans in the last 15 minutes, top-up lag 10-30 min, 3-4 h cooked life. Interventions: staged top-up timed to the crest, smaller first batch plus pre-staged raw material, or smoothing arrivals. The 8:30 class is not the main driver (Sunday is latest), so timetable changes are low leverage.
4. **Separate the two halves of the problem.** The no-show half (registration as a standing booking, RC1) and the cooked-surplus half (uncalibrated conversion, unrouted measurement) need different interventions and different owners.
5. **The T-4 registration close is a rule-level leverage point with a weaker defender than assumed.** The kitchen plans T-1 and questioned it; the constraint to test is administrative, not procurement.
6. **Preserve the two-week menu cycle.** It is the kitchen's calibration unit; any menu intervention should keep matching-day comparability.
7. **Candidate design principles:** (a) route measurement to whoever holds the lever; (b) the person who measures should be able to act, or be connected to who can (Yuktāhār's change worked because measurer and decider were the same); (c) make registration carry daily information, not a weekly default; (d) calibrate on records, not memory (50% vs 28%); (e) build on existing operator practice (paper registers, twice-daily meeting) rather than imposing software ("prediction will be based on our data only"; Zoho dropped); (f) respect legitimate variation (Sunday, exams) and do not treat all no-shows as dysfunction; (g) keep the absorption routes (reuse, staff meals) but make them visible as numbers.
8. **Governance intervention point:** a formal events/exams/holiday feed to the kitchen ("We don't come to know until we sit in the meeting") is cheap and removes a known blind spot.
9. **Menu intervention point:** a route from operator item-waste lists (uttapam, set dosa, upma) to the menu owner, with the sambar case as a warning that cuts made without explanation read as price-for-less to students.
10. **Evidence limits to carry into Phase 3:** records exist for Kadamba and Yuktāhār breakfast only; Bakul/Palash have none; noisy-audio numbers must be verified before any intervention is sized on them.
