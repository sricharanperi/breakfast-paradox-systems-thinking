## Reading

The single most consequential gap is the **attendance-gap-to-policy-design** feedback path: the data exists
(E4), the office holding it can articulate the problem precisely, and yet no evidence in this project's
research shows that knowledge translating into a registration- or billing-policy conversation. This is not
asserted as proven organizational failure — it may simply be outside this evidence set's visibility — but it
is the gap most directly implicated in RC3 (fragmented decision rights) and is flagged as the top P0
validation priority in `08_validation_gaps.md`.

Two **fully closed** feedback loops are now confirmed in this system: menu complaints → Mess Committee →
rotation adjustment (B3), and Skip Meal declarations → same-week kitchen prep-quantity forecasting (B1's
B→C link). This matters for the overall diagnosis: it shows the institution is *capable* of functioning
feedback loops in general — the registration-attendance gap is not persisting because feedback mechanisms
are impossible here, but because this *specific* feedback path (aggregate gap → policy design) has been
confirmed to have no channel or owner at all, while a third, even more direct mechanism — the shock-adaptive
model (B4) — shows the institution can run full attendance-based billing when it chooses to, just not as a
standing policy.
