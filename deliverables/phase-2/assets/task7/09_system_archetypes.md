# Full Re-Test of All 11 Standard System Archetypes — Breakfast Paradox (v2)

**Purpose of this pass:** the existing archetype work (`deliverables/phase-2/assets/task7/09_system_archetypes.md`,
folded into `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md`'s "System Archetypes" section) seriously tested only
Shifting the Burden and gave the other ten a shallow, one-paragraph dismissal each — several rejections
read as "no evidence found" without actually mapping the archetype's variables onto this system's actual
data first. This pass re-derives every archetype from scratch, directly against primary sources, before
accepting or rejecting a fit. Evidence tags follow this project's own convention: **CONFIRMED** (directly
stated by a named source or a real logged number), **SINGLE-SOURCED** (one account, not cross-checked),
**CANDIDATE** (a plausible, named mechanism, not independently verified), **ASSUMED** (a systems-thinking
inference, not evidenced). Nothing below is asserted more strongly than its evidence status supports.

**Headline result:** four archetypes are RETAINED in full or strong-partial form beyond the one the
prior pass found (Shifting the Burden) — **Limits to Growth/Success**, **Success to the Successful**,
**Rule Beating**, and **Seeking the Wrong Goal** all have real, well-evidenced structural fits that the
prior pass never actually tested against the right variables. **Growth and Underinvestment** is a
genuine but only partially-evidenced candidate. **Fixes That Fail** is retained as a candidate (same
status the prior pass gave it — re-verified, not upgraded). **Drifting Goals, Tragedy of the Commons,
Escalation, and Accidental Adversaries** are rejected, each for a specific, stated structural reason, not
by default.

## 1. Fixes That Fail

**Required structure:** a quick fix relieves a symptom now, but has a delayed, unintended consequence
that makes the underlying problem *worse* than before the fix.

**Candidate mapping:** the quick fix = the **December 2024 cancellation-cap tightening**. Per the
Power-Interest/Leverage Map and System Timeline (`deliverables/phase-1/03-power-interest-leverage-map.md`,
`05-system-timeline-bot-map.md`): triggered by **Mess Office workload concerns, compounded by a Kadamba
renovation**, the Mess Office tightened cancellation rules (weekly caps, portal migration) with **no
evidenced Committee involvement or student consultation beforehand**. Ping ran a student opinion survey
that returned majority disappointment; Parliament representatives held two meetings with the Mess Office
before the policy took effect; the policy was implemented regardless. The symptom relieved: fewer
cancellation transactions for Mess Office staff to process (workload). The candidate unintended
consequence, per `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md`'s unintended-consequences table: a **tighter cap gives
students who exceed it no legitimate way to correct a registration close to service, so the marginal case
that would have been a clean cancellation instead becomes an uncancelled no-show** — plausibly *increasing*,
not decreasing, exactly the pattern (uncancelled no-shows / ghost registration) a cancellation policy
exists to manage.

**Evidence check:**
- The tightening event itself: **SINGLE-SOURCED**, from Prathyusha's governance research (web/Ping-derived,
  "🟡 search-derived, not full-text-verified" in her own audit), not independently corroborated by a CFS/CDS
  primary source in this evidence set.
- The causal claim (tightening → *more* uncancelled no-shows, not fewer) has **no before/after data** —
  no cancellation-count or no-show-count time series exists anywhere in this project for December 2024.
- The mechanism itself is structurally coherent and consistent with everything else confirmed about this
  system (registration is already cheap-to-hold and costly-to-correct; tightening the one correction channel
  without changing the underlying incentive to over-register would predictably show up as more silent
  no-shows, not fewer) — but coherence is not the same as evidence.
- A second, weaker candidate instance exists in the same window: a **portal migration**, reported in the
  January 2025 "Cleaning up the Mess?" follow-up as having caused its own issues — even more thinly
  evidenced (a title-level mention only, no described mechanism or consequence).

**Verdict: PARTIAL/CANDIDATE.** Same status as the prior pass's assessment — re-verified independently
here, not just carried forward. The archetype's shape fits cleanly (administrative quick fix, plausible
backfire on the exact metric it targeted), but it rests on one secondary account with no primary
corroboration and no quantitative before/after comparison. **What would promote it:** an independent
CFS/CDS or Mess Committee confirmation that the December 2024 tightening happened as described, plus any
cancellation-rate or no-show-rate data from before and after.

## 2. Shifting the Burden

**Required structure:** a symptomatic solution is used in place of a fundamental one, and using the
symptomatic solution *reduces the perceived need* for the fundamental fix — a self-reinforcing substitution,
not just a missed opportunity.

**Instance 1 — Registration/Resale (R1), reinforcing, CONFIRMED.**
- **Fundamental problem:** billing is tied to registration count, not attendance (confirmed structurally
  across every document in this project — the CDS Student Handbook itself states billing is "deducted based
  on actual consumption" in aggregate but registration, not per-meal attendance, is the unit CDS plans and
  sources against).
- **Symptomatic solution:** the informal Mess Cell WhatsApp resale market — a student who registered but
  won't attend posts the slot for sale, recovering some value from an otherwise wasted registration.
  **CONFIRMED** as real and commonly used: Daily eater-1 ("Yes, I have both bought and sold registrations"),
  Skip-1 ("I have seen it a few times"), Skip-2 ("Yes, I have bought and sold my breakfast registration"),
  Skip-3 ("Yes, but rarely... I do register for breakfast for the whole month because Kadamba is hard to
  get... I resell breakfast very rarely, because breakfast registrations can sometimes be available at a
  nice price").
- **Fundamental solution not pursued:** redesigning registration/billing so that registering only when
  intending to attend is the low-friction default (e.g., something closer to loop B4 below, made standard).
- **Side effect that closes the loop:** resale "works... functioning smoothly" for the respondent who
  originated this project's core anecdote (2026-09-03 interview) — because the symptomatic fix recovers
  enough value that the financial pain of a no-show never accumulates into pressure on the institution to
  fix billing. This is the defining feature of the archetype, not just a workaround existing alongside the
  problem.

**Instance 2 — Waste/Composting (R6), reinforcing, ASSUMED mechanism over CONFIRMED structural facts.**
- **Symptomatic solution:** production waste and plate waste are competently composted and food-safety
  sampled under the FSSAI "Eat Right Campus" 5-star programme (confirmed, Kadamba kitchen walkthrough +
  poster).
- **Fundamental solution not pursued:** measuring waste by volume and feeding it back into procurement/
  registration policy — **confirmed absent**, direct user statement 2026-09-13: no waste-volume figure
  exists anywhere in this project, and there is no formal link from waste tracking to policy.
- **Side effect:** a well-functioning disposal channel removes the one visible signal (an accumulating pile
  of unused food) that might otherwise force a demand-planning conversation. The causal claim that
  composting *specifically* is what suppresses this pressure (rather than the pressure simply not existing
  for unrelated reasons) is **ASSUMED**, not confirmed by any interview — but the two structural facts either
  side of it (disposal exists and works; no waste-to-policy link exists) are both confirmed.

**Variant checked — Shifting the Burden to the Intervenor:** this variant requires a recurring external
"helper" whose repeated symptomatic intervention causes another actor's own fundamental capability to
atrophy (the classic case: a manager who keeps calling in a consultant instead of letting the team learn).
No actor in this system plays that role. The Mess Cell market is peer-to-peer among the same population
experiencing the problem (students helping students), not an external intervenor whose repeated help
erodes someone else's capability — there is no "own capability" on the student side that resale is
displacing (a student who resells was never going to develop a different, better coping skill instead).
**Rejected as a distinct sub-finding**, though this does not affect Instances 1 and 2 above.

**Verdict: RETAINED, two independent instances**, one confirmed (R1), one structurally confirmed with an
assumed causal mechanism (R6). The Intervenor variant specifically does not apply.

## 3. Limits to Growth / Limits to Success

**Required structure:** a reinforcing loop drives growth or improvement; that growth runs into a balancing
loop (a real constraint), which slows or reverses it — often surprising whoever expected the growth to
continue.

**This is the clearest archetype the prior pass mis-tested.** The earlier rejection (`09_system_archetypes.md`)
checked this only against aggregate breakfast *turnout percentage* ("a stable 35-40% gap, not a growth
curve") — the wrong variable. The right variable is **Kadamba's registration volume against its physical/
policy capacity**, where real growth-then-limit evidence exists:

- **Reinforcing engine (confirmed):** Kadamba is repeatedly, independently described as the campus "go-to"
  mess — "food was liked by many people" (2026-09-03 interview), "the biggest issue is the mismatch between
  demand and food availability... even though only around 30% of registered students may actually show up"
  (Daily eater-2/3, describing Kadamba specifically), "**Kadamba is hard to get and it gets full**" (Skip-3,
  explaining why they register for the whole month specifically at Kadamba). Reputation and scarcity
  reinforce each other: a well-liked mess attracts more registration, which makes it harder to get, which
  in turn pushes rational students (per Skip-3, directly) to register earlier/more defensively to guarantee
  a slot — a self-reinforcing demand-concentration engine.
- **Balancing constraint (confirmed, historical instance):** "**Bakul's creation**, triggered by Kadamba's
  non-vegetarian capacity reaching its ceiling, resulted in a new mess being established — a confirmed
  structural response to a limit" (`03-power-interest-leverage-map.md`). This is a textbook Limits to Growth
  event: Kadamba's own reinforcing growth in non-veg demand ran directly into a hard capacity ceiling, and
  the balancing response was not "growth continues" but an institutional workaround (build overflow
  capacity elsewhere) — exactly the archetype's predicted shape when a system hits a real limit rather than
  removing it.
- **Balancing constraint (confirmed, currently active):** the institute enforces a **1:3 seats-to-turnout
  ratio** at Kadamba (420 seats against a stated 1,200 turnout figure); the CDS overview poster states
  explicitly that "**unregistered dining is limited... especially near the end of the session, to avoid food
  shortages for registered diners**" — a direct, confirmed institutional acknowledgment that Kadamba's
  popularity is being actively capped/rationed, not left to grow freely.

**Evidence check:** the reinforcing half (reputation → registration → scarcity → more defensive
registration) is confirmed via direct quotes from three independent respondents plus screenshot data
(Kadamba Veg breakfast at 551/700 = 79% of capacity, the highest uptake of any line in the system, against
Bakul's 108/350 = 31% and 37/350 = 11% for its veg/non-veg lines on the same date). The balancing half (a
real, historically-triggered capacity response, plus a currently-enforced ratio and throttling policy) is
confirmed by two independent primary sources (Prathyusha's governance research; the CDS poster + CFS Chair
interview). Both halves of the required structure — a real reinforcing growth engine and a real balancing
constraint it visibly ran into — have direct evidentiary support, which the prior pass's single-variable
check missed entirely.

**Verdict: RETAINED.** Kadamba's demand trajectory is a genuine, evidenced Limits to Growth instance: the
1970s-textbook shape (growth approaches a ceiling; the response is a workaround at the ceiling, not removal
of the ceiling) played out concretely as Bakul's creation, and the same ceiling-and-rationing dynamic is
still actively enforced today (the 1:3 ratio, the end-of-session walk-in throttle). This is a genuinely new
finding this pass, not previously named anywhere in the project under this label.

## 4. Success to the Successful

**Required structure:** two parties compete for a shared, limited resource; the one that starts more
successful is allocated more of the resource (by an allocator, or by a self-reinforcing preference
mechanism), becoming still more successful, while the other, starved of the same resource, falls further
behind.

**Parties:** Kadamba (South Indian, on-site automated kitchen, established) vs. Bakul/Palash (North Indian,
smaller, currently off-site/temporary-vendor kitchen). **Shared, limited resource:** two distinct
resources need to be checked separately, since the archetype requires the *same* resource to be contested —
(a) student registration attention/slots, and (b) institutional capital investment.

**(a) Registration/demand — RETAINED, well evidenced.** This is a genuine, confirmed self-reinforcing
demand-concentration mechanism, not just a static preference gap:
- Kadamba Veg breakfast: 551/700 registered (**79%**). Bakul Veg: 108/350 (**31%**); Bakul Non-Veg: 37/350
  (**11%**). Palash: 146/400 (**36.5%**). — **CONFIRMED**, screenshot data, 2026-09-09.
- The reinforcing mechanism is directly evidenced, not inferred: Skip-3 states they register for the
  **entire month** at Kadamba specifically **because** "Kadamba is hard to get and it gets full" — i.e.,
  Kadamba's existing popularity (success) is the stated *reason* for a student's own hoarding behavior,
  which further concentrates demand there rather than letting any overflow redistribute to Bakul/Palash.
  This is precisely the reinforcing mechanic the archetype requires: success (scarcity) is what drives the
  next unit of demand toward the already-successful party, not away from it.
- This directly corroborates the already-identified B2 "Capacity Redistribution" loop (`06-system-map.md`),
  which the project had previously framed only as "broken redistribution" — reframed here as the demand
  side of Success to the Successful: the two messes are not independent; Kadamba's scarcity actively pulls
  demand toward itself instead of letting it spill over.
- **Confirmed, competing explanation that limits how much of the gap this archetype can claim:** the
  project's own cuisine-preference finding (`01-system-context-brief.md`: "the gap tracks cuisine, not any
  single mess's individual circumstances — it holds consistently across three independently operated messes
  sharing that one variable") is an *exogenous* taste factor, not a resource-allocation feedback dynamic.
  Success to the Successful explains why the gap, once it exists for any reason (cuisine or otherwise),
  self-reinforces via hoarding behavior — it does not claim to be the *origin* of the gap.

**(b) Institutional investment — CANDIDATE only, weaker.** Kadamba has received continuing capital
attention (a renovation, a new commercial dishwasher, on-site automation), while Bakul/Palash have run on a
"temporary" off-site external-vendor kitchen since before this project began, with the permanent Felicity
Kitchen & Dining Facility not expected until **December 2026**. This pattern (the already-winning mess
keeps receiving upgrades; the lagging ones stay on a "temporary" arrangement for years) is consistent with
the archetype's resource-allocation half. **But** the braindump is explicit that "Kadamba underwent
renovation — explicitly **not related to turnout**" (`prathyusha_braindump_2.md` §6) — a direct statement
cutting against the claim that Kadamba's success is *why* it received investment. No source states or
implies that Bakul/Palash's low registration numbers were ever used as a reason to delay the Felicity
Kitchen or deprioritize their facilities.

**Verdict: RETAINED for the demand-concentration mechanism (registration/scarcity), CANDIDATE only for the
institutional-investment mechanism.** State both halves separately in the deliverable rather than as one
undifferentiated claim — the demand-side half is one of the best-evidenced findings in this entire re-test;
the investment-side half is plausible but has a direct piece of counter-evidence (the renovation's stated
non-turnout motive) and no confirmed decision-maker reasoning linking low attendance to under-investment.

## 5. Tragedy of the Commons

**Required structure:** a shared resource with no individual usage limit; each individual gains fully from
their own use while the depletion cost is spread across everyone; usage grows until the resource degrades
for the whole group.

**Candidate mapping considered:** the finite, portioned food supply within the 7:30–9:30 AM window as a
"commons" that late/clustered arrival degrades for latecomers — the item-runout pattern (idli, puri,
bhatura, fruit running out near 9:20–9:30, confirmed by multiple respondents and the CFS Chair's own
minute-level account: ~80% of students arrive around 9:20 AM, entry stops at 9:30).

**Why this does not fit, checked structurally rather than dismissed:**
- The archetype requires an **unregulated** shared resource. This system's access to that resource is
  already gated by registration, a scanned QR, and a capped, separate "Unregistered" walk-in allotment
  (flat 30 slots per mess) that is explicitly **throttled further near session end** specifically to
  protect registered diners — i.e., the institution already runs the exact kind of quota/access-control
  intervention that resolves a commons problem. Any tragedy-of-the-commons dynamic this system might once
  have had is evidenced to already be substantially pre-empted by design, not actively unfolding.
- The item-level runout is better explained by a **different, already-identified mechanism**: uniform,
  average-based portioning applied to items with highly skewed demand (a high-demand item like biryani or
  puri draws far more than an "average" assumption predicts) plus a sharp arrival-time clustering at 9:20,
  not by unrestrained aggregate over-registration depleting a shared pool. Total registered numbers are
  capped by mess capacity by rule; the failure is a *distribution-across-items-and-time* problem, not a
  *total-quantity* commons-overuse problem.
- The direction of the core paradox also runs opposite to the archetype's shape: Tragedy of the Commons
  describes overuse relative to supply; this system's defining feature is **under**-use relative to
  registration (attendance below registration, not consumption exceeding the registered pool).

**Verdict: REJECTED.** The required structural element — an unregulated shared resource being degraded by
individually-rational overuse — has no counterpart here: access is already quota-gated, and the observed
scarcity-at-the-margin (item runouts) is a forecasting/portioning and arrival-clustering problem, not a
commons-overuse problem. This confirms, via a fuller structural check than the prior pass ran, the same
rejection the earlier pass reached by inspection alone.

## 6. Escalation

**Required structure:** two parties each perceive their position relative to the other and ratchet up
competitive responses defensively, in a spiral neither started alone (classically: two firms undercutting
each other's prices, or two nations arms-racing).

**Candidates checked:**
- **Institution vs. students, via the cancellation cap:** does a rule tightening provoke a student
  workaround, which provokes a further institutional tightening, in a repeating cycle? The evidence shows
  **one** tightening event (December 2024) and **one** loosening event (5→10 during the LPG shortage, later
  presumably reverted) — a single adjustment in each direction tied to a specific administrative or supply
  trigger, not an accelerating back-and-forth spiral. No source describes the institution reacting to a
  student countermeasure, or students reacting to a second institutional counter-tightening.
- **Kadamba vs. Bakul/Palash, competing for registration:** the demand concentration described in Section 4
  is a one-directional reinforcing pull (toward Kadamba), not two parties each ratcheting a competitive
  response against the other — Bakul/Palash have no evidenced active countermeasure (a price cut, a
  marketing push, a service change) taken *in response to* Kadamba's pull, and Kadamba does not appear to be
  reacting to Bakul/Palash at all.

**Verdict: REJECTED.** No two actors in this system are shown ratcheting responses against each other; every
candidate resolves to a single one-off adjustment or a one-directional pull, not a mutually escalating
spiral. This is a correct rejection, not a hasty one — checked against both a governance-side and a
demand-side candidate.

## 7. Accidental Adversaries

**Required structure:** two parties who could mutually benefit from cooperating instead undermine each
other, because a well-intentioned action by one unintentionally hurts the other, which retaliates (also
well-intentioned), and the harm compounds — neither party intends the damage.

**Candidate 1 — CFS/CDS's own menu-transparency decision vs. its own procurement-predictability need:**
the CFS Chair self-identified this exact tension (posting the rotation menu in advance may let students
selectively skip on disliked-menu days, undermining the same office's forecasting). **Rejected, as the
prior pass correctly found**: this is one office holding both roles and naming the tension itself — not two
separate parties unknowingly undermining each other. The archetype requires two distinct actors; this is
one actor's own internal trade-off.

**Candidate 2 — students' hedging behavior vs. the Mess Office's cancellation-cap response (tested fresh
this pass, not considered by the prior pass):** could students' well-intentioned over-registration (hedging
against scarcity) hurt CDS's forecasting/workload, prompting a well-intentioned cap tightening (December
2024) that then hurts students (fewer legitimate correction options), in a mutually-worsening spiral? This
is structurally close to Accidental Adversaries, but breaks down on two specific points:
- The December 2024 tightening's **stated cause is Mess Office workload and a Kadamba renovation** — general
  administrative load, not a response *to* students' hedging/hoarding behavior specifically. No source
  frames the tightening as retaliation for, or even attention to, over-registration.
- There is **no evidenced student counter-response** that further hurt CDS after the tightening — the
  student-side reaction on record (a Ping opinion survey, two Parliament meetings) is advocacy/complaint,
  not a harmful countermeasure. A spiral requires each side's response to actually injure the other in turn;
  only one plausible half of that (tightening → possibly more uncancelled no-shows, i.e. Fixes That Fail,
  Section 1) has any support, and that already fits a different archetype's shape more precisely.

**Verdict: REJECTED**, on both candidates — checked seriously, including one candidate (students vs.
CDS via the cancellation cap) the prior pass never considered. What's missing in both cases is the same
required element: two *distinct* parties each independently taking a well-intentioned action that
unintentionally harms the other, with the other's response looping back to cause further harm. Candidate 1
fails on the "two distinct parties" test; Candidate 2 fails on the "harm caused *by* the other's action,
looping back" test — its evidence better supports Fixes That Fail (one party's fix backfiring on itself)
than a two-party adversarial spiral.

## 8. Growth and Underinvestment

**Required structure:** demand for a system grows; the capacity/quality investment needed to serve it well
lags; because it lags, service quality erodes; decision-makers use that *eroded* performance itself as the
signal that demand isn't really there (or isn't worth investing in), justifying continued underinvestment —
a self-fulfilling trap distinct from ordinary underfunding.

**Candidate mapping — Bakul/Palash's kitchen and facilities history, tested directly as instructed:**
- **Confirmed underinvestment pattern:** Bakul and Palash have run on a shared, **temporary, off-site**
  vendor kitchen (not cooking on-site at all) since before this project's research began, with the permanent
  **Felicity Kitchen & Dining Facility not expected until December 2026** — a multi-year "temporary"
  arrangement. Meanwhile Kadamba received a renovation, a new commercial dishwasher, and on-site automation
  in the same period. Bakul is independently described (2026-09-13 session) as "**untidy, with insects/
  spiders near food**" — a facilities-quality signal with no connection to registration or billing mechanics.
- **The already-named candidate loop (B6, `06-system-map.md`), re-examined directly for this archetype:**
  poor physical conditions at a mess → students who have a choice avoid it → lower recorded attendance
  there → attendance data used to justify facilities-investment priority → the low-attendance mess receives
  lower investment priority → conditions stay poor or worsen (closing back to more avoidance). This is
  structurally identical to the archetype's core mechanic: eroded performance (poor facilities) depresses
  the very metric (attendance) that would otherwise justify fixing it.

**What is missing, stated plainly:**
1. **No confirmed "growing demand" precondition for Bakul/Palash specifically.** The archetype requires
   demand that is growing (or would grow) faster than capacity can follow. Bakul/Palash's registration has
   been **persistently low since inception**, not growing-then-suppressed — and the project's own
   best-evidenced explanation for that low uptake is an *exogenous* cuisine preference (North Indian food
   being less preferred on this specific campus), not a demand surge that investment failed to keep pace
   with. Kadamba, by contrast, *did* have the growth-hitting-a-ceiling pattern — but that is Section 3's
   archetype (Limits to Growth), not this one, because Kadamba's response to hitting its ceiling was
   *more* investment (Bakul's creation), not underinvestment.
2. **No confirmed decision-maker reasoning** linking Bakul/Palash's low attendance numbers to a deliberate
   choice to deprioritize their facilities investment. The B6 loop's closing link (low attendance →
   justifies lower investment priority) is explicitly labeled **ASSUMED** in the source document, not stated
   by any CFS/CDS source — no interview confirms that low Bakul/Palash turnout has ever been cited as a
   reason for keeping them on the temporary external-kitchen arrangement this long.

**Verdict: PARTIAL/CANDIDATE.** The facilities-quality half of the pattern (poor conditions, delayed
permanent investment, an independent "untidy" observation) is real and confirmed. The two structural
elements the archetype specifically requires — a genuine growing-demand precondition, and decision-makers
using eroded performance as their investment rationale — are both currently unevidenced (the first is
actively contradicted by the cuisine-preference finding; the second is an assumed inference, not a
confirmed statement from any source). **What would promote it:** a CFS/CDS statement (even informal)
explicitly citing Bakul/Palash's low registration numbers as a reason the Felicity Kitchen wasn't
prioritized sooner, or any evidence that demand for a North Indian option was ever higher than current
facilities could serve.

## 9. Drifting Goals / Eroding Goals

**Required structure:** a persistent gap between an explicit goal/standard and actual performance is closed,
repeatedly, by quietly lowering the goal rather than improving performance toward it.

**Candidates checked, systematically, rather than assumed absent:**
- **The cancellation cap (5 → 10 → presumably back to 5) during the LPG shortage:** this is a **temporary,
  condition-triggered parameter adjustment** (Loop B4, confirmed), reversed once the shock passed — not a
  standard being quietly eroded under chronic pressure and left eroded. The structure explicitly requires
  the lowered goal to *stay* lowered as an accommodation to unmet performance; here the adjustment is
  bidirectional and tied to an external trigger, the opposite of a one-way drift.
- **The December 2024 tightening:** this moved the cap *stricter*, not looser — the wrong direction for
  "eroding a goal to match underperformance." (It is a better fit for Fixes That Fail, Section 1.)
- **The CDS Student Handbook's own stated objective** ("advance registration lets CDS estimate demand
  accurately... reduced food wastage... lower meal prices") is the closest thing to an explicit, named goal
  in this system. **No evidence exists that this objective was ever revised downward** in response to the
  confirmed 35-45% breakfast turnout gap — it still appears, unchanged, on the current (Monsoon 2026)
  poster, worded exactly as an aspirational demand-matching goal, despite the CFS Chair privately knowing
  the gap's exact size.
- **The closest real analog, R4 ("Known-But-Unowned-Gap Reinforcement," `06-system-map.md`):** the CFS
  Chair's awareness of the gap, confirmed to have never reached policy discussion, normalizes the gap as
  "how the system has always worked," reducing the urgency of raising it again. This *looks* adjacent to
  Drifting Goals — a target quietly stops being pursued — but the required mechanism is different: Drifting
  Goals needs an **explicit goal that gets redefined downward**; what's evidenced here is a goal that was
  **never operationalized into an actionable target in the first place** (no number was ever set as "the
  turnout we should be hitting"), so there is nothing on record to have been lowered. Normalization of an
  unaddressed gap and erosion of a previously-set standard are related but structurally distinct dynamics.

**Verdict: REJECTED**, checked against three separate candidates rather than dismissed by default. The
specific missing element: no source shows an explicit, quantified goal or standard that existed at one
level and was later revised downward in response to a persistent shortfall. What exists instead — an
aspirational goal stated once and never operationalized or revisited (R4) — is a different, if related,
failure mode, better described under Seeking the Wrong Goal (Section 11) and the existing R4 finding than
forced into this archetype's specific shape.

## 10. Rule Beating

**Required structure:** people comply with the literal wording of a rule while defeating its underlying
intent.

**The rule and its stated intent:** the CDS Student Handbook and overview poster both state the intent of
meal registration directly: **"advance registration lets CDS estimate demand accurately and prepare the
right quantity of food"** — i.e., the rule exists so that a registration is a truthful signal of intended
attendance, which downstream sourcing and prep decisions can be planned against.

**Confirmed rule-beating behavior, directly quoted, not inferred:**
- **Skip-3:** "I try to catch the registrations. I register for breakfast for the whole month because
  **Kadamba is hard to get and it gets full**... I do register for breakfast for the whole month because
  Kadamba is hard to get and it gets full. But I resell breakfast very rarely, because breakfast
  registrations can sometimes be available at a nice price." This is registration used **to guard scarce
  access to a popular mess regardless of actual attendance intent** — the letter of "register to eat" is
  satisfied, but the intent ("signal true demand so CDS can plan accurately") is defeated, since the
  respondent is registering as a capacity-hedging strategy, not a demand signal, with resale as backup
  monetization if unused.
- **Daily eater-1:** "I register for a month, and if I don't feel like eating, I may sell it. Sometimes I
  don't sell it either" — the same broad-registration-as-default-hedge pattern, population-level (not one
  isolated respondent).
- **Skip-2:** bought and sold registrations "for lunch and dinner, and never for breakfast" — confirms the
  resale market functions as a general-purpose registration-gaming mechanism across meal types, not a
  breakfast-specific anomaly.
- This is **structurally distinct** from Shifting the Burden (Section 2) even though it involves the same
  behavior: Shifting the Burden is about the *institutional* consequence (a symptomatic fix reducing
  pressure for a fundamental one); Rule Beating is about the *individual* mechanism (a student satisfying
  the registration rule's letter while defeating its demand-signaling purpose). Both are legitimately true
  of the same real-world action at once.

**Weaker, unconfirmed extension:** a third-party auth-key API is a real, confirmed portal feature
("These keys allow other applications to log in and manage mess registrations on your behalf" — Settings
page, screenshot-confirmed) that implies a **possible shadow ecosystem of student-built bots/scripts for
auto-registration or auto-cancellation** (`research/primary-research/screenshots/analysis.md`) — this would
be a mechanized, scaled-up form of the same rule-beating behavior, but it is explicitly flagged as
unconfirmed (no respondent mentioned using or knowing of such a tool) and should not be cited as established.

**Verdict: RETAINED.** The core mechanism — registering broadly/defensively at a scarce mess specifically to
guarantee access, satisfying the rule's letter while defeating its stated demand-signaling intent — is
directly evidenced by multiple independent respondent quotes, not inferred. The bot/script extension is a
plausible but unconfirmed amplifier, flagged separately.

## 11. Seeking the Wrong Goal

**Required structure:** the system optimizes for an easily measured proxy instead of the actual desired
outcome, because the proxy is what gets tracked, billed against, or rewarded — and the system faithfully
hits the proxy while missing the real goal entirely.

**The proxy vs. the real goal, named explicitly:**
- **Real goal (institution's own stated words):** "nutritious, hygienic, and affordable meals while ensuring
  efficient operations and **minimizing food wastage through advance meal planning**" (CDS overview poster) —
  i.e., food actually matched to people who actually eat it.
- **Proxy actually used to drive decisions:** **registration count.** Billing is calculated from
  registrations; vendor sourcing is locked to the T-4 registration count; the weekly stock assessment is
  "driven by consumption patterns" at the category level, but the base unit feeding sourcing is still how
  many people registered, not how many will actually attend. Registration is easy to measure (a database
  count, enforceable by a hard deadline) in a way "true intent to attend" is not.
- **Confirmed divergence between the proxy and the real goal:** high-demand items >90% turnout, general
  items ~70%, **breakfast specifically 35-45%** — the CFS Chair can name this gap precisely. The system is
  faithfully optimizing the proxy it actually tracks (registration-based billing and sourcing continue
  exactly as designed) while comprehensively missing the real goal that same office's own poster states
  (minimizing wastage, matching food to real demand) — for breakfast specifically, over 55-65% of the
  planning input is known to be inaccurate, and this has been true long enough for the CFS Chair to be able
  to state the exact figure from memory.
- **Confirmed that this is not merely unnoticed, but *known and left unacted-on*:** "the CFS Chair's
  awareness of the turnout gap has never reached a registration or billing policy discussion" —
  **CONFIRMED**, direct user statement, 2026-09-13. The organization is not failing to see the wrong-goal
  problem; it is continuing to manage the proxy (registration accuracy, billing predictability) while
  privately knowing the proxy has diverged sharply from the real goal it was meant to approximate.
- **A second, independent confirmation the same pattern is structural, not accidental:** the institution has
  already run a *different* model — the shock-adaptive precedent (Loop B4, confirmed) — where billing
  tracked actual walk-in attendance instead of registration, during LPG-shortage and holiday conditions.
  That the institution reverts to the registration-proxy model once the trigger passes (rather than treating
  the attendance-tracked version as generally superior and expanding it) is itself evidence that
  administrative ease of the proxy, not accuracy toward the real goal, is what the standing system is
  actually built to protect.

**How this differs from simply restating Root Cause 1 (billing decoupled from attendance), already named in
`07-systemic-problem-analysis.md`:** RC1 describes the *mechanism* (cost is incurred at registration, not
consumption). Seeking the Wrong Goal is a different, additional lens on the *same* mechanism: it names *why*
that mechanism persists despite being known to fail its own stated purpose — because the thing that's easy
to measure, bill against, and enforce with a hard deadline (registration count) has quietly become the
thing the system actually optimizes, displacing the harder-to-measure real target (true attendance/demand)
that the same policy documents still claim to be pursuing.

**Verdict: RETAINED.** This is arguably the single best-evidenced archetype fit in this entire re-test —
every element (a stated real goal, a named and tracked proxy, a confirmed and quantified divergence between
them, and confirmed institutional awareness of the divergence with no resulting change) has direct primary
evidence, including two independent office-level confirmations (the turnout figures, and the never-reaches-
policy-discussion statement). It was not named under this label in any prior pass of this project.
