# Iceberg Model v2 — The Breakfast Paradox (IIIT Hyderabad)

## Why this replaces the existing Iceberg section

The current `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md` Iceberg section mislabels standing facts about
system *design* — "registration locks four days before serving," "the CFS Chair gave turnout
percentages," "billing is tied to registration not attendance" — as **Events**. Per this course's
own Lecture 4 framing, an Event is a single, concrete thing that *happened* — not a rule, not a
statistic, not a mechanism description. This document rebuilds the Iceberg from scratch, mining
every source for genuine dated/instance-level occurrences, and reserves "Structure" for the rules,
incentives, and designs that a Pattern of such Events reveals.

**Evidence tags** (this project's own established convention, see `MASTER_CONTEXT.md` D-35):
**confirmed** (directly stated by a named source or independently corroborated) · **single-sourced**
(one respondent/account, not cross-checked) · **candidate** (a plausible, named mechanism, not yet
verified) · **assumed** (a confident systems-thinking inference, explicitly labeled as such, never
given the weight of a confirmed claim).

**Sources mined directly:** `MASTER_CONTEXT.md` (session log, esp. 2026-09-06 and 2026-09-13
entries); `2026-09-03_student-self-account-and-mess-system-overview.md`;
`2026-09-06_five-student-interviews-guide1.md`; `prathyusha_braindump.md`;
`prathyusha_braindump_2.md`; `deliverables/phase-1/03-power-interest-leverage-map.md` (historical
precedent section); `06-system-map.md` (background only, per instruction).

## Chain 1 — Ghost Registration & Resale
### (the core registration/billing/attendance gap)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

The institution's mental model — *aggregate registration is an adequate stand-in for real demand,
and administrative simplicity at scale is worth more than per-meal accuracy* — is what keeps billing
wired to registration counts rather than attendance. That structure removes any cost to a student for
registering broadly and skipping (Skip Meal returns no money; a no-show costs the same as a show), so
students form the mirror-image mental model — *a registration, once made, costs nothing extra to
leave unused, so there is no reason to be conservative about registering.* Together these produce the
standing **pattern** of registration volumes far exceeding attendance, concentrated specifically at
breakfast, month after month, with virtually no one using the tool designed to correct it. That
pattern is what a single respondent's own recurring **event** — registering for Kadamba veg breakfast
at ₹48 every single day and not attending most of them — is one instance of.

## Chain 2 — Peak-Hour Run-Outs
### (the kitchen/supply-chain side)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

The kitchen's operating mental model — *portioning to an average consumption assumption applies
fairly across every item, and procurement/administrative simplicity is worth more than matching
production to the true shape of demand* — is embedded in a structure built around a four-day (T‑4)
sourcing lock, a weekly consumption-pattern-driven stock assessment, and a single person managing all
cold and dry inventory on FIFO/FEFO. None of these mechanisms can see, let alone react to, the sharp
demand spike that forms in the last ten minutes of a two-hour window. That structure produces the
standing **pattern** of specific high-turnover items running out before service closes, concentrated
in the same narrow window every day — a pattern multiple respondents each independently report living
through as a specific, dated **event**.

## Chain 3 — Complaint-Ignored-Until-Crisis
### (the governance/feedback side)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

The Mess Office's operating mental model — *operational decisions are ours to make unilaterally;
broad student/committee consultation is optional overhead, especially under time or workload
pressure* — is what a governance structure with fragmented decision rights and no confirmed
committee-sign-off requirement for operational policy changes allows to persist unchecked. That
structure produces a recurring institutional **pattern**: a complaint is raised, goes unaddressed
through ordinary channels, and is only resolved reactively once a further incident or crisis forces
the issue. Two dated, named breakfast-adjacent **events** and one crisis in a different domain at the
same institution all fit this identical shape.

## Chain 4 — The Shelved Fix
### (the shock-adaptive / holiday exception)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

The institution's mental model here is sharper than "nobody has solved this" — it is *procurement
certainty is worth more than demand-matching accuracy as a standing, permanent default, even though
both have been directly demonstrated as achievable.* That belief is what keeps an already-built,
already-working structure — a walk-in, attendance-tracked billing model with a raised cancellation
cap — quarantined to rare, explicitly triggered conditions instead of promoted to the standing
system. The **pattern** this produces is that every time the institution has actually run
demand-matched billing, it has worked and then been deliberately retired the moment the trigger
passed — never evaluated for partial or permanent adoption. Two dated, named **events** are the
concrete instances of that pattern.

| Layer | Content | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mental model — institution** | Procurement certainty is worth more than demand-matching accuracy as a permanent default, not a case-by-case judgment | **assumed** (inferred directly from the fact that a working alternative is retired immediately once its trigger passes, rather than evaluated for wider adoption) | `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md` mental models §; `06-system-map.md` "Shock-Adaptive Precedent" §|
| **Mental model — institution** | An attendance-based billing model is acceptable only as an emergency exception to be reverted, not as a standing risk worth normalizing | **assumed** | same |
| **Structure — resource/temporal** | The outsourced vendor's contract depends on a fixed lead-time guarantee (the T‑4 lock) that a daily walk-in model would remove entirely | **assumed**, but structurally coherent with confirmed billing mechanics | `06-system-map.md` |
| **Structure — rule (tunable parameter)** | The cancellation cap is a parameter the institution has already demonstrated it can move (5/month standing, 10/month during the shortage) | **confirmed** | `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md` Events § |
| **Structure — decision rights (gap)** | No formal mechanism exists to evaluate whether an exceptional policy should become a standing or partial default — fragmentation (Chain 3's structure) is confirmed as the reason a demonstrated success has never been generalized | **confirmed** | `07-systemic-problem-analysis.md` Root cause network |
| **Pattern** | Each time expected-attendance uncertainty spikes (a shortage or a holiday/fest), the institution relaxes registration compulsion and billing tracks real walk-in consumption instead of pre-committed intent — then reverts once the trigger passes | **confirmed** (loop B4, the headline finding of the whole project) | `MASTER_CONTEXT.md` 2026-09-13 systems-boundary-expansion session |
| **Event** | During an LPG/gas-shortage period, the mess reduced to serving one item for the same cost, and the cancellation cap was raised from five to ten per month as a workload/cost-relief measure | **confirmed**, direct user statement | `06-system-map.md` "Shock-Adaptive Precedent" § |
| **Event** | During holidays and long weekends (named example: Felicity fest, when a large share of students leave campus), registration was made non-compulsory — students could walk in and scan a QR, or register one to two days ahead at any mess, with no auto-billing penalty for non-use | **confirmed**, direct user statement | `06-system-map.md` "Shock-Adaptive Precedent" § |

## Chain 5 — Menu Rotation Win, and Its Shadow
### (a working feedback loop, and the second-order risk it created)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

Here the mental model runs the *opposite* direction from Chain 3 — it is a genuine counter-example.
CDS's belief that *menu variety is worth actively soliciting a policy change for* is what let a real
escalation structure (Student Council → Parliament → Committee) actually fire, changing a standing
rule. But sitting underneath that same fix is a second, subtler mental model — *more menu
transparency is straightforwardly good* — a belief the CFS Chair is now visibly revising, because the
same structure that fixed the pattern of menu fatigue created a new pattern: students knowing the
menu in advance and using that knowledge to selectively skip. Both patterns trace to the same
single, dated **event**: the vote that ended the fixed-semester menu.

## Chain 6 — The Invisible Mess
### (student social/cultural side)

**Narrated, deepest first:**

Underneath the numbers sits a set of ordinary, individually reasonable beliefs and habits. A student's
mental model — *sleep is worth more than a registered breakfast the moment the two conflict, and a
meal I'm not physically cued to notice isn't a meal I'm likely to walk to* — is reinforced by a social
and physical structure: breakfast is a low-companionship meal (friends pull attendance toward lunch,
not breakfast) inside a hostel layout that doesn't put the mess in a student's line of sight or
routine the way lunch does. That structure produces the standing **pattern** that late sleep and
schedule overlap dominate the skip decision, with social companionship acting only as a secondary,
non-decisive pull — a pattern two respondents' own **events** each illustrate from opposite sides (one
pulled toward attendance by friends, one entirely unaffected by them).

| Layer | Content | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mental model — student** | Sleep is worth more than a registered breakfast the moment the two conflict ("I prefer sleep over breakfast") | **confirmed** (direct quote) | `2026-09-06_five-student-interviews-guide1.md`, Daily eater‑1 |
| **Mental model — student** | A meal skipped once becomes easy to keep skipping once cooking/eating it stopped feeling worth the time — "felt like a time waste in the morning" | **confirmed** (direct quote, one respondent) | `2026-09-03_student-self-account-and-mess-system-overview.md` §7 |
| **Mental model — institution (implicit, in the registration design)** | Breakfast is a mandatory first meal that every student defaults into unless they actively opt out (the automatic vegetarian allocation for non-registrants) | **confirmed** as a system design choice; **assumed** that it reflects an unexamined universal-breakfast belief rather than deliberate cultural inclusivity | `prathyusha_braindump_2.md` §11.2; `06-system-map.md` "Social, Cultural and Health Dimensions" § |

## What this document deliberately does NOT do

- It does not call any rule, statistic, mechanism description, or standing system feature an "Event" —
  every Event row above is a specific thing that happened, on or around an identifiable occasion, to
  identifiable people or a specific measured instance.
- It does not promote a single-sourced account to "confirmed" status merely because it is vivid or
  well-documented (see the November/December 2024 incidents, and the Fixes-That-Fail candidate in
  Chain 4 territory — held at single-sourced/candidate deliberately, per this project's own D-29).
- It does not present an "assumed" mental model with the same confidence as a directly quoted one —
  every mental-model row states plainly whether it is confirmed-by-quote, confirmed-by-repeated-
  institutional-action, or assumed-by-systems-thinking-inference.
