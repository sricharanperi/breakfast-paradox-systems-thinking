## PART 3 — SYSTEMIC TENSIONS

Each tension below persists *given the current structure* — none is resolvable by simply asking one side to behave differently, because each side's behavior is the individually rational response to the structure it operates inside.

### T1 — Individual rationality vs. system-level cost

- **In tension:** a student's rational self-interest (register broadly to keep options open; resell rather than eat what you won't use) vs. the system's aggregate efficiency (minimize waste, crowding, and run-outs).
- **Held by:** students (individual side) vs. the institution/system-as-a-whole (aggregate side) — no single institutional actor personally bears this tension; it is diffuse.
- **Structure creating it:** Root Cause 1 (decoupled billing) removes any individual cost from inaccurate registration, so individually sensible choices sum to system-level waste without any individual behaving irrationally.
- **Current coping mechanism:** the Mess Cell resale market (recovers value for the individual) and composting (removes the physical waste) — both genuinely functional, both Shifting-the-Burden absorptions that quietly prevent the aggregate cost from ever becoming visible enough to force a structural fix.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** diffusely — the institution overprovisions, the environment absorbs unmeasured waste, and no single actor experiences a sharp enough loss to be the one who complains.
- **Evidence this is structural, not a one-off:** confirmed, repeatedly, across independent respondents with different attendance patterns (a near-daily eater, a zero-attendance-this-semester respondent, and a declining-attendance respondent all describe the identical "register anyway" logic) — **OBSERVED — confirmed.**

### T2 — Procurement certainty vs. demand-matching accuracy

- **In tension:** the vendor/CDS's need for a stable, lead-time-bound forecast (procurement certainty) vs. the system's need for food production to actually track true attendance (demand-matching accuracy).
- **Held by:** CDS/the outsourced vendor (procurement-certainty side) vs. students and system-level efficiency (demand-matching side).
- **Structure creating it:** the T-4 sourcing lock and the vendor's lead-time-bound contract terms on one side; daily attendance volatility (35–45% breakfast turnout, swinging by respondent and by day) on the other.
- **Current coping mechanism:** the shock-adaptive exception (§1.5) — run only during exceptional, short, rare conditions, specifically *because* the vendor can absorb one-off uncertainty it could not absorb as a daily standing condition.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** under the standing model, students absorb the cost (paying for food they don't eat); under the exception, the vendor briefly absorbs demand unpredictability, which is exactly why the exception is kept rare rather than made standard.
- **Evidence this is a real, structural, persistent tension rather than a one-off:** **both sides of the trade-off have actually been operated by the same institution** — the standing model and the shock-adaptive model each demonstrably work on their own terms, and the institution has chosen the standing model as the default every time the exceptional trigger passes. This is the single most concrete evidence in the whole project that a tension, not merely an oversight, is at work. **OBSERVED — confirmed.**

### T3 — Administrative/operational simplicity vs. demand-matching granularity

- **In tension:** treating "the mess" as one uniform system with one registration rule and one average-based portioning method (simplicity) vs. the reality that demand varies sharply by item, by dining hall, and by arrival time (granularity).
- **Held by:** CDS/Mess Office (favors uniform rules, one label, one billing mechanism) vs. the lived experience of students at each of three structurally different kitchen systems (Kadamba automated, Yuktahar manual, Bakul/Palash externally-sourced).
- **Structure creating it:** a single registration/billing/portioning framework applied across four dining halls with different capacities, cuisines, kitchen types, and (per the interview) different per-item demand skew.
- **Current coping mechanism:** none structural — only ad hoc, informal fixes (staff "promised to bring more" of a run-out item before close, but per direct respondent testimony "don't tend to return" it). This is a coping mechanism that fails on its own terms, repeatedly.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** students who arrive after the roughly-9:15 crowding point (idli, puri, bhatura, and fruit are the specifically named items that run out); and, at the mess level, Bakul and other lower-registration halls, which per the confirmed investment-follows-attendance-data pattern receive lower facilities priority precisely because their attendance is already artificially suppressed by other factors (a compounding, self-reinforcing effect — **HYPOTHESIZED — assumed** for the investment-priority link specifically).
- **Evidence:** **OBSERVED — confirmed** for the run-out pattern itself (multiple independent respondents, specific named items, specific time window); **HYPOTHESIZED — candidate** for average-based portioning as the mechanism.

### T4 — Transparency/predictability vs. gaming/selective non-attendance

- **In tension:** giving students advance menu visibility (reduces fatigue, a legitimate and already-successful fix) vs. the same visibility letting students make an informed decision *not* to attend on disliked-menu days (undermining the very demand-forecasting the registration system depends on).
- **Held by:** CDS/CFS (wants satisfied, informed students) vs. the same office's own forecasting accuracy goal, in unacknowledged tension with itself.
- **Structure creating it:** the biweekly rotation policy (§1.4) — a real, already-implemented leverage-point win that has a self-identified downside baked into its own design.
- **Current coping mechanism:** none — this is a self-identified, unaddressed risk, not yet even measured.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** the kitchen/vendor, if the effect is real, since it introduces a *new*, predictable dip in an already-volatile demand curve on top of the existing registration-attendance gap, without any additional signal reaching procurement.
- **Evidence:** **OBSERVED — single-sourced** for the mechanism being named (the CFS Chair raised it unprompted, a strong signal of genuine institutional awareness); **HYPOTHESIZED — candidate** for whether it measurably moves attendance (only the first link — rotation → predictability — is directly confirmed).

### T5 — A one-size-fits-all registration model vs. legitimate cultural/dietary/individual variation

- **In tension:** a single administrative model that treats all non-attendance as the same "paradox" (wasted registration) vs. a genuinely diverse student population for whom skipping or minimizing breakfast is not dysfunction but a legitimate practice (intermittent fasting, regional/cultural norms, religious dietary observance).
- **Held by:** the registration/billing model (implicitly assumes uniform breakfast participation as the default-correct behavior) vs. students whose non-attendance is a considered choice, not an error (one respondent explicitly practices intermittent fasting as a deliberate dietary choice, confirmed directly).
- **Structure creating it:** a uniform CDS-wide registration and billing rule, with only one dining hall (Yuktahar) offering distinct Jain/pure-vegetarian lines — dietary accommodation exists at the *menu* level but not at the *registration/attendance-expectation* level.
- **Current coping mechanism:** individual non-participation (students who don't attend simply don't attend) — which is itself then counted, by the very metrics used to diagnose "the paradox," as part of the problem this project studies, rather than being distinguished from it.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** these students bear a form of institutional misclassification (their legitimate choice is folded into the headline waste/gap number); the project's own diagnostic clarity is diluted, since the true size of the *fixable* share of the gap is smaller than the headline turnout figures alone suggest.
- **Evidence:** **OBSERVED — confirmed** for the intermittent-fasting respondent's own account and for the confirmed dietary-line differentiation at Yuktahar; **HYPOTHESIZED — assumed** for the *aggregate share* of the gap this explains, which no source quantifies.

### T6 — Governance responsiveness to loud complaints vs. quiet aggregate costs (the direct expression of Root Cause 3)

- **In tension:** the CDS escalation structure's genuine capacity to fix a problem, once it is loud enough (menu rotation, §1.4) vs. its structural inability to notice or act on a problem that is quantified but quiet (the registration-billing gap).
- **Held by:** the CDS Committee/escalation pathway (responsive to intensity) vs. the diffuse population of students paying the quiet monthly cost (no individual complaint large enough to trigger the same pathway).
- **Structure creating it:** the confirmed intensity-based feedback triage described directly in Part 2, RC3.
- **Current coping mechanism:** none by design — this tension is not being managed, it is the reason the underlying problem is not being managed.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** students collectively, in recurring monthly overcharges, while no actor in the escalation chain experiences the pressure that would prompt a fix.
- **Evidence:** **OBSERVED — confirmed** — the same intensity-triage design produced two opposite, directly observed outcomes (B3 succeeded; the registration-billing gap has never been escalated despite being precisely quantified by the one office that would need to escalate it).

### T7 — Vendor revenue interest vs. system-level demand-matching efficiency

- **In tension:** the vendor's interest in predictable, registration-guaranteed revenue regardless of attendance vs. the system-level goal (implicit in every waste/efficiency finding in this project) of matching production to true demand.
- **Held by:** the outsourced caterer (revenue-certainty side) vs. the institution's own stated efficiency objective (per the CDS Student Handbook's own framing: "reduced food wastage... lower meal prices").
- **Structure creating it:** registration-based billing (Root Cause 1) happens to also protect vendor revenue from attendance variance, not only student incentive.
- **Current coping mechanism:** none — this tension is not managed or even named in any institutional document read for this project; it surfaces only through inference from confirmed billing mechanics and confirmed outsourcing.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** students and the institution absorb waste and overprovisioning cost so the vendor does not have to absorb attendance-driven revenue risk.
- **Evidence:** **HYPOTHESIZED — assumed.** Included because it is a direct, low-uncertainty logical implication of two confirmed facts (registration-based billing; outsourced, contract-based vendor relationship) even though no source states the vendor's interest explicitly — flagged at its true, lower confidence tier rather than inflated.

### T8 — Academic timetable autonomy vs. the mess system's need for coordinated timing

- **In tension:** Academic administration's legitimate authority to set class schedules for its own defensible reasons (faculty scheduling economy, room contention, cognitive-freshness sequencing — **HYPOTHESIZED — assumed**, but grounded in how comparable institutions timetable) vs. the mess system's need for arrival timing that doesn't collide catastrophically with a hard 9:30 AM cutoff.
- **Held by:** Academic administration (timetable side) vs. CDS/CFS and, ultimately, students caught between the two (mess-timing side).
- **Structure creating it:** **zero formal or informal relationship** between Academic administration and any mess-governance actor — confirmed independently three separate ways in this project's Phase 1 power research, and reconfirmed in Phase 2.
- **Current coping mechanism:** entirely individual — students who have an 8:30 AM class self-manage by skipping breakfast, rushing, or attending unusually early; there is no institutional coordination mechanism at all.
- **Who absorbs the cost:** students with early classes bear a forced trade-off (skip breakfast or risk lateness) that neither institutional side pays any part of.
- **Evidence:** **OBSERVED — confirmed** for the absence of any formal channel and for multiple independent respondents naming the 8:30 AM collision as a direct skip-reason; **HYPOTHESIZED — assumed** for the specific institutional logic behind the 8:30 AM slot itself, since no source from the Academic administration side has been interviewed.
