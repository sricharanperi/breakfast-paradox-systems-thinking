# Task 7 — Supporting Asset: Systemic Tensions

Persistent conflicts where improving one objective makes another harder given the *current* structure —
not one-off disagreements. Only tensions with real evidence are included; the spec's example list is a
prompt, not a checklist to fill mechanically.

## T1 — Administrative Simplicity vs. Demand-Matching Accuracy

- **SIDE A NEED:** Billing/registration stays simple to administer (monthly, registration-based) — RC1.
- **SIDE B NEED:** Kitchen needs an accurate, granular demand signal to plan procurement without waste or
  shortage.
- **STAKEHOLDERS:** Mess Committee/CDS (Side A), kitchen/vendor (Side B), students (bear the cost either way).
- **STRUCTURE CREATING IT:** ST1 (billing decoupled from attendance) directly trades demand-signal accuracy
  for administrative ease.
- **CURRENT COPING MECHANISM:** T-4 aggregate lock (ST3) — plans to the best available number, accepting it
  is a known overstatement (E4).
- **WHO ABSORBS THE TRADE-OFF:** Students (pay for uneaten registered meals) and kitchen (provisioning risk
  either way — over-preparing wastes food, under-preparing causes the shortages Skip-2's transcript hints at).
- **SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE:** A stable, self-reinforcing 35-40% breakfast turnout rate that no single actor is
  positioned to change without redesigning billing itself.
- **EVIDENCE:** ST1, E3, E4, cross-corroborated by S7's independent identification of the same tension.
- **CONFIDENCE:** ✅ CONFIRMED as a structural tension; the specific magnitude of "who absorbs how much" is
  not quantified.

## T2 — Vendor Lead-Time Certainty vs. Registration Responsiveness

- **SIDE A NEED:** Vendor needs a stable lock point to source ingredients reliably (hence T-4).
- **SIDE B NEED:** Students' actual intent to attend can change inside that 4-day window, and the system has
  no way to act on that change even when it's known (via Skip Meal or resale).
- **STAKEHOLDERS:** Vendor (Side A), students and kitchen (Side B).
- **STRUCTURE CREATING IT:** ST3 (T-4 lock, a commercial/contractual constraint).
- **CURRENT COPING MECHANISM:** Skip Meal (designed but not confirmed to close, per loop B1) and informal
  resale (ST9) both exist as partial workarounds operating *around* the lock rather than changing it.
- **WHO ABSORBS THE TRADE-OFF:** Kitchen (stuck planning to stale numbers), students without access to or
  awareness of the resale channel.
- **SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE:** Even a well-designed correction mechanism (Skip Meal) cannot fully resolve the gap,
  because the lock point structurally precedes it.
- **EVIDENCE:** E3, E6, E7, E9.
- **CONFIDENCE:** ✅ CONFIRMED structure; 🟡 PLAUSIBLE that this specific lead-time length (rather than a
  shorter one) is the binding constraint — the exact minimum lead time the vendor actually requires is not
  in evidence.

## T3 — Menu Transparency (Trust) vs. Demand Predictability

- **SIDE A NEED:** Students want visibility into upcoming menus (stated institutional rationale for
  transparency, S5 §8).
- **SIDE B NEED:** Kitchen needs demand spread evenly enough across days to plan reliably.
- **STAKEHOLDERS:** Students (Side A), kitchen/CFS office (Side B) — notably, the *same* office (CFS Chair)
  is simultaneously the champion of transparency and the one who has to plan around its side effect.
- **STRUCTURE CREATING IT:** The advance-posting policy itself.
- **CURRENT COPING MECHANISM:** None yet identified — this tension is the CFS Chair's *stated concern*
  (E8), not a resolved trade-off; it corresponds to candidate loop R3.
- **WHO ABSORBS THE TRADE-OFF:** Undetermined — depends on whether the concentration effect (R3) is real at
  the scale the CFS Chair worries about.
- **SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE:** Hypothesized, not observed — see `03_unintended_consequences.md` UC3.
- **EVIDENCE:** E8 only.
- **CONFIDENCE:** ❓ UNKNOWN-REQUIRES VALIDATION — the tension is real as a *stated concern*; whether it is
  real as an *operating dynamic* is unconfirmed.

## T4 — Fragmented Governance Autonomy vs. Cross-Cutting Problem Ownership

- **SIDE A NEED:** Each governance function (menu, kitchen execution, academic scheduling, billing) benefits
  from focused, independent authority — this is presumably why B3 (menu governance) works so well.
- **SIDE B NEED:** The registration-attendance gap is cross-cutting and has no natural owner under that same
  fragmentation.
- **STAKEHOLDERS:** Mess Committee, Warden, kitchen/vendor, academic office, students.
- **STRUCTURE CREATING IT:** ST6/RC3 (fragmented decision rights).
- **CURRENT COPING MECHANISM:** None — this is precisely the gap RC3 identifies; no compensating mechanism
  is evidenced.
- **WHO ABSORBS THE TRADE-OFF:** Students, who experience the full cross-cutting problem while each
  governance actor sees only their own well-functioning slice (e.g., CFS Chair can point to B3 working).
- **SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE:** A well-governed institution on every measurable sub-dimension can still have a
  persistent, unowned systemic problem.
- **EVIDENCE:** RC3's full evidence base (S1, S5).
- **CONFIDENCE:** ✅ CONFIRMED as a structural condition.
