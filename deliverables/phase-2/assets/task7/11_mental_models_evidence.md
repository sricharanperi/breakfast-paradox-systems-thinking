# Task 7 — Supporting Asset: Mental Models — Full Evidence Basis

Expands MM1-MM3 from `01_iceberg_matrix.md` with the full reasoning chain from evidence to inferred belief,
so the inference is auditable rather than asserted.

## MM1 — "Registering costs me nothing extra if I don't show up"

- **ACTOR:** Individual students (directly evidenced for the S1 respondent only).
- **DIRECT EVIDENCE:** S1 describes registering for Kadamba veg breakfast (~₹48) every day and never availing
  it, framed as an ongoing, unremarkable pattern rather than a one-off mistake or a source of active distress.
- **WHY THIS SUPPORTS A MENTAL MODEL, NOT JUST A BEHAVIOR:** The behavior (registering daily) continues
  despite (a) a known cost already being incurred (₹48/meal) and (b) an available formal alternative
  (cancellation, up to the monthly cap). A one-time oversight would not need repetition to be explained;
  a *stable, repeated* choice not to change behavior despite available options is the signature the spec
  requires ("repeated behavior") for inferring a mental model.
- **WHAT WOULD STRENGTHEN THIS TO CONFIRMED:** A direct quote where the respondent (or another student)
  articulates the belief itself — e.g., something equivalent to "why would I bother cancelling, it doesn't
  change anything for me either way."
- **WHAT WOULD WEAKEN/REJECT IT:** Evidence that the respondent finds this genuinely costly and distressing
  but simply forgets or lacks a viable cancellation window — which would point instead to a friction/UX
  problem (e.g., ST5's portal issues) rather than a belief that non-attendance is costless.
- **STATUS:** 🟡 Potential mental model — requires validation.

## MM2 — "Aggregate registration numbers are close enough to actual demand for procurement purposes"

- **ACTOR:** Mess/vendor governance (institutional level, not a named individual).
- **DIRECT EVIDENCE:** The T-4 procurement lock (ST3) treats registration as the operative demand signal.
  Separately, the CFS Chair can state precisely that breakfast turnout runs 35-40% against registration
  (E4) — i.e., the same institutional apparatus that runs the practice also holds data showing the practice's
  central assumption is significantly off, specifically for breakfast.
- **WHY THIS SUPPORTS A MENTAL MODEL, NOT JUST A PRACTICE:** Per the spec's allowance, an institutional rule
  that persists *despite* contradicting evidence held by the same institution is treated as evidence of an
  operating (if unstated) belief, not merely a neutral administrative default — because a default with no
  belief behind it would be the first thing revisited once contradicting data surfaced internally.
- **WHAT WOULD STRENGTHEN THIS TO CONFIRMED:** A direct statement from CFS/Mess Committee explaining *why*
  the T-4/registration-based approach continues despite the known gap (e.g., "we've looked at this and it's
  still the best available signal because—").
- **RESOLVED, 2026-09-13:** the user directly confirmed the CFS Chair's awareness of the gap (a further
  tentative ~45% figure, alongside the earlier 35-40% range) has never reached a registration or billing
  policy discussion. This rules out "considered trade-off" and confirms "unexamined operating belief."
- **STATUS:** ✅ CONFIRMED — the practice continues despite known contradicting data, with no evidence of it
  ever having been reconsidered. This is now the strongest-evidenced institutional mental model in the set,
  alongside MM3.

## MM3 — "More menu transparency is straightforwardly good" (a belief currently being revised by its own holder)

- **ACTOR:** CFS Chair / Mess Committee (the office that both set the transparency policy and now flags its
  risk).
- **DIRECT EVIDENCE:** E8 — a direct, self-reported statement of concern about the transparency policy's
  possible side effect (selective attendance on posted "good" days).
- **WHY THIS IS THE STRONGEST-EVIDENCED MENTAL MODEL IN THE SET:** Unlike MM1 and MM2, which are *inferred*
  from behavior, MM3 is close to a **direct quote-level admission** — the actor is naming their own prior
  assumption as something they are now questioning. This is the clearest case in the whole evidence set of a
  mental model becoming visible through institutional self-reflection rather than through outside inference.
- **WHAT WOULD STRENGTHEN THIS FURTHER:** Confirmation that a specific menu-transparency decision (e.g., how
  far in advance to post, or whether to post at all) was actually revised as a result of this concern.
- **WHAT WOULD WEAKEN IT:** If the "concern" turns out to be a passing remark not connected to any actual
  hesitation about the policy — i.e., if transparency continues completely unchanged and unexamined despite
  the stated concern, the "currently being revised" framing would need to soften to "named but not yet
  acted on."
- **STATUS:** 🟡 PLAUSIBLE, closest of the original three to institutional confirmation.

## MM4 — "Procurement certainty is worth more than demand-matching accuracy, as a permanent default rather than a case-by-case judgment" — added 2026-09-13

- **ACTOR:** The institution/vendor relationship, collectively (not a named individual).
- **DIRECT EVIDENCE:** The shock-adaptive precedent (loop B4) — the institution has already run a working
  attendance-based billing model during holidays and the LPG shortage, and returns to the decoupled-billing
  standing model as soon as the triggering condition passes, rather than evaluating whether any part of the
  relaxed model could be retained permanently.
- **WHY THIS SUPPORTS A MENTAL MODEL, NOT JUST A PRACTICE:** an institution that had already proven an
  alternative works, and still treats it as an emergency-only measure rather than a standing option worth
  weighing, is behaving as though the trade-off (procurement certainty over demand-matching accuracy) is
  fixed and non-negotiable — not as though it has been actively, recently reconsidered and reaffirmed.
- **WHAT WOULD STRENGTHEN THIS TO CONFIRMED:** A direct statement from CDS/CFS explaining that a permanent or
  partial version of the shock-adaptive model was actually considered and rejected for a specific, named
  reason (e.g., a concrete vendor-contract renegotiation cost).
- **WHAT WOULD WEAKEN/REJECT IT:** Evidence that the vendor contract makes a standing attendance-based model
  genuinely infeasible for a hard, non-negotiable reason (e.g., a binding minimum-order clause) — this would
  replace "unexamined default belief" with "a real, external constraint," which is a different and less
  critical finding.
- **STATUS:** 🔷 ASSUMED — this is the newest and least directly evidenced mental model in the set, but it is
  the one whose resolution would most change any future intervention design, since it decides whether B4
  represents an untapped opportunity or a one-off exception with no generalizable lesson.
