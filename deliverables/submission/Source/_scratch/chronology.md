# Chronology of the work (for the synthesis writer)

Source: the project's session log, decision log, mistakes log, evidence index and rebuild plan. People by role except the three team members. Tooling and AI items left out on purpose.

## 3 to 6 September: Phase 1 from a first interview

The first commit of the project, on 3 September, held the course materials, a first interview with a team member who lives in a hostel and skips breakfast, six screenshots of the dining portal, a first draft of the System Context Brief and the research method notes. The portal screens gave the first real numbers: capacities, rates, the difference between Skip Meal and cancellation, and the random allocation of seats at some halls. On 5 September the Context Brief was rewritten around three questions (what the system is for, what it actually does, and the gap between the two), and the interview guide was rewritten after a trial with two simulated respondents exposed four leading questions. The simulated transcripts were marked as not evidence from the start.

The same day the Stakeholder Map went through two rebuilds. The first framed the halls by vegetarian and non-vegetarian lines, the second by cuisine (South Indian at Kadamba, North Indian at Palash and Bakul, Jain at Yuktāhār); the cuisine framing was later overturned. The Mess Committee and Mess Office were split into separate actors, and the informal layer was found: the student resale group, a student publication and unofficial tool builders. On 6 September the power analysis was built from first principles and audited, and it was scored by the team itself as strong but not yet complete. Two findings were revised on the same day by later answers: a student representative on the committee softened the claim that students are consulted but cannot decide, and a precedent of organised student action aimed at governance turned "sleeping" collective leverage into "exercised but unmeasured" leverage. Independent research also found that the Student Parliament elects a Mess Secretary and a deputy for each mess, a reachable stakeholder that was missing from the map, and showed that a "Mess Council" thought to exist was most likely a name borrowed from a different institution.

Five student interviews were held on 6 September (two daily eaters and three skippers). They gave Activities 4 and 5 their first real evidence, and both were written in two parts: what was established, and a plan for the kitchen side nobody had yet observed. Two independent readings of the same transcripts agreed on the facts. They disagreed on wording, and two corrections were adopted from the better-supported reading: registration is monthly and not weekly, and the reported two peaks of crowding are one crowd that builds until the close. A labelling gap in the source (two respondents labelled as the second daily eater, one transcript) was flagged and the sample counted as four to five distinct people. Four findings from these six voices stayed fixed: Skip Meal was not used by anyone, a friend's absence did not change anyone's decision, staff promises of a restock often did not arrive, and everyone who suggested a fix suggested a later close and not a later start. The Phase 1 consolidated report and the five deliverables were finished that night.

## 12 to 14 September: Phase 2

Phase 2 began on 12 September with a first system map and a first systemic problem analysis, written as a pure synthesis of Phase 1. A second pass the same day went to the course's own frameworks, added a service blueprint, and re-drew the diagrams. On 13 September the facilities head was interviewed, the Kadamba kitchen was walked through, and four posters in the dining halls were photographed and transcribed word for word. That day also corrected the picture of the off-site kitchen (Bakul and Palash share one, until a new dining facility opens at the end of 2026) and gave the first real operating numbers: a breakfast turnout of 35 to 40 per cent, a registration close four days ahead, a menu rotation every fortnight, and an arrival spike near 9:20.

Most of the analytic corrections of the project were made on 13 September. The problem analysis was rebuilt with an evidence discipline, widened by assumption (and the assumptions labelled), then consolidated into one document. The team learned of the gas-shortage precedent, in which registration was optional for a time and the cancellation limit was doubled. It changed the framing of the root causes, from an unowned gap to a proven fix that was deliberately not the default. Then the iceberg reading was found to be wrong. Every earlier pass had labelled standing facts about the system's design ("registration locks four days before serving") as events, while the course's own definition of an event is a single, concrete occurrence. The reading was rebuilt topic by topic from the primary sources, and in doing so five mechanisms that had been drawn as loops were re-tested: three became chains, one was disconfirmed and one was absent. On 14 September the reframe was written, rejected as a consulting-style memo and rewritten as a systems statement: the system is stable and not broken. Activity 8 was split into its two deliverables, and the causal loop analysis was labelled as a deliverable of its own.

## 26 to 28 September: field evidence, three passes and Phases 3 and 4

On 26 September the team photographed 26 pages of Yuktāhār's registers and forms, recorded the site head, the Kadamba manager and the Bakul staff, and received the dining team's export of every Kadamba breakfast registration and scan for April 2026 (32,162 rows). The first records overturned the working figure for the gap. It was not about 30 per cent: it was about 63 per cent at Kadamba and about 72 per cent at Yuktāhār, and 30 per cent fitted lunch. The crowd curve became a measured one, with 26.5 per cent of scans between 9:15 and 9:30. Pass 1 on 27 September revised every Phase 1 and Phase 2 deliverable by audit, revision and independent check, followed by one consistency edit for each phase: one loop scheme (L1 to L17 and L3b), composting removed from the absorbers, the four-day lock recast as a lock on registration with the cooked first batch as the binding constraint, and "the gap is invisible" replaced by "the gap is measured twice and routed nowhere".

The same day a team member's first-hand account of Kadamba and Yuktāhār arrived (the command chain, the seventy per cent rule, gram standards, the food-safety sequence, the book system). It took precedence over the first field summary wherever they conflicted, so pass 2 re-ran the cycle: the kilogram waste figures were withdrawn, registration was described as an automatic default and not a weekly booking, the absorbers were revised to include outside diners and half of Kadamba's staff, and two operator regimes were described. Two open conflicts were flagged and never merged: who heads the dining services body, and whether a Skip Meal declaration reaches Kadamba's kitchen.

Phases 3 and 4 were written on 27 September on the revised evidence: twelve-point leverage map, fourteen intervention points, principles, concepts, a backtested scenario model and a recommendation. On 28 September they were revised against the course's later lectures (a place scale counted from the deepest, an archetype check, two more levers, a seventh principle), and the recommendation was resequenced after the shifting-the-burden pattern showed that fixing the kitchen first could remove the pressure to change the rule. On the same day a team member's synthesis of the Bakul recordings (pass 3) made Bakul a third operator regime, corrected where its kitchen is (Hafizpet, about 8 km away), withdrew a "70 to 80 per cent change" that was really variation by item, and recorded that Bakul reports payment on plates served while Kadamba's remark points to registrations. That conflict kept the payment-dependent levers held.

## Early October: assembling the record

On 6 October the team found that none of the earlier documents or diagrams had been re-rendered since the first week of the project, so every submitted file still showed overturned facts. The revised text was current, and the rebuild redrew every diagram from it. The evidence was frozen for the rebuild, with open questions shown as unresolved, and this documentation was written as one storyline from the rebuilt deliverables.

## Mistakes and corrections worth naming

- A 30 per cent gap, repeated through three documents, was a lunch-sized number (corrected on 27 September).
- The Mess Council was treated as a real body before a check showed it was probably another institution's (6 September).
- Sleeping collective leverage was overturned by a later precedent (6 September).
- The vegetarian and non-vegetarian framing, then the cuisine framing, of low uptake did not survive the portal-snapshot check and the kitchen's own register (27 September).
- Loops were labelled loops before closure was tested (13 September).
- Standing facts were labelled events in the iceberg (13 September).
- The four-day lock was wrongly named the bottleneck; it locks registration, and kitchens set quantities the day before (27 September).
- Composting was listed as an absorber and had to be removed (27 September).
- A transcription gloss ("portal above") hid a real inconsistency on a poster until the image was read again (13 September).
- Two figure standards were repaired in review: the after-close share recomputed from the records (9.2 per cent, not 9.1), and a leverage-place numbering that mixed two directions (28 September).
