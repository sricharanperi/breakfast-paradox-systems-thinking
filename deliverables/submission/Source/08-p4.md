# Phase 4: Designing, Testing and Evaluating Interventions {#ch-phase4}

Phase 4 consists of Activity 10, which develops and assesses interventions that improve outcomes across the system as a whole. The concepts aim at the three confirmed structures that hold the paradox in place and at the step in which registrations become a first batch. No pilot was possible within the course, so every test is on paper: model outputs are labelled projected (arithmetic on real Kadamba records under stated assumptions), stakeholder reactions simulated (reasoned, no feedback collected) and co-design tests planned (none has run).

### Generating Alternative Interventions {#sec-a10-concepts}

Eight concepts were generated. Seven aim at a lever; G is a deliberate foil, kept in every test so that its failure shows in the numbers ([[tab:a10-concepts]], [[fig:a10-concepts]]). "B with C" is the calibrated first batch plus the 9:00 late batch; "the bundle" is the combination recommended at the end.

![The eight concepts mapped to the twelve leverage points. G, the foil, pulls on none.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-3-4-rebuild/Assets/a10-concepts-levers.png){w=100 #a10-concepts}

**A** leaves the charge visible, since kitchen levers shrink surplus but not the charge until a rule moves. A cooked-against-eaten threshold, anchored outside the hall's own past, alerts the escalation forum beside the complaint trigger, and a weekly note of exams and events goes to planning.

**C** projects the day at 9:00 from the arrival card and starts a late batch landing by about 9:15; a grace period after 9:30 covers only late-batch food. It depends on Kadamba's 10 to 15 minute on-site top-up. A Bakul top-up needs a phone call, cooking at Hafizpet and a 20 to 40 minute drive (single-sourced), so its hedge is the roughly 50 portions held at 9:20 (candidate), and C there means sizing that reserve and the latest call that can still land.

**E** brings the items that wasted most (uttapam, set dosa and upma at Kadamba, single-sourced) and those that ran out to each menu cycle. The Bakul manager thinks pairings are the real problem (single-sourced), so operator input is a voice, never authority, read beside consumption data and residents' wishes; E supplies the missing link of L16. **F** is held until each operator's payment basis is confirmed.

**H** asks each belief holder what produces their figure and what would change it: the Yuktāhār site head's "about 50 per cent" against his register at about 28, the Kadamba supervisor's crest prediction, the registration side's reasons for the default. CFS and CDS are asked to adopt the CDS poster's goal (minimising food wastage through advance planning) as two indicators, booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, read beside the quality measures of P7.

Decision: Keep concept G, a blunt cut the team expected to fail, in every test as a foil, because testing only believed ideas risks every test passing.

Decision: Hold concept F (billing at consumption) behind the plate basis: it has the deepest student-side effect, but its effect on the operator side depends on how plates are paid, which the team cannot move.

#### The scenario model

The model explores how the system might respond and makes no predictions. It replays Kadamba's April 2026 records (confirmed: 32,162 rows with scan times), setting what each rule would have cooked against what was eaten, arrival by arrival. Kitchen forecasts use only earlier records, so the backtest covers 23 out-of-sample days (8 to 30 April); student-side scenarios use the whole month.

The inputs are a 70 per cent first batch per line (tested 60 to 80, single-sourced); posted rates of Rs 48 veg and Rs 66 non-veg at Kadamba and Rs 53 at Yuktāhār (confirmed as posted, use for April assumed); a forecast equal to the mean of earlier same weekdays; arrival profiles from earlier same weekdays, with the crest 5 points lighter to 10 heavier in stress runs; a 15-minute top-up (30 in a gas shortage); about 30 outside diners plus 0 to 30 staff; skip uptake of 25 or 50 per cent of no-shows (tested 10 to 75, assumed); opt-in turnout of 60, 72 or 85 per cent; eaters held at records (plus or minus 10 per cent, 25 per cent for exams or a favourite item); and adoption on assumed dates.

Decision: Run the model on Kadamba's records only and size nothing for Bakul, which has no records and a different supply chain, so a Kadamba rule is never applied where it could trim a batch no top-up can replace.

**Scenario 1: the kitchen rules, backtested.** The baseline cooks far ahead of demand, with the veg line at about 525 a day for 237 eaten. The ratio cut fails both ways: 138 of its 150 surplus portions are still veg, and non-veg (turnout up to 59 per cent on busy days) runs short on 7 of 23 days, because one flat ratio cannot serve two lines 16 points apart. A calibrated single batch cuts surplus to 60 but moves the cost to the crest: 16.8 short a day, and on 26 April the veg line would have run out at 9:24, 45 short (L14 in reverse).

### Testing Interventions against System Objectives {#sec-a10-eval}

The objectives belong to the system: notice its own cost, cut the first batch without trading waste for run-outs, leave no one worse off, and be usable. Four gates make a concept ineligible whatever its score ([[tab:a10-gates]]).

Table: The four gates. {#a10-gates cols=22,56,22}
| Gate | Test | Source |
|---|---|---|
| G1 Absorbers kept | Resale, reuse, outside-diner and staff meals, walk-ins, late service and outside venues survive; only the over-cooked batch, reheating and the bin are targeted | P5 |
| G2 Safety kept | Every step of the food-safety sequence stays intact | P4 |
| G3 Aimed at structure | Neither asks students to behave better nor blames operators; not gated on an unknown that could reverse it | P3; LP10 held |
| G4 Quick fix paired with fundamental fix | Any kitchen lever is paired with a route, default, goal or belief lever landing on a date independent of kitchen performance | Shifting the burden; P5 |

Concepts that pass are scored from 0 (worse) through 1 (no effect) to 4 (large gain in the model) against nine criteria, weighted out of 400 by the team's judgement ([[tab:a10-criteria]]).

Table: The nine criteria and their weights. {#a10-criteria cols=22,60,18}
| Criterion | What it asks (result measure) | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| O1 Booked against eaten | Does the booking carry more of the morning's demand? (charges for uneaten breakfasts) | 15 |
| O2 Cooked against eaten | Does the first batch shrink toward eaten? Read only with O3 (first-batch surplus) | 15 |
| O3 Crest service | Does the last diner still eat, at every hall? (shortage per hall and line, with run-out indicator) | 15 |
| O4 Fundamental capability | Does cost reach a rule owner, a learning loop exist, goal and beliefs get tested? (route built; L11 at Kadamba; indicators adopted) | 15 |
| O5 Fairness | Does it avoid shifting cost onto the least powerful? (who bears any new cost) | 10 |
| O6 Food quality | Does it cut holding and reheating and keep portion, taste, texture? (share served fresh or late; holding time; grams) | 10 |
| O9 Desirability | Is it desirable to those who must use it? (simulated response; co-design tests once run) | 10 |
| O7 Feasibility now | Can the student team build or pilot it with an engaged actor? | 5 |
| O8 Reversibility | Can it be piloted small, measured and undone? | 5 |

The weights changed in revision. Food quality rose from 5 to 10 because students eat reheated surplus and worse portions are the quickest way to cut surplus; it now includes holding time and texture after the Bakul manager reported that carried aloo paratha loses its texture (single-sourced). Desirability entered at 10, since a design nobody will use fails as surely as one not needed.

A leads the single concepts because it builds the route every other lever needs. The two bundles have the same parts and score, since a weighted score cannot see sequencing and the gate can: the first draft made the fundamental fix depend on the quick fix performing, a small case of shifting the burden. Scenario 4 shows the wait is unnecessary, so the re-sequenced bundle, which passes G4, is recommended.

Decision: Apply four gates before any score and let the gates overrule the score, because a weighted score cannot see the order in which parts run and two bundles with the same parts scored the same.

Decision: Weight food quality, desirability and the fundamental capability above feasibility, and keep process checks out of the score, so the evaluation does not drift toward whatever a student team can do quickest.

### Deliverable 5: The Final System Design Recommendation {#sec-a10-rec}

#### The recommended intervention

**Route and read the gap together, open the Sunday exit on a fixed date, and calibrate the kitchen to buy time.** The kitchen bundle runs alongside, labelled the quick fix wherever reported. Stage 1 (weeks 0 to 4) builds the ledger with its charge line, runs the co-design tests, holds the joint reading at week 4 and takes the goal and indicators to CFS and CDS, while the cards and sheet are drafted with the Kadamba supervisor. Stage 2 (weeks 5 to 8) proposes the Sunday skip to the rule owner at week 6, through student representatives and with the plate-basis question, while the Kadamba Sunday veg pilot runs the card, allowance, 9:00 batch and sheet and Yuktāhār re-anchors. Stage 3 (weeks 9 to 20) puts the skip in force from about week 10 if adopted, takes the every-day decision at week 14, hands over from week 16 and ends at week 20.

![The recommended sequence, with decision points and handover.|Source: authors' design (planned).](phase-3-4-rebuild/Assets/a10-sequence.png){w=100 #a10-sequence}

Bakul, a later hall outside this pilot, is a third regime with no records, and the Kadamba model does not transfer. The recommendation extends there only once its scan export gives weekday turnout and arrivals, the call-to-counter lead time is measured, an item-level record backs the weekly review, any kitchen lever cuts waste without raising shortage risk, and, for the Sunday exit, its payment basis is confirmed.

Decision: Pilot on Kadamba's veg line on Sundays, size nothing for Bakul until it has records, and plan the team's own withdrawal at week 20: the pilot sits where three levers meet, the Bakul model has no data, and a team that stays becomes the thing the system depends on.

#### Theory of change

The bundle acts on the place-3 goal, shifting the working measure from registration count to cooked-against-eaten, an aim already on the institution's own poster ([[fig:a10-toc]]). Each root cause has its own change and expected response: the unrouted gap is met by the ledger, its charge line and the threshold (cost becoming a trigger beside complaint, candidate); the goal and beliefs by the stated goal with two indicators and the shared readings; the missing learning record at Kadamba by the plan card and daily sheet (surplus from 254 to 471 portions a day to about 69 to 120, projected); the upward-only top-up by the 9:00 late batch (shortage of about 0.5 to 7.7 a day as the crest varies, projected); the booking default by the Sunday skip and the every-day skip on its rule; and billing by the ledger building the case, held until the plate basis is known.
