# Limitations and Ethical Considerations {#ch-limits}

## Limitations of the Evidence {#sec-limits-evidence}

The evidence has five main limits. Six students were interviewed, one transcript had a labelling gap, and six people are not a population. Each kitchen was visited once and the operators describe their own practice. Two sets of records cover two halls in two different months, and Bakul has no records at all. The scenario model uses only Kadamba's scans, and its stress tests apply assumed shocks. The stakeholder responses are simulated from stated positions, no feedback has been collected, and every co-design test is planned and has not been run. Each limit is placed beside the claim it affects in the activity sections.

## Limitations of the Method {#sec-limits-method}

Three features of the method also limit what can be claimed. The loop test is strict by design, which means some real mechanisms were held as candidates for want of one link, and the count of three closed loops describes the evidence and not the system. The scoring weights in Activity 10 are the team's judgement, and two bundles tied because a weighted score cannot see order, which is why the gates overrule the score. The three-hall comparison differs in five ways besides the planning rule, so it shows that a learning practice is possible under today's rules and does not show why it exists at one hall and not another.

## Open Questions {#sec-open}

Open questions remain, and [[app:open]] collects them by who could answer. The most consequential is what a plate means in each operator's monthly payment. Others concern who set the four-day close and the limit of five, whether a Skip Meal declaration reaches the Kadamba kitchen, who heads the dining services body, what Kadamba's waste figure is a percentage of, whether a late batch can be cooked at 9:00 without slowing service, how many students would take a Sunday skip (assumed to be a quarter to a half of no-shows), and which registration rule applied at Yuktāhār in June.

## Ethical Considerations {#sec-ethics-final}

The study involved interviews with students, staff and operators, photographs of kitchen records and an export of administrative data. Individuals are referred to by role only. The data export was handled in aggregate, and kitchen records are used as aggregates. The team set itself the rule that no recorded figure may be used against the person who recorded it, and carried it into the design as a principle. The recommended intervention keeps every arrangement that feeds or protects someone: resale, reuse, the meals of outside diners and staff, and late service. The pilot has stop conditions that include the withdrawal of consent and the use of any figure against a person. The team's plan includes its own withdrawal, so that the system does not come to depend on a student project. The interviewers were students of the institution, and the respondents were their peers and the staff of the halls, a relationship that can shape what is said. For that reason operator statements about their own practice were read as single-sourced until a record agreed.
