## Activity 7: Identifying Systemic Patterns and Root Causes {#sec-a7}

### Purpose, Approach and Timeline {#sec-a7-approach}

Activity 7 moved from symptoms to the structures and mechanisms that produce them. The deliverable is the Systemic Problem Analysis (Deliverable 3 of Phase 2); the loop work is reported in [[sec:a6]].

Table: Framework key activities of Activity 7, the method used and where each is reported. {#a7-methods cols=26,48,26}
| Key activity | Method used | Reported in |
|---|---|---|
| Distinguish events, patterns, structures and mental models | Seven areas traced from a dated event to the belief behind it, each layer sourced and labelled | [[sec:a7-c1]] to [[sec:a7-c7]] |

A team member drafted a first analysis on 12 to 14 September. On 13 September a rebuild required every layer to be sourced and labelled, no invented mental models, no single respondent turned into a population claim and the fictional opening description never used as evidence.

### Reading the System through the Iceberg {#sec-a7-iceberg}

An event is one thing that happened; a pattern is what keeps happening; a structure is the rule or design producing it; a mental model is the belief keeping it standing. Evidence labels follow [[ch:framework]]. Two observations recur: the institution has run breakfast with billing following attendance and switched it off, and the gap is measured twice and reaches nobody who can change the rules. Where lunch is recorded too, the gap belongs to breakfast.

![The seven chains at a glance: a visible event in each chain traced down to the belief that holds it in place.|Source: authors' analysis of interviews, records and field visits.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-seven-chains.png){w=100 #a7-chains}

### Chain 1: Students Register and Do Not Show Up {#sec-a7-c1}

Table: Chain 1, registered but not eating. {#a7-c1 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | A student books Kadamba vegetarian breakfast (Rs 48) daily, skips most days and never used Skip Meal, which "doesn't give any money back". Sunday 6 September: 267 registered at Yuktāhār, 50 ate (18.7 per cent). |
| Patterns | Registration beats attendance daily, far more at breakfast than lunch; registrations are flat while turnout swings, the mark of a default ([[sec:a5]]). Yuktāhār's September breakfast average of about 28 per cent sits below the 35 to 45 per cent the CFS Chair gave; Bakul's manager estimates at most about 60 per cent (single-sourced; candidate upper bound). |
| Structures | Billing counts registrations. Every student is registered automatically; the only exit is five cancellations per meal type per month, each at least four days ahead (confirmed). |
| Mental models | The mess office treats registration totals as a stand-in for demand, the strongest belief here because it survives contrary evidence. Students hold the booking is not theirs to manage ("We don't have any opportunity to nullify it"). |

The Kadamba manager believes friends eat in absent registrants' places (single-sourced); the scan export has no student identifier to test it.

**Root cause.** Billing tracks registration on a booking the student never actively makes. The counterfactual has happened: billing followed attendance in the shortage and holidays and the no-show problem eased (CFS Chair; [[sec:a7-c4]]), though no attendance record has been seen. The same gap appears under opposite kitchen philosophies, so the cause is upstream of the kitchen.

### Chain 2: Food Runs Out before the Mess Closes {#sec-a7-c2}

Table: Chain 2, run-outs at the close. {#a7-c2 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | Around 9:20 most mornings idli, puri and bhatura are gone at Kadamba and promised top-ups before the 9:30 close usually do not come. Thursday 3 September: Yuktāhār re-cooked upma at 9:00; its production sheet shows 77 of 278 registered eating (28 per cent), the attendance register 110 (40 per cent), two confirmed records that disagree. |
| Patterns | One crest: 26.5 per cent of Kadamba's April scans fell between 9:15 and 9:30, about nine a minute at 9:29 (confirmed; [[sec:a5]]). |
| Structures | The first batch is fixed before any turnout signal and top-up only moves upward: ten to fifteen minutes at Kadamba; at Bakul a call to Hafizpet, cooking and a drive of 20 to 40 minutes, behind a reserve for about 50 at 9:20 (candidate). Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour, which carried about 15 per cent of Kadamba's scans. |
| Mental models | The Yuktāhār site head carries "about 50 per cent" against a register of 18 to 40 per cent (confirmed). Kadamba has "no idea" of forecasting. |

**Root cause.** A kitchen can react to a same-day spike only upward and late; Yuktāhār's projection samples the calm opening.

### Chain 3: Complaints Are Ignored until Something Forces a Response {#sec-a7-c3}

Table: Chain 3, complaints and the response to them. {#a7-c3 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | November 2024: a frog in Kadamba's chicken biryani; non-vegetarian meals pulled without an official reason. December 2024: the cancellation cap was tightened after a renovation added Mess Office workload, with no record of committee sign-off, despite an unhappy Ping survey and two meetings with Parliament representatives. 2023: drinking-water complaints went unanswered until more than 38 confirmed typhoid cases. |
| Patterns | A complaint sits until a larger incident forces action; for a quick change the Mess Office tends to act alone; complaints reach the operator, who points to the rule owner. |
| Structures | No confirmed rule requires committee approval before an operational change. Menu, kitchen, calendar and billing sit with different owners. |
| Mental models | The Mess Office appears to see these decisions as its own, consultation optional under time pressure. At Yuktāhār waste is to learn from, not punish. |

**Root cause.** Nobody owns the whole picture, and on registration, billing and menu the system reacts only when something is loud. Oversight is intense on safety and taste, where failure is loud, and absent on quantity, where surplus is quiet. At Bakul a run-out has a route to the managing director and a surplus has none (candidate).

### Chain 4: A Working Fix Exists and Is Switched Off {#sec-a7-c4}

Table: Chain 4, the shock exception. {#a7-c4 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | In a gas shortage the mess cut to one item at the same price and the cancellation cap doubled from five to ten; Kadamba says it cut quantities and bought items outside (single-sourced). |
| Patterns | By the CFS Chair's account it worked every time and was switched off when the shortage or holiday ended, with no review of keeping part of it. |
| Structures | Kitchens' horizon for perishables is one day. Kadamba cooks about 30 extra portions for 30 to 50 outsiders such as night-shift security (an earlier figure for paying walk-ins may be the same allocation; unconfirmed). |
| Mental models | Someone in registration and billing values an automatic, pre-committed count over matching food to demand (candidate), most likely from administrative workload. |

![The same breakfast under the standing default and under the relaxed shock model.|Source: authors' analysis; the relaxed model is single-sourced.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-fix-switch.png){w=92 #a7-fixswitch}

**Root cause.** The counterexample to Chain 1: the institution solved the student half twice and did not keep it, and kitchens do not need the four-day close. Unchecked reasons: per-plate payment may count registrations, so a walk-in model would cost the operator revenue ([[sec:a3]]); an automatic default is less work; or the model reverts with the calendar and nobody decides. The shortage was described by a team member as war-related, uncorroborated by any institutional source.

### Chain 5: A Real Fix, and the Shadow It Cast {#sec-a7-c5}

Table: Chain 5, the menu rotation. {#a7-c5 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | CDS convinced the Student Council, which convinced the Student Parliament, to replace one semester-long menu with a fortnightly rotation. Unprompted, the CFS Chair worried that a known menu lets students skip disliked days. |
| Patterns | Menu-fatigue complaints dropped (CFS Chair). Non-vegetarian registrations at Kadamba were used at 48.5 per cent against 32.4 per cent for vegetarian (confirmed); biryani days reach about 700 registrations. |
| Structures | A real path runs from complaint to policy: Student Council, Parliament, Committee, and this time it worked; feedback is sorted by loudness. The menu is posted two weeks ahead; the cycle lets Yuktāhār plan from the matching day in its Wastage Book. |
| Mental models | CDS believed variety was worth a policy change and now questions whether more transparency is automatically good. The Bakul manager reasons from traditional pairings; whether they matter to residents is untested. |

**Root cause.** The opposite of Chain 3: the system responds when a problem is loud enough. Visibility that cures fatigue also enables avoidance, and the rotation gave kitchens a repeating unit to calibrate against that a cycle-breaking change would remove.

### Chain 6: A Meaningful Slice Is Not a Problem at All {#sec-a7-c6}

Table: Chain 6, legitimate non-attendance. {#a7-c6 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | One student fasts on purpose and stopped cooking breakfast years ago. Others say friends are a reason to go but never the deciding one. |
| Patterns | Late sleep is the reason given most, and records agree: Sunday is the lowest day in both halls (27.3 per cent at Kadamba, 17.9 to 24.1 per cent at Yuktāhār) with the latest arrivals (confirmed). Hackathons and exams push numbers down (Yuktāhār site head). |
| Structures | Breakfast draws less company than lunch. Only Yuktāhār offers a Jain line, about eight to fifteen registrations per breakfast. Everyone is registered by default, so a fast or a trip counts as a no-show. |
| Mental models | For at least one student sleep outranks a booked breakfast. The system assumes everyone should default into breakfast. |

**Root cause.** This slice sits outside the billing and governance story: some of the paradox is how a varied student body eats, and it is too small to explain the gap.

### Chain 7: The Kitchen Cooks to a Guess and Absorbs the Difference {#sec-a7-c7}

Table: Chain 7, cooking to a guess. {#a7-c7 cols=15,85}
| Layer | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Events | Shown the 18.7 per cent Sunday row, the Yuktāhār site head said the kitchen would adjust "not even next month, next week"; shown his own register, he still said "about 50 per cent" (confirmed). |
| Patterns | Kadamba's 70 per cent first batch against 37 per cent actual is about 1.9 times what is eaten; in April, 1,072 registrations and 398 scans a day make about 750 portions and a surplus near 350 (candidate arithmetic). At Bakul the 70 to 80 per cent share and weekend floor of about 70 per cent exceed the 60 per cent the manager expects (candidate). |
| Structures | Yuktāhār plans at a twice-daily meeting from portal registrations (regular, Jain, cash), the Wastage Book for the matching day and the weekday attendance register, with events or exams only if someone raises them; the book works as a rule ("If it runs out at 1:30 we re-cook; if at 2:20 we don't"; [[sec:a4]]). |
| Mental models | Three stances under one booking rule: Kadamba cooks to registrations as the job it is paid for ("We don't have that idea"); Yuktāhār treats waste as money, spends part of the margin on rock salt, cold-pressed oil and mill visits, and dropped a commercial ERP to "remain close to the data"; Bakul treats a shortage as the failure to avoid and waste as its price. |

**Root cause.** Once cooked, the conversion from booking to plate cannot be reversed, and no kitchen can change the number it starts from. The breakfast gap is about as large in both halls with records under opposite philosophies: calibration cuts waste and run-outs, not no-shows.

### Structural Causes and the Mechanism beneath Them {#sec-a7-structures}

Three structural causes recur across the chains, splitting the problem into students not showing up for what they booked and kitchens cooking more than is eaten ([[fig:a7-rootcauses]]).

![Three structural causes, the two conditions that keep them unaddressed, and the conversion ratio beneath them.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-root-causes.png){w=100 #a7-rootcauses}

**Registration is an automatic standing default.** With a narrow, early exit it cannot follow the morning decision, which makes the problem breakfast's: lunch runs under the same booking and mostly works.

**Billing fires at the booking, not at consumption.** This one rule explains the incentive to book broadly, the unused Skip Meal, the cap and Mess Cell. It was tested when billing followed attendance. It does not explain why kitchens cook above turnout.

**The gap is measured but unrouted.** The central system holds a timestamped scan for every registration and Yuktāhār holds per-item turnout and waste; neither reaches a rule owner, and oversight checks safety and taste but never quantity. That is why a fix that worked twice was never evaluated for keeping.

Two conditions keep all three unaddressed. First, action follows loudness at institution level while inside the kitchen quiet numbers work: menu fatigue was loud and got fixed, the larger registration gap is quiet and never travelled, and Yuktāhār cut waste with no complaint once measuring was blame-free. Second, the absorption layer: resale, the operator's discount and live top-up, leftover reuse, outside diners and staff, held and reheated food (candidate), the walk-in buffer, late service, outside venues and the bin each take up a consequence of the gap before anyone with authority sees it ([[sec:a8-equilibrium]]).

Beneath the kitchen half is the uncalibrated conversion ratio, in three regimes ([[fig:a7-ratio]]; [[sec:a4]]). It is downstream of the booking and the unrouted measurement, so not a root cause, but it is the most actionable structure because it sits inside authority operators already hold; one calibrates and a second adjusts weekly.

![The three regimes that turn a registration into a first batch.|Source: operator interviews and Yuktāhār's books; Bakul figures are the manager's statements.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-ratio-regimes.png){w=100 #a7-ratio}

Operators are paid monthly as a lump sum on plates, and what a plate counts is contested: the Bakul manager says plates served (single-sourced), Kadamba's operator says it is paid for the registrations (candidate, not a contract term). No contract has been seen.

### Feedback Loops Bearing on the Chains {#sec-a7-loops}

Eighteen mechanisms were checked for closure in [[sec:a6]] (L1 to L17 plus L3b). Three close, all balancing: L4 (menu rotation, Chain 5), L11 (matching-day calibration, Chain 7) and L12 (in-meal top-up, Chain 2).

### System Archetypes {#sec-a7-archetypes}

Eleven archetypes were checked against the system's variables ([[fig:a7-archetypes]]). Five hold up. Shifting the burden appears twice: Mess Cell resale for fixing billing, and reuse, outsiders, held food and the bin for calibrating the first batch. Growth hitting a ceiling fits Kadamba's popularity, with Hafizpet's kitchen a candidate ceiling for Bakul and Palash. Success to the successful fits Kadamba's scarcity feeding defensive registration while Bakul stays underfilled.

### Unintended Consequences {#sec-a7-unintended}

Billing on registration, meant to give the kitchen a stable number, produced registrations out of habit with no cost to skipping and a flat count that carries nothing about the weekday pattern. The cancellation cap, meant to limit workload, leaves students who exceed it or decide inside four days no legitimate correction, likely pushing them to silent no-shows or Mess Cell. Skip Meal pays nothing, so almost nobody uses it. The fixed first batch cooks below registrations yet over-prepares at breakfast: 70 and 70 to 80 per cent sit near Yuktāhār's lunch turnout, well above the 28 to 37 per cent breakfast turnout.

### Systemic Tensions {#sec-a7-tensions}

Eleven tensions were identified ([[fig:a7-tensions]]); in each both pulls are reasonable and operate at once.

![Eleven tensions: each pull is reasonable, and both operate at once.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-tensions.png){w=100 #a7-tensions}

1. Individual and collective: registering broadly and reselling is individually smart and, summed, is the waste and crowding.
2. Fixed count and real demand: administration wants an automatic count while the kitchen plans one day ahead.
3. Cooking once and in stages: once is simpler and keeps quality even (Yuktāhār: "student fluctuation is very high"; Bakul's aloo paratha started around 3 AM loses texture in the box).
4. Safety and freshness: sampling, display and tasting keep food safe and, with cooking ahead, leave it reheated through a two-hour service (candidate).
5. Duty and waste: at Kadamba cooking to registrations is duty and generates surplus; at Yuktāhār waste reduction is a profit lever.
6. Local and visible data: paper records keep planning with the planners and out of the rule owners' sight.
7. Welfare and visibility: feeding outsiders and staff absorbs surplus uncounted, and a cut to the first batch takes their food.
8. Shortage and waste: Bakul settled it by rule, as a shortage has a route to the managing director and a surplus none.
9. Nutrition and habit: the menu is chosen for nutrition and, in the Bakul manager's untested view, eaten by habit; advance notice fixes boredom and enables selective skipping.
10. Default and difference: registration built for one kind of student folds someone who fasts into the no-show figure.
11. Loud and quiet: escalation answers a loud problem and ignores a large quiet one.

The 8:30 class start has not been raised with the academic side, and the analysis does not suggest changing it: the crest is latest on Sundays, when there are no classes (candidate).

### Power Asymmetries {#sec-a7-power}

[[sec:a3]] holds the authority map. Students carry nearly all the visible cost (meals never eaten, crowding, run-outs) and control almost none of the rules. The operator is data-rich and authority-poor: Kadamba and Yuktāhār have no part in the registration rules that set their starting number or in the menu, and the Bakul manager's knowledge of which items are eaten and which pairings work goes unused by the menu process.

The academic side controls the schedule collision named in nearly every interview and faces none of its consequences; the calendar reaches Yuktāhār's kitchen by word of mouth (confirmed). The Committee sets the menu without a view of eaten against registered. The Warden holds authority over contracts and structure without a confirmed link to the numbers.

### Workarounds {#sec-a7-workarounds}

Every workaround absorbs a cost that would otherwise force someone to fix the real problem ([[fig:a7-workarounds]]). Mess Cell absorbs the financial pain of over-registering, so pressure to fix billing never builds, and it hides the gap, since a resold meal probably still appears as eaten by the original registrant; students replaced Skip Meal with it because it pays. Eating elsewhere (canteen, juice stall, delivery app) gets around a fixed window and a fasting calendar, usually by paying twice.

![Where the system leaves a gap and what people have built to absorb it.|Source: student interviews and operator interviews and visits.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s07-workarounds.png){w=100 #a7-workarounds}

Operators have their own layer ([[sec:a7-c7]]). Yuktāhār holds raw material to an impurity norm, opens two roti counters at peak and covers absences by agreement in the daily meeting. Kadamba writes ID and name by hand when the scanner fails.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a7-decisions}

Decision: An event is a single concrete happening; standing design facts (four-day close, billing on registration, the CFS Chair's percentages) moved to structure and pattern, because a mislabelled iceberg would send every later layer wrong. Keeping the old labels was rejected.

Decision: The fictional opening description was never used as evidence about the institute, and no single respondent became a population claim; what it names (an energy crash, a second purchase) stays untested.

Decision: Seven areas were each traced fully instead of one worked example and six summaries; Chain 6 was kept although it is not a problem to fix, so legitimate non-attendance is not counted as dysfunction.

Decision: Only structures passing a root-cause test are root causes; the four-day procurement lock and coarse information were demoted, and the conversion ratio is an actionable mechanism, not a cause.

Decision: All eleven archetypes were tested against real variables; fixes that fail stays a candidate on second-hand accounts of the December 2024 tightening, and four were rejected with a stated reason.

Decision: One loop scheme (L1 to L17 plus L3b) serves Phase 2 and older names are retired; the two readings of the plate basis, Skip Meal and the head of CDS stay open.
