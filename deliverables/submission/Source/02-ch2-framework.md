# Project Framework and Approach {#ch-framework}

## The Course Assignment {#sec-fw-brief}

The project was set within a course in Systems Thinking and Design. Four campus puzzles were offered to the student teams: the Breakfast Paradox, the 11 O'Clock Cliff, the Research Lab Commons and the Induction Week Pile-Up. The instruction that came with them set the tone for everything that followed.

Team Invictus chose the Breakfast Paradox. The course text presents it as a morning in the life of a student called Rohan. His alarm has gone off four times at 7:45 and he worked late the night before, so twenty extra minutes of sleep give him immediate relief while the benefit of breakfast feels far away. Kadamba serves breakfast from 7:30 to 9:30 for the entire hostel batch, classes start at 8:30, and barely a third of the hostel shows up on most mornings.

The course asks one system question of this story: how do class schedules, sleep habits, food preparation and daily energy levels feed into each other to create a continuous loop of wasted food and tired students? The story names a single villain, the clock, and a chain of consequences.

## The Course Framework {#sec-fw-framework}

The framework supplied by the course divides the work into phases, each phase into activities, and each activity into a purpose, a set of key activities or methods, and an expected deliverable. Phase 1 (Activities 1 to 5) is about understanding the system, Phase 2 (Activities 6 to 8) about modelling it and reframing the problem, and Phases 3 and 4 (Activities 9 and 10) about finding where it can be changed and designing, testing and evaluating interventions. There are ten activities and fifteen deliverables.

The framework lists five deliverables for Phase 1, five for Phase 2 and five for Phases 3 and 4. Phase 2 is the only phase where one activity yields two deliverables: Activity 6 yields the System Map and the Causal Loop and Feedback Analysis, and Activity 8 yields the Reframed Systemic Problem Statement and the Design Opportunity Statement.

The purpose, key activities and expected deliverable of each activity are set out at the head of its section in Chapters 5 to 8, in the framework's own wording.

## The Working Method {#sec-fw-method}

Three habits governed how the team worked, and each was adopted because an earlier week had gone wrong without it.

The first was dependency order. Phase 2 stands on Phase 1, and Phases 3 and 4 stand on both, so when new evidence arrived the team revised Phase 1 first, then Phase 2, then Activity 9, then Activity 10.

The second was a fixed revision cycle for every deliverable. A gap audit compared the deliverable section by section with the new evidence.

The third was evidence precedence. Each batch of field evidence was written up as one dated evidence summary, and every later revision drew its facts from that summary and no longer from earlier deliverables.

Decisions were logged as they were taken, with the date and the reason, and when a later decision changed an earlier one both stayed on record with the link between them. Appendix B is the register of those decisions in the order they were made.

## Documentation Conventions {#sec-fw-conventions}

A small set of conventions was fixed early and applied to every deliverable. Staff and interviewees are named by role, and the three team members are the only people named. The spellings Yuktāhār, Palash, Kadamba and Bakul are used throughout. Section headings in each deliverable follow the framework's own wording and order, so that a reader can find each required method without being told where to look, and each deliverable closes with the evidence still outstanding.

## From One Phase to the Next {#sec-fw-handover}

Each phase ended in a statement that the next phase used as its starting point. Phase 1 closed with a picture of a system in which registration is a default, each kitchen applies its own guess to it, and the gap is measured twice and routed nowhere ([[ch:phase1]]). Phase 2 turned that picture into one map and one loop scheme, traced seven events down to their structures, and closed with a reframed problem and a design opportunity that came with four intervention points, a list of what any intervention must respect, and two questions that gate the rest ([[ch:phase2]]).
