### System Boundary and Context {#sec-a1-boundary}

#### System boundary

The system is the breakfast meal as registered, billed, cooked and served in four halls: Kadamba, Palash, Bakul and Yuktāhār. Three layers sit inside the boundary ([[fig:a1-boundary]]). Registration and billing is the dining portal plus the counter QR scan. The kitchen layer is each hall's outside caterer (Prism, ABC Hospitality Services, Vijayalakshmi Caterers at Bakul, cooking at Hafizpet, single-sourced).

On the boundary are suppliers, the garbage contractor, the site team (relays complaints), the facility team (weekly inspection) and the tender. At Kadamba 30 to 50 outsiders such as night security and about half the operator's staff eat from the same pots, so cutting surplus also cuts food they rely on (single-sourced). The class schedule is outside but overlaps the system's timings; other places to eat lie at the outer edge.

#### Context

The published window is 7:30 to 9:30 AM, though service ran later ([[sec:a5]]). Classes run 8:30 AM to 6:40 PM, with a break from 1:00 to 2:00 PM. Breakfast capacity on one September day totalled 2,755 ([[tab:a1-halls]]). Without registration the same breakfast cost Rs 81, 111 and 92, about 70 per cent more (confirmed, posted rates).

Registration is automatic: every student is registered for every meal, on campus or not, holidays included. Skip Meal tells the kitchen not to prepare a portion but leaves the charge.

- Academic administration sets the timetable; exams, events and holidays reach a kitchen only if raised in its planning meeting: "we don't come to know until we sit in the meeting" (Yuktāhār site head, confirmed).
- Governance is split between a Mess Committee, a Mess Office and a Warden; operators speak of a "CDS team" and Kadamba's describes CFS to CDS to Prism (single-sourced). The mapping is unconfirmed ([[sec:a2]]).

- Mess Cell is a student WhatsApp group where a registration is sold at a negotiated price by passing on the QR credential.
- The food ecosystem: Vindhya Canteen (Monday to Saturday from 10:00 AM), juice canteens, delivery apps (orders until 11:00 PM).

### Purpose and Intended Outcomes {#sec-a1-purpose}

#### Purpose

The system exists to give resident students reliable, adequate, good-quality food each morning at a predictable, affordable cost, covering vegetarian, non-vegetarian and Jain requirements, for health and academic performance. The institution states its objective as nutritious, hygienic and affordable meals with efficient operations and "minimizing food wastage through advance meal planning", relying on advance registration.

#### Actual mechanism

Registration is a default students do not choose, so it records the absence of an opt-out and stays flat across the week ([[sec:a5]]). Kitchens do not cook to it: each operator reads the closed count the day before and applies its own conversion ([[sec:a4]]). Yuktāhār plans from its books and the first half hour of service (confirmed for the books). Kadamba cooks a fixed first batch of about 70 per cent and a supervisor calls for more in 10 to 15 minutes; no record carries one day's outcome into the next.

Every rule sits above turnout. Kadamba's 70 per cent is about twice the 37 per cent that arrives (candidate). Bakul's batch and floor exceed its manager's own ceiling of about 60 per cent, a belief and, if Bakul resembles the recorded halls, an upper bound (candidate; no Bakul records).

Surplus has four exits: reuse at Yuktāhār, which weighs plate and kitchen waste (confirmed), outside diners and staff at Kadamba, held and reheated food (candidate) and the bin. Composting (Kadamba's waste goes to a garbage contractor, single-sourced) is not an exit. Kadamba's operator puts daily waste at 5 to 10 per cent without saying of what, an open tension with a first batch near twice turnout ([[sec:a6]]).

#### Gap between purpose and mechanism

Advance planning works only if registration expresses intent to eat. Registrations overstate breakfast turnout about 2.7 times at Kadamba and 3.5 times at Yuktāhār (confirmed, records), and every operator discounts by its own rule. The gap is already measured, in scan records and registers, but nothing carries it into registration or billing rules; at Bakul it may exist only in one manager's head.

#### Resources

The first resource is the institution's scan-level record of who ate and when, which measures the registration-to-turnout gap for every meal scanned (confirmed for Kadamba breakfast; assumed elsewhere). The second is the operators' own records, so the gap is already measured locally by those who cook. Kadamba holds per-dish gram standards, a live count in service, 72-hour retained samples and a daily water log (single-sourced).

### Initial Problem Framing {#sec-a1-framing}

Most breakfast registrations go unused, about 63 per cent at Kadamba in April 2026 and about 72 per cent at Yuktāhār in September, while Yuktāhār lunch is mostly used; students pay for every registered breakfast either way. Each kitchen cooks to its own discounted guess, yet Kadamba's first batch stays far above arrivals while the closing rush still causes shortage. Bakul shows the same pattern on its manager's account (single-sourced; no records), who blames students not waking and taste and dish pairing (a candidate influence, untested with residents).

> The problem: breakfast registration does not reflect who is going to eat, by a margin of roughly two thirds, while the same system works for lunch at Yuktāhār, the one hall with lunch records. Why not, and what in the system causes that gap? And why does each kitchen have to guess its own conversion from registrations to plates, when the data needed to calibrate it is already being recorded, and when one operator learns from its own books, another applies a fixed ratio, and a third adjusts by judgement?

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a1-decisions}

Decision: Scope the study to breakfast and keep lunch and dinner outside the boundary as a control, to test whether the cause is the meal or the system.

The opening story named one cause, a late night against an early class; each link was written as a claim, and only the first (late nights leading to skipped mornings) had testimony behind it.

Decision: Treat the opening description as a set of hypotheses and record which have evidence, instead of starting from its single cause.

On 5 September the purpose section was split in three, because a registration that rewards staying booked over attending explained more than the class clash alone.

Decision: Report purpose, actual mechanism and the gap between them as three separate findings, so that the rules are examined before the students are.

Decision: Follow the framework's section wording and order, state findings directly, and attach an evidence label to every claim.

Decision: Keep a contributor's original text on file next to any hypothesis taken from it, so that attribution survives the merge (a teammate's hypothesis that Skip Meal and Mess Cell make staying booked dominant was integrated this way).

Decision: When field evidence contradicts a statement in the brief, change the statement and mark the revision where it appears, without blending old and new versions.
