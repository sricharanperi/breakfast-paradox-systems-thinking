# Phase 3: Identifying Leverage Points and Design Principles {#ch-phase3}

Phase 3 consists of Activity 9. Phase 2 left the team with a diagnosis: the Breakfast Paradox is a stable equilibrium held in place by three confirmed structures (an automatic registration default with a narrow exit, billing when the registration is made, and a gap that the scan record and the operators' books both measure but that reaches nobody who sets the rules), and by the step in which a registration becomes a cooked first batch that any kitchen can top up and none can shrink. Phase 3 asks where such a system can change. It inherits from Phase 2 the reframed statement, the four intervention points, the guardrails and the gating questions ([[sec:a8]]), the loop analysis ([[sec:a6]]) and the records of [[sec:a5]]. It hands seven design principles and fourteen intervention points to Phase 4.

The activity was written on 27 September 2026, directly after the second revision of Phases 1 and 2, and revised twice more on 28 September. The revisions are told in the last section of the activity. Claims keep the project's evidence labels. Bakul has no records of any kind, so no Bakul effect is sized anywhere in Phases 3 and 4.

The evidence is the whole of Phases 1 and 2, the Kadamba scan records for April 2026, the Yuktāhār registers for September, and the field accounts of the three operators. The activity was drafted on 27 September, with its figures, and then checked claim by claim against the sources and the decision log by a second reviewer, whose corrections were applied in place. On 28 September it was revised against the team's course material on leverage and intervention, which added two levers (LP11 and LP12), a seventh principle, the archetype diagnosis, the case for the status quo and the stated depth-plus-feasibility ranking. The Bakul account of the same day was then carried through the dependency chain: no lever or intervention point was added and the ranking did not change, but the shortage constraint, the held reserve and the per-hall treatment of the crest were written into LP5, LP6, LP8, LP11 and the principles.

Three conventions govern the whole activity. Depth is counted on Meadows' (1999) twelve places, with place 1 the deepest (the power to transcend a paradigm), place 2 the mindset, place 3 the goals, place 5 the rules, place 6 the information flows and place 12 the parameters; some presentations number the scale the other way round, so every lever carries the words "place n of 12". Place 5 is used only where a rule itself changes. Place 9, on delays, would mean flattening the closing crest, which no lever attempts, and places 11 and 12 appear only as settings inside a deeper lever: cutting Kadamba's 70 per cent ratio by decree would move the cost from surplus to run-outs at the crest, and the cancellation cap, raised from five to ten during a gas shortage and back at five now (single-sourced), changes only how many exits the default allows. The second convention is the ruling-out rule below. The third is the feasibility scale for a student team, which can propose, pilot and evidence a change but cannot enact a rule: high means a prototype or pilot with an actor already engaged, medium an evidenced proposal to an existing forum, low a proposal only, either because the holder is out of reach or because the lever rests on a fact nobody has.

Decision: Count depth from the deepest place and write "place n of 12" on every lever, because the course material and the published scale number the places in opposite directions and mixed labels had already crept into the first draft.

Decision: Rule out any lever that removes a mechanism protecting someone (resale, reuse of leftovers, meals for 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff, the walk-in buffer, late and back-door service) and treat only the over-cooked first batch, repeated reheating and the bin as fair targets. The alternative, a lever that creates a signal by removing an absorber, was rejected because it would raise the cost of someone who is not at fault.

#### LP1: Build the missing routes

Of Kadamba's 32,162 breakfast registrations in April 2026, 37.1 per cent were eaten, and Yuktāhār's September breakfast ran at 17.7 to 40.2 per cent against 56 to 89 per cent for lunch (confirmed). No route runs from any record to any rule owner, and Yuktāhār's own books disagree on 3 September, 77 eaten against 110. The lever is a standing breakfast view, the ledger, of booked, cooked, eaten and left over per hall, weekday and line, sent to the rule owner and back to the operators under one agreed definition of "ate". It also carries a weekly note of exams, events and holidays down to the day-before planning meeting, which at Yuktāhār reaches the kitchen by word of mouth. It has to be blame-free and accept paper, because Yuktāhār has already dropped a commercial ERP system once (single-sourced). Delivery waits on a receiver, since two different people are on record as head of CDS. A bill line showing breakfasts paid for and not eaten is a student-facing variant, but without a way to respond it would only confirm that the charge cannot be avoided. The records already exist on paper, as the photographed Yuktāhār register shows ([[sec:a3]]).

#### LP2: Change the breakfast default and its exit

Registrations hold at about 1,070 every weekday while turnout runs from 42.1 per cent on Tuesdays to 27.3 per cent on Sundays (confirmed). The four-day lead protects only dry goods, since Kadamba buys perishables the day before and Yuktāhār takes milk daily, and the Kadamba team has already pushed back on the cut-off (single-sourced). A coalition of students and operators for a shorter lead is therefore the first shared interest on record (candidate). The lever is the default and its exit, and the cap is secondary. The options, lighter to heavier, are a one- or two-day lead, an evening-before confirm-or-skip that lowers the count, Sunday opt-in, and no registration while off campus. A lower count shrinks Kadamba's first batch only if it is the count the kitchen reads, and whether Skip Meal already lowers it is an open conflict. A skip must never cost a hall slot: one Kadamba respondent books the whole month because the hall fills up (L3b, candidate). The payment basis is unconfirmed for every operator; the Bakul manager says plates served and Kadamba's remark points to registrations (both single-sourced), so Bakul's position on LP2 is likely neutral or favourable if its account holds (candidate). If resale falls, it falls because the need has gone, and no absorber is removed.

#### LP3: Operator goal: surplus is a cost

The purpose would move from "cook for every booking" to "feed whoever comes, at least cost" (place 3), with a numeric target inside L11 (place 8). The operators state different purposes under the same rules. Yuktāhār names quality, less waste, lower cost, standardisation and profit. Kadamba cooks to registrations "because it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it". The Bakul manager's rule is "there should not be a shortage of food, even if some wastage occurs" (all single-sourced), and at Bakul a run-out sends a complaint to the managing director while surplus stays silent, a kitchen-level case of a system that reacts only to what is loud (candidate). The change is a wastage board, a target and a purpose stated by the operations manager, with the target anchored to the best recorded performance (Yuktāhār's rate or the institution's own goal) and never to last month's average, which drifts down. Smaller portions are the cheapest cut, so every target carries grams per plate, taste and run-outs (P7). A cost-per-line case can go to Prism's operations manager, who has not been interviewed.

#### LP4: A standing operator forum that runs its own trials

The operators already plan differently, and practice separates them more than rules do: Yuktāhār's matching-week lookup (books photographed, confirmed), Kadamba's fixed ratio, and Bakul's weekly judgement above a floor (both single-sourced). Yuktāhār's waste fell between August and December once weighing was made blame-free (single-sourced, with no before-and-after figures). Kadamba keeps gram standards but no learning record, and the Bakul operator believes Kadamba fills 90 to 95 per cent where the records say 37 per cent at breakfast (confirmed). The lever is a forum convened by CDS and hosted by Yuktāhār's two project managers, where each hall runs its own trial and the ledger shows the results. Swapping books alone is place 6, while a forum with the power to use the ledger to choose what works is place 4. What has to transfer is the set of preconditions: blame-free weighing, daily discussion and a purpose that treats waste as cost. Kadamba's layered chain adds a risk of reprimand.

#### LP5: Record-calibrated first batch with a daily learning record

L11 closes at Yuktāhār, is a candidate instance bounded by a floor at Bakul, and is absent at Kadamba. Creating it at Kadamba is place 6, and strengthening it where it already runs is place 8. Kadamba's 70 per cent becomes 100 per cent for biryani lunch (single-sourced), so the ratio follows expectation and no record. April turnout was 32.4 per cent on the veg line and 48.5 per cent on non-veg (confirmed), so the batch is about 2.2 times what was eaten on veg and about 1.4 times on non-veg (candidate arithmetic). The lever sets the batch as the gram standard times expected turnout per hall, weekday and line, plus a named allowance for outside diners and staff and a float for bonda, puri, vada and idli. It starts on the veg line with a one-page paper record read the next day. A 70 per cent batch against about 398 eaten implies roughly 350 surplus portions (candidate), at odds with Kadamba's stated 5 to 10 per cent waste, so the lever cannot yet be sized. The Bakul manager puts turnout at most around 60 per cent, below his floor of about 70 per cent (single-sourced), and the floor stays a named constraint until item-level records test it. LP5 is a quick fix, paired with LP1 against the L17 risk and with LP6 against run-outs.

#### LP6: Crest-aware top-up and a staged late batch

At Kadamba 26.5 per cent of April scans land between 9:15 and 9:30, rising to about nine a minute by 9:29, and 9.2 per cent, about 37 a day, arrive at or after 9:30, the latest at 10:07 (confirmed). The operator's estimate of about 150 diners in the last ten minutes overstates this and may drive the hedge (candidate). Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour, when only 15.3 per cent of Kadamba's scans have arrived. The lever has three parts: a weekday arrival card telling the supervisor at 8:00 what share of the day has come (place 6); a late batch started just after 9:00 on that basis (place 10, since it changes when stock is created); and a grace period after 9:30, always paired with the late batch. The share seen by 8:00 runs from 13.5 per cent on Sundays to 22.5 per cent on Wednesdays, and the share after 9:15 from about 32 per cent (Tuesdays, Wednesdays) to about 46 per cent (Sundays), so one flat factor would misread Sunday and Wednesday by about a quarter (confirmed; used as factors, candidate). Nothing here transfers to Bakul, where the manager puts his rush at 8:00 to 8:30 and aims to have food for about 50 at 9:20 (single-sourced). A Bakul top-up needs a phone call, cooking at Hafizpet and a drive of about 8 km, roughly 20 minutes if called early and 40 otherwise, and the end-to-end lead time has never been measured. The Bakul lever is the size of the held reserve, which is a hedge and belongs in a separate category from the absorbers (candidate), and the latest call that can still land, both set from measured data. LP6 belongs to the quick fix.

#### LP7: A cost trigger for attention, carried on the oversight line

Menu fatigue was fixed through the Student Council, the Parliament and the CDS Committee (confirmed, L4), but the far larger breakfast gap never entered that pathway. The facility team tastes every Kadamba meal with two Prism staff (confirmed, observed) and never checks quantity. The lever is a standing threshold, such as cooked-against-eaten above a set level for several weeks, that sends the case to the Committee as complaints already do. It is anchored to the stated goal, lunch turnout or Yuktāhār's rate, never to the hall's own past, and it reports run-outs alongside surplus. The cheapest carrier is one extra line at the tasting. Because an inspector recording quantity can look like an audit, a breach triggers a review and never a deduction.

#### LP8: Operator voice in the menu

Kadamba's uttapam, set dosa and upma waste most while puri, bonda, vada and idli move well (single-sourced), yet students or the committee choose the menu and the kitchen has no say. The Bakul manager wants menu input, knows what sells (pav bhaji) and which pairings he thinks work (kachori with chole), but his view that students would eat more of familiar pairings is untested with residents (single-sourced). The lever is a standing operator slot at each two-week menu cycle to report item waste and run-outs. It builds the missing route L16, the quiet counterpart of L4. Operator input is read with consumption records and checked against residents' views, so it stays a voice and never becomes authority.

#### LP9 and LP10: the two held levers

Decision rights over the outcome are split at least five ways, across registration, billing, menu, cooked quantity and the academic calendar (confirmed), and whoever sets registration and billing bears none of the cost they produce. LP9, a named owner of booked, cooked and eaten, would put the consequence where the decision is made and would inherit LP1; Yuktāhār's Wastage Register keeper is a lighter version. It is held until the head of CDS is identified. LP10, the cost of a booking, rests on a precedent: attendance-tracked billing ran during a gas shortage and some holiday and fest periods, and it worked (confirmed). Operators are paid monthly as a lump sum on plates, with deductions (single-sourced, noisy audio), but no contract has been seen and the basis may differ by operator. It is held because a billing change made blind to the payment terms could push the operator incentive the wrong way, and because student reliance on resale income needs checking first.

Decision: Hold LP9 and LP10, and every concept that depends on them, until the head of CDS and the payment basis are known. A billing change made without the payment terms could reverse the operator incentive, so the team recorded the lever and left it unscored as a recommendation.

#### LP12: Change the goal the system actually seeks

The CDS poster gives the institution's goal as minimising food wastage through advance meal planning (confirmed). The goal it pursues in practice is the registration count: students are billed on it and Kadamba cooks on it (candidate reading). The measure of the stated goal is recorded and read by no one. The lever makes booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, per hall and line, the real indicators, read with quality and run-outs, because a wastage goal alone could be met by running short. CFS and CDS carry this by restating their own goal and asking for its indicators. LP12 is the move from the registration count to cooked-against-eaten, and the other levers make that move possible.

Kadamba and Yuktāhār share registration, billing and, as far as is known, tendered plate payment, yet Yuktāhār plans from records, re-cooks by run-out time and weighs blame-free on paper, while Kadamba cooks a fixed ratio ([[fig:a9-comparison]]). ### Developing Design Principles {#sec-a9-principles}

#### The design objective and the case for the status quo

The objective is a breakfast system in which registration matches each student's intent, the kitchen cooks to expected eaters, and the cost of any gap reaches whoever sets the rules. It does not mean ending registration, resale or all waste: registration gives the planning count, resale recovers money, and some surplus is the price of never running out. Over-booking and over-cooking should become noticed exceptions. The guiding idea from the archetypes is to change the architecture and to stop competing at the kitchen layer.

The status quo persists because it pays each actor. The levers keep what each actor values (the seat, the resale, the absorbers, run-out protection) and change what drives over-booking ([[tab:a9-status-quo]]).

#### Deliverable 2: the design principles

Seven principles follow from the leverage points, the archetypes and the evidence, and every Phase 4 concept must pass all seven ([[fig:a9-principles]], [[tab:a9-p]]).

![The seven principles, with what each rules out.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-3-4-rebuild/Assets/a9-principles.png){w=92 #a9-principles}

Table: The design principles P1 to P7: statement, basis and what each rules out. {#a9-p cols=22,45,33}
| Principle | Basis | Rules out |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Route before you measure, and route on paper | The scan export, Yuktāhār's books and Kadamba's stated waste share already measure the gap. Yuktāhār dropped a commercial ERP over cost and staff readiness (single-sourced), so routes start from a book page and use existing channels: the daily meeting, the tasting, the menu cycle | New apps, sensors or forecasting software as a first step; designs that need operators to go digital |
| P2 Make measurement blame-free, and judge it over months | No recorded figure may justify a deduction against the staff who recorded it. Yuktāhār's weighing worked only once it was blame-free and discussed daily, and waste took from August to December to fall (single-sourced). One run-out after a smaller batch, judged over a week, would bring the hedge straight back | No audit-style quantity checks, no deductions from recorded figures, no one-week trials |
| P3 Change the default, the goal and the belief, not the people | Every actor is treated as rational given its incentives and information. Students are registered by a default they never chose, operators run the only working balancing loops (L11, L12), and the institution states one goal and pursues another | Nudges or campaigns aimed at students; blaming operators; changing the cancellation count while the default stays untouched |
| P4 Act on the first batch the day before, and on the morning only through the top-up | Morning levers work through smaller, later top-ups or, where a top-up cannot land in time, a sized reserve. Kadamba fixes the batch three and a half to four hours before the crest, Bakul can start cooking around 3 AM (single-sourced), and arrival curves and line turnouts differ widely. One hall's rule is not carried to another before that hall has records | No lever that cuts waste by raising shortage risk; no one ratio for every hall |
| P5 Feed the absorbers by plan, name them as symptomatic outlets, and target only the over-cooked batch, repeated reheating and the bin | A concept that shrinks surplus must name who eats it and budget their meals. Thirty to fifty outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff already eat this food (single-sourced), and calling their meals "not wasted" would hide the gap again | Restricting resale, closing the back door and cutting the batch without an allowance |
| P6 Measure success as cooked against eaten, run-outs and food quality, not turnout | Falling waste with rising run-outs does not count. Some skipping is legitimate (confirmed across interviews), and Bakul's food, held at about 60 to 72 °C, keeps its temperature while aloo paratha softens (single-sourced) | Turnout targets; waste cuts sized before Kadamba's 5 to 10% base is known; temperature-only quality checks |
| P7 Measures are diagnostic and plural, never a single target | Measures are for learning and never for reward or penalty. Cooked-against-eaten always comes with grams per plate, taste and run-outs, because a kitchen held to one figure can meet it by cutting portions or letting the crest run short, and a vendor rewarded on it can meet it through the rate | A single headline target; any bonus, deduction or ranking tied to one ledger figure; waste figures reported alone |

Decision: Make blame-free measurement a rule of the design (P2), and add a seventh principle against single targets (P7) after the revision, because a figure that can be gamed or used against the person who recorded it stops being recorded honestly.

### Key Findings {#sec-a9-findings}

1. The system has its measurements and no routes for them. The enabling lever (place 6 of 12) routes existing records to the kitchen and the rule owners, and a student team can prototype it now as a paper ledger.
2. The loops form a shifting-the-burden structure. Kitchen levers are the quick fix; booking, goal, belief and route levers are the fundamental fix.
3. The deepest levers are the beliefs that let the registration count stand in for demand (place 2) and the system's working goal (place 3). The deepest rule lever that is not gated on an unknown is the breakfast default and its exit (place 5), provided no skip ever costs a student a hall slot.
4. Only two levers touch an absorber that protects someone, LP5 and LP6, and both keep it intact. LP9 and LP10 are held until the head of CDS and the payment basis are known.
5. Kadamba and Yuktāhār show that a learning practice is possible under the same rules, but five other differences keep that reading a candidate.
6. Sunday breakfast on Kadamba's veg line is where three levers meet, and it is the first slice for Phase 4. Every figure behind it is Kadamba's, because Bakul has no records.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a9-decisions}

The decisions of the activity are recorded where they were taken. Three revisions followed the first draft of 27 September.

The first check, by a second reviewer against the sources, produced a set of corrections. The untagged claim that the cancellation cap had moved from five to ten and back without changing the pattern was replaced by the single-sourced statement used above, and the half that claimed an unchanged pattern was dropped because no record supports it. The precedent for LP10 was widened from a gas shortage to some holiday and fest periods, the Yuktāhār comparison was downgraded from "proves" to "shows (candidate)", and a blanket statement about the map was split between the route, goal and convenor levers and the rule levers. The intervention points were numbered on the diagram.

The revision of 28 September against the course material on leverage and intervention changed more. The place numbering was fixed in one direction after the team found that Meadows counts the scale from the deepest place while the course material counts parameters as the first level, and that the first draft had mixed the two. LP3 was split into an operator purpose (place 3) and a waste target (place 8). LP4 was renamed a standing forum that runs its own trials and moved to place 4. LP5 was placed at 6 where the learning link is new and at 8 where it is strengthened. LP11, LP12 and P7 were added, the archetype section was written, and the stated ranking replaced an earlier "deepest actionable" ordering. The archetype of eroding goals, first judged a candidate, was reduced to a risk because no lowered target is on record, a conflict with the Phase 2 archetype check that is stated and not resolved.

The third revision, on 28 September, carried the Bakul account through the dependency chain. LP5 at Bakul became the strengthening of an existing weekly calibration with its floor and shortage constraint explicit; LP6 became per hall; LP3 and LP12 gained the shortage-first goal; LP11 gained the four Bakul beliefs with their tests; LP8 was guarded by records and resident validation; LP10 stayed held with Bakul's "plates served" recorded; and P4, P6 and P7 gained the shortage constraint and the texture and holding-time measures. The "70 to 80 per cent raised from 70" claim was removed from four places. No new lever or intervention point was added.
