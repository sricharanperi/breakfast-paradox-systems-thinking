# Phase 4: Designing, Testing and Evaluating Interventions {#ch-phase4}

Phase 4 consists of Activity 10. It asks the team to develop and assess interventions that improve outcomes across the system as a whole, whichever stakeholder gains most. The concepts aim at the three confirmed structures that hold the paradox in place and at the step in which registrations become a first batch. A pilot was not possible within the course, so every test in this chapter is a test on paper. The scenario model replays real Kadamba records under stated assumptions, and its outputs are labelled projected: they are arithmetic on records, never an observed result. The reactions of stakeholders are labelled simulated, because they are reasoned from each actor's stated position and no feedback was collected, and every co-design test is labelled planned, because none has been run. All modelled figures are Kadamba's, since Bakul has no records.

The activity was written on 27 and 28 September 2026 on the output of Activity 9 ([[sec:a9]]), which it treats as authoritative for LP1 to LP12, IP1 to IP14 and P1 to P7.

**A: The Breakfast Ledger.** Each week the scan export and the operators' books merge into one sheet per hall, by weekday and line, with the money charged for uneaten breakfasts. Kitchen levers shrink surplus but leave that charge flat until a rule moves, so a falling surplus cannot pass for "problem solved". A cooked-against-eaten threshold, anchored outside the hall's own past, alerts the escalation forum next to the existing complaint trigger, and a weekly note of exams and events goes to planning. A builds the missing route (L10), adds a second trigger to L4, counters L17 and brings the exam dips of L7 to the kitchen.

**B: The Kitchen Learning Kit.** Kadamba's fixed ratio becomes a day-before plan card built from expected eaters, with the 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff allowed for by name, and a daily paper sheet read the next day. An operator forum, convened by CDS and hosted by Yuktāhār's project managers, carries over what makes Yuktāhār's Wastage Book work: blame-free weighing, daily discussion and surplus treated as cost. At Bakul, whose manager already adjusts weekly by item, the kit works as a check more than a rule, and the floor stays until a record shows how often a smaller batch would run short. B creates L11 at Kadamba, shortens the kitchen half of L13 and reaches L8 through cost.

**C: The Crest Plan.** At 9:00 the supervisor projects the day from a weekday arrival card and starts a late batch that lands by about 9:15, so the first batch covers only the earlier diners. A grace period after 9:30 covers only late-batch food. All of it depends on Kadamba's 10 to 15 minute on-site top-up. A Bakul top-up needs a phone call, cooking at Hafizpet and a 20 to 40 minute drive (single-sourced), so its hedge is the roughly 50 portions held at 9:20 (candidate), and C there means sizing that reserve and the latest call that can still land. C gives L12 sight of the crest, removes L14's reason to hedge and bounds L15.

**D: The Sunday Exit.** Sunday has the lowest turnout at both recorded halls. It was drafted first as an opt-in, charged only if confirmed by 8 pm on Saturday, with a student who forgets paying the walk-in rate. A lighter variant keeps the default and adds a skip, open until the same hour, that removes the charge and lowers the kitchen's count. Other days keep the four-day lead and the cap of five, and no skip may cost a hall place. D acts on the booking end of L13, on L2 (Skip Meal fails today because a skip returns nothing) and on L1.

**E: The Operator Menu Slot.** At each fortnightly menu cycle operators bring the items that wasted most (uttapam, set dosa and upma at Kadamba, single-sourced) and those that ran out. The Bakul manager thinks pairings are the real problem (single-sourced), so operator input counts as a voice and never as authority, read beside consumption data and residents' wishes. E supplies the missing link of L16.

**F: Billed at Consumption (held).** Breakfast is charged on the scan, as it was during a gas shortage and some fest periods (confirmed), acting on L1, L2, L6 and L8, subject to a check on resale income. It is held until each operator's payment basis is confirmed, since Bakul's manager reports plates served while Kadamba's operator says it cooks on, and is paid for, registrations (both single-sourced).

**G: Ratio Cut to 50 Percent (foil).** Kadamba's first batch drops from 70 to 50 per cent by instruction, with no record, late batch or allowance. It changes no loop and breaks P4 and P5.

**H: The Shared Reading.** Joint sessions around the ledger ask each belief holder what produces their figure and what would change it: the Yuktāhār site head's "about 50 per cent" against his own register at about 28, the Kadamba supervisor's crest prediction, and the registration side's reasons for the automatic default. CFS and CDS are asked to adopt the CDS poster's existing goal, minimising food wastage through advance planning, as two indicators, booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, read beside the quality measures of P7. The Bakul manager's beliefs wait on a Bakul record. H acts on the mental models under every chain and on the working goal behind L10, L13 and L17.

Decision: Keep concept G, a blunt cut that the team expected to fail, in every test as a foil. Testing only ideas the team believed in would risk every test passing, and G's failure in the model shows what a good concept does differently.

Decision: Hold concept F (billing at consumption) behind the plate basis. It has the deepest student-side effect, but whether it helps or harms the operator side depends on how plates are paid, and the team cannot move that.

#### The scenario model

The model explores how the system might respond and makes no predictions. It replays Kadamba's April 2026 breakfast records (confirmed: 32,162 registration rows with scan times), setting what each rule would have cooked against what was eaten, arrival by arrival. Kitchen forecasts use only earlier records, so the backtest covers 23 out-of-sample days (8 to 30 April); student-side scenarios use the whole month. Money is the posted rate times counted uneaten registrations, and no food cost or waste weight is used. "Surplus" here means portions cooked beyond what registered students ate, which is not waste in the strict sense. Demand is censored, since on a run-out day "eaten" is a floor and an unadjusted forecast would drift down, and the ledger's run-out rule corrects for that.

The inputs are a 70 per cent first batch per line (tested from 60 to 80, single-sourced); posted rates of Rs 48 veg and Rs 66 non-veg at Kadamba and Rs 53 at Yuktāhār (confirmed as posted, use for April assumed); a forecast equal to the mean of earlier same weekdays; arrival profiles from earlier same weekdays, with the crest 5 points lighter to 10 heavier in the stress runs; a 15-minute top-up (30 in a gas shortage); an allowance of about 30 outside diners plus 0 to 30 staff; skip uptake of 25 or 50 per cent of no-shows (tested from 10 to 75, assumed); opt-in turnout of 60, 72 or 85 per cent; eaters held at records (plus or minus 10 per cent, and 25 per cent for exams or a favourite item); and adoption on assumed dates. The records cover one hall, one meal and one month, and rest on the operator's own ratio.

Bakul's account differs on every kitchen input: a rush at 8:00 to 8:30 with 10 to 15 arrivals in the last ten minutes, cooking about 8 km away from around 3 AM for some items, an unmeasured top-up lead time, and turnout at most about 60 per cent (candidate, possibly itself an upper bound). The arrival card, the 9:00 batch, the 1.5 cover and the thresholds of Scenario 4 apply to Kadamba only.

Decision: Run the model on Kadamba's records only and size nothing for Bakul, because Bakul has no records and a different supply chain, and a Kadamba rule applied there could trim a batch that no top-up can replace.

**Scenario 1: the kitchen rules, backtested.** The baseline cooks far ahead of demand, with the veg line at about 525 a day for 237 eaten. The ratio cut fails both ways: 138 of its 150 surplus portions are still veg, and non-veg, whose turnout reaches 59 per cent on busy days, runs short on 7 of 23 days, because one flat ratio cannot serve two lines 16 points apart. A calibrated single batch cuts surplus to 60 but moves the cost to the crest: 16.8 short a day, and on 26 April the veg line would have run out at 9:24, 45 short, which is L14 in reverse. Adding the late batch cooks 274 fewer portions than the baseline and leaves 91 surplus and 2.6 short, which the ordinary top-up covers. The effective ratio becomes an output, from 29 per cent (Sunday veg) to 67 per cent (Friday non-veg). [[tab:a10-model]] and [[fig:a10-scenarios]] give the results.

**Scenario 2: projecting the day from the morning.** A flat projection from the count at 8:00 assumes a quarter of diners are in by then, when only 13.5 to 22.5 per cent are, and it under-reads by 26.9 per cent on average (mean absolute error 34.8 per cent, range from minus 56 to plus 54). A weekday profile at 8:00 removes most of the bias, though the noise of a small early count remains (over-reads by 15.6 per cent, absolute error 28.6). Reading at 9:00 halves the error again (over-reads by 2.6 per cent, absolute error 13.4, range from minus 23 to plus 70), which is why the trigger is set at 9:00, with the card rebuilt monthly.

**Scenario 3: one Sunday on the veg line.** Across three backtest Sundays the baseline cooked about 563 for about 190 eaten, a surplus of about 373, which is 2.5 to 3.4 times what was eaten across ratios of 60 to 80 per cent. The refined design would cook about 233, leaving about 45 surplus and about 2 short ([[fig:a10-sunday-veg]]). As the week's largest single surplus block, Sunday veg is the natural pilot slice.

**Scenario 4: the student side and the default.** In April, Kadamba students were charged for 20,221 breakfasts they did not eat (confirmed): about Rs 7.4 lakh veg and Rs 3.2 lakh non-veg, Rs 10.6 lakh in all (projected). Fewer registered plates cost the vendor only if it is paid on them (unconfirmed). If the kitchen keeps its ratio, opt-in starves the crest, since the ratio assumes 30 per cent of bookings go uneaten: 70 per cent of a confirmed count leaves each line about 23 short on Sundays at 72 per cent opt-in turnout and about 65 short at 85 per cent, nearly all after 9:15. A skip is less exposed. A kitchen keeping a 70 per cent ratio stays safe on Sundays down to 82.3 per cent uptake on veg and 67.2 per cent on non-veg, and every day down to 66.1 and 38.0 per cent. At a 60 per cent ratio the Sunday thresholds fall to 72.5 and 49.0 per cent, and at 80 per cent they rise to 89.7 and 80.9 per cent. A Sunday skip is therefore unlikely to short the crest below about two-thirds uptake on non-veg, even if a quarter of skippers still eat. An every-day skip at 50 per cent uptake runs non-veg short on 7 of 30 days, about 1.4 a day, rising to 3.0 if a tenth of skippers still eat and 8.9 if a quarter do. The Sunday skip can go on its own fixed date, and the every-day skip waits until the kitchen reads eaters on every line.

### Testing Interventions against System Objectives {#sec-a10-eval}

The objectives belong to the system and not to any one stakeholder: notice its own cost, cut the first batch without trading waste for run-outs, leave no one worse off, and be usable. Four gates make a concept ineligible whatever its score ([[tab:a10-gates]]).

Table: The four gates. {#a10-gates cols=22,56,22}
| Gate | Test | Source |
|---|---|---|
| G1 Absorbers kept | Resale, reuse, outside-diner and staff meals, walk-ins, late service and outside venues all survive; only the over-cooked batch, reheating and the bin are targeted | P5 |
| G2 Safety kept | Every step of the food-safety sequence stays intact | P4 |
| G3 Aimed at structure | Neither asks students to behave better nor treats operators as the source of waste, and is not gated on an unknown that could reverse it | P3; LP10 held |
| G4 Quick fix paired with the fundamental fix | Any kitchen lever is paired with a route, default, goal or belief lever landing on a date independent of kitchen performance | Shifting the burden; P5 |

### Deliverable 5: The Final System Design Recommendation {#sec-a10-rec}

#### The recommended intervention

**Route and read the gap together, open the Sunday exit on a fixed date, and calibrate the kitchen to buy time.** The kitchen bundle runs alongside, labelled the quick fix wherever it is reported. Stage 1 (weeks 0 to 4) builds the ledger with its charge line, runs the co-design tests, holds the joint reading at week 4 and takes the goal and indicators to CFS and CDS, while the cards and sheet are drafted with the Kadamba supervisor. Stage 2 (weeks 5 to 8) proposes the Sunday skip to the rule owner at week 6, through student representatives and with the plate-basis question, while the Kadamba Sunday veg pilot runs the card, allowance, 9:00 batch and sheet and Yuktāhār re-anchors. Stage 3 (weeks 9 to 20) puts the skip in force from about week 10 if adopted, takes the every-day decision at week 14, hands over from week 16 and ends at week 20. Billing and payment basis (LP10) and an outcome owner (LP9) are held until their facts are known ([[fig:a10-sequence]]).

![The recommended sequence, with decision points and handover.|Source: authors' design (planned).](phase-3-4-rebuild/Assets/a10-sequence.png){w=100 #a10-sequence}

Bakul, a later hall outside this pilot, is a third regime with no records, and the Kadamba model does not transfer. The recommendation extends there only once its scan export gives weekday turnout and arrivals, the call-to-counter lead time is measured, an item-level record backs the weekly review, any kitchen lever cuts waste without raising shortage risk, and, for the Sunday exit, its payment basis is confirmed.

Decision: Pilot on Kadamba's veg line on Sundays, size nothing for Bakul until it has records, and plan the team's own withdrawal at week 20. The pilot sits where three levers meet, the Bakul model has no data to rest on, and a team that stays becomes the thing the system depends on.

### Key Findings {#sec-a10-findings}

1. The system has its measurements and no routes for them. The enabling lever (place 6 of 12) routes existing records to the kitchen and the rule owners, and a student team can prototype it now as a paper ledger.
2. The loops form a shifting-the-burden structure. Kitchen levers are the quick fix. Booking, goal, belief and route levers are the fundamental fix, which the recommendation runs on fixed dates whatever the kitchen pilot shows.
3. Cutting Kadamba's ratio to 50 per cent fails on both counts, and a calibrated first batch alone moves the cost into the crest. Adding a 9:00 late batch brings surplus down with a residue the ordinary top-up covers (projected). The design fails loud when eaters exceed the forecast, so it depends on the raw reserve and the stop rules.
4. Kitchen levers leave the student cost untouched, and only a rule that returns something moves it. A Sunday skip is unlikely to run the crest short below about two-thirds uptake on non-veg, so it can start on a fixed date now. The every-day skip waits on its own decision rule.
5. Kadamba and Yuktāhār show that a learning practice is possible under the same rules. The halls differ in five other ways yet leave a similar breakfast gap, so the cause stays a candidate reading. Targets are anchored outside each hall's past and paired with portion, quality and run-out measures, and no lever may cut waste by raising shortage risk.
6. Every modelled figure is Kadamba's. Bakul, the third regime, has no records and needs its own scans, a measured top-up lead time and an item-level record before any lever can be sized. Sunday breakfast on Kadamba's veg line is the first test slice.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a10-decisions}

The decisions of the activity are recorded where they were taken. The first draft of 27 September had seven concepts (A to G), three gates and eight weighted criteria, and recommended "Route, calibrate and stage, then open the Sunday exit", with the kitchen bundle in the first two stages and the Sunday skip proposed in the third. It scored the refined bundle at 80 per cent, ahead of the single concepts. A check against the model outputs and the sources followed, and the model was re-run on every number. It corrected five points: a capped late batch, which the text had mentioned and the model had not run, was added to the model and restated as 21 portions saved for 2.7 more short; the share of diners by 9:00 in Prototype C was corrected to 39.4 per cent; the share before 9:15 in Prototype B to 54.7 per cent and the 173 cooked against 179 eaten were stated; the Sunday concept was made consistent across its two scored forms, with opt-in fairness lowered to 2; and the bundle's score for booked-against-eaten was lowered from 3 to 2, which moved it from 84 to 80 per cent.

The revision of 28 September against the course material on leverage and intervention changed the design. The recommendation was re-sequenced so that the fundamental fix (the route, the joint reading and the Sunday skip proposal) runs on fixed dates whatever the kitchen does, and its title changed to the one used in this chapter. Concept H, the gate G4, the criterion O9 and the architecture path were added. Four weights changed and one was added, and the scores moved accordingly: A fell from 74 to 68, B from 65 to 58, G from 58 to 48, and the two bundles tied at 79. The co-design plan, the sensitivity and stress runs (Scenario 6, which moved the behaviour-over-time projection to Scenario 7 with ranges and added Scenario 8), the table of adaptations, the iceberg interventions and the monitoring after the pilot were added. The verification of this revision re-ran every scenario file with identical output and recomputed the weighted scores exactly.

The Bakul account of the same day changed the text and left the numbers alone. Concept B gained a check variant for Bakul, concept C became per hall, concept E was guarded by records and resident views, and F and DP4 were held with the plate basis recorded per operator. O2 is read with O3, O3 is read per hall with a run-out indicator, and O6 gained holding time and texture. Two planned tests (Bakul residents, the Bakul manager) and a simulated Bakul row were added, with the five conditions for extending the work to Bakul. All twelve scores were recomputed and the model outputs were byte-identical on re-run, so no score, weight, model number or core recommendation changed.
