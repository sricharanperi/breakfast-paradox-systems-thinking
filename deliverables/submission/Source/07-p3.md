# Phase 3: Identifying Leverage Points and Design Principles {#ch-phase3}

Phase 3 consists of Activity 9. Phase 2 left a diagnosis: the Breakfast Paradox is a stable equilibrium held by three confirmed structures (an automatic registration default with a narrow exit, billing at registration, and a measured gap that reaches nobody who sets the rules) and by a first batch that any kitchen can top up and none can shrink. Phase 3 asks where such a system can change.

## Activity 9: Defining Design Principles and Intervention Opportunities {#sec-a9}

### Purpose, Approach and Timeline {#sec-a9-approach}

The purpose is to identify where and how the system could be changed. The team added three methods that each produced a finding: an archetype diagnosis that orders the levers, a depth and feasibility ranking, and a check of every lever against the absorbers and stakeholders it touches ([[tab:a9-methods]]).

The evidence is Phases 1 and 2, Kadamba's April 2026 scan records, the Yuktāhār registers for September and the three operators' accounts. The activity was drafted on 27 September 2026 and checked claim by claim by a second reviewer.

Depth is counted on Meadows' (1999) twelve places, place 1 the deepest (transcending a paradigm), 2 the mindset, 3 the goals, 5 the rules, 6 the information flows, 12 the parameters; as some presentations number the scale the other way, every lever carries "place n of 12". Place 9 (delays) would mean flattening the closing crest, which no lever attempts. Places 11 and 12 appear only as settings inside a deeper lever: cutting Kadamba's 70 per cent ratio by decree would move cost from surplus to run-outs, and the cancellation cap (raised from five to ten in a gas shortage, back at five; single-sourced) changes only how many exits the default allows.

Decision: Count depth from the deepest place and write "place n of 12" on every lever, because the course material and the published scale number the places in opposite directions and mixed labels had crept into the first draft.

Decision: Rule out any lever that removes a mechanism protecting someone (resale, reuse of leftovers, meals for 30 to 50 outside diners and about half of Kadamba's staff, the walk-in buffer, late and back-door service) and treat only the over-cooked first batch, repeated reheating and the bin as fair targets, because a signal created by removing an absorber raises the cost of someone not at fault.

#### LP1: Build the missing routes

Of Kadamba's 32,162 April registrations, 37.1 per cent were eaten; Yuktāhār's September breakfast ran at 17.7 to 40.2 per cent against 56 to 89 per cent for lunch (confirmed). No route runs from any record to any rule owner, and Yuktāhār's books disagree on 3 September (77 eaten against 110).

#### LP2: Change the breakfast default and its exit

Registrations hold near 1,070 every weekday while turnout runs from 42.1 per cent (Tuesdays) to 27.3 per cent (Sundays) (confirmed). The four-day lead protects only dry goods and Kadamba's team has pushed back on the cut-off (single-sourced), so a student and operator coalition for a shorter lead is the first shared interest on record (candidate). Options, lighter to heavier: a one- or two-day lead, an evening-before confirm-or-skip, Sunday opt-in, no registration while off campus. A lower count shrinks Kadamba's batch only if the kitchen reads it; whether Skip Meal does so is an open conflict.

#### LP3: Operator goal: surplus is a cost

The purpose would move from "cook for every booking" to "feed whoever comes, at least cost" (place 3), with a numeric target inside L11 (place 8). The operators now state different purposes under the same rules: Yuktāhār quality, less waste and profit; Kadamba cooking to registrations "because it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it"; Bakul "there should not be a shortage of food, even if some wastage occurs" (all single-sourced).

#### LP4: A standing operator forum that runs its own trials

Practice separates the operators more than rules do: Yuktāhār's matching-week lookup (books photographed, confirmed), Kadamba's fixed ratio and Bakul's weekly judgement above a floor (both single-sourced). Yuktāhār's waste fell from August to December once weighing was blame-free (single-sourced, no figures); Kadamba keeps gram standards but no learning record, and the Bakul operator believes Kadamba fills 90 to 95 per cent where records say 37 (confirmed).

#### LP5: Record-calibrated first batch with a daily learning record

L11 closes at Yuktāhār, is a candidate instance bounded by a floor at Bakul, and is absent at Kadamba, where creating it is place 6 (strengthening it elsewhere is place 8). Kadamba's 70 per cent becomes 100 per cent for biryani lunch (single-sourced), so the ratio follows expectation, not a record. April turnout was 32.4 per cent on the veg line and 48.5 per cent on non-veg (confirmed), so the batch is about 2.2 and 1.4 times what was eaten (candidate arithmetic). The lever sets the batch as gram standard times expected turnout per hall, weekday and line, plus a named allowance for outside diners and staff and a float for bonda, puri, vada and idli, starting on the veg line with a one-page paper record read next day.

#### LP6: Crest-aware top-up and a staged late batch

At Kadamba 26.5 per cent of April scans land between 9:15 and 9:30, reaching about nine a minute by 9:29, and 9.2 per cent (about 37 a day) arrive at or after 9:30, the latest at 10:07 (confirmed). The operator's estimate of about 150 in the last ten minutes overstates this and may drive the hedge (candidate). Yuktāhār projects from the first half hour, when only 15.3 per cent of Kadamba's scans have arrived. The lever has three parts: a weekday arrival card telling the supervisor at 8:00 what share has come (place 6); a late batch started just after 9:00 (place 10, as it changes when stock is created); and a grace period after 9:30, always paired with the late batch.

#### LP7: A cost trigger for attention, carried on the oversight line

Menu fatigue was fixed through the Student Council, the Parliament and the CDS Committee (confirmed, L4), but the far larger breakfast gap never entered that pathway. The facility team tastes every Kadamba meal with two Prism staff (confirmed, observed) and never checks quantity. The lever is a standing threshold, such as cooked-against-eaten above a set level for several weeks, that sends the case to the Committee as complaints do, anchored to the stated goal, reporting run-outs beside surplus, and carried by one extra line at the tasting.

#### LP8: Operator voice in the menu

Kadamba's uttapam, set dosa and upma waste most while puri, bonda, vada and idli move well (single-sourced), yet the kitchen has no say in the menu. Bakul's manager knows what sells (pav bhaji, kachori with chole), but his view that residents would eat more of familiar pairings is untested (single-sourced). The lever is a standing operator slot at each two-week menu cycle to report item waste and run-outs, building the missing route L16. It is read with consumption records and residents' views, so it stays a voice, never authority.

#### LP9 and LP10: the two held levers

Decision rights are split at least five ways, across registration, billing, menu, cooked quantity and the academic calendar (confirmed), and whoever sets registration and billing bears none of the cost. LP9, a named owner of booked, cooked and eaten, would put the consequence where the decision is made and inherit LP1; Yuktāhār's Wastage Register keeper is a lighter version. LP10 rests on a precedent: attendance-tracked billing ran during a gas shortage and some holiday and fest periods, and worked (confirmed).

Decision: Hold LP9 and LP10, and every concept that depends on them, until the head of CDS and the payment basis are known, leaving them unscored as recommendations, because a billing change made without the payment terms could reverse the operator incentive.

#### LP12: Change the goal the system actually seeks

The CDS poster gives the institution's goal as minimising food wastage through advance meal planning (confirmed). The goal pursued in practice is the registration count: students are billed on it and Kadamba cooks on it (candidate reading), while the measure of the stated goal is recorded and read by no one. The lever makes booked-against-eaten and cooked-against-eaten, per hall and line, the real indicators, read with quality and run-outs, because a wastage goal alone could be met by running short.

LP5 and LP6 are the quick fix. They are worth doing because students eat the food saved and they buy time, but the ledger must label them so, since a better-run kitchen presented as the answer would end the search for the fundamental fix (the L17 risk). LP1, LP2, LP11 and LP12, with LP10 once released, are the fundamental fix, and LP12 answers the sharpest finding of the problem analysis, that the system chases the wrong goal.

Decision: Name LP5 and LP6 the quick fix and LP1, LP2, LP11 and LP12 (with LP10 once released) the fundamental fix, and require every report of a kitchen improvement to say so, because presenting the kitchen levers as the answer is the shifting-the-burden trap in miniature.

#### Ranking: depth and feasibility

LP1 ranks first though shallower than LP11, LP12 and LP2, because every other lever needs its input. LP11 is deepest but needs the ledger first. LP2 is the deepest rule lever not gated on the plate basis. LP12 is fourth because nobody able to carry it has engaged.

Decision: Rank by a stated composite of depth and feasibility and explain each departure from pure depth, so that LP1 leads although three levers are deeper.

#### The natural comparison

Kadamba and Yuktāhār share registration, billing and, as far as is known, tendered plate payment, yet Yuktāhār plans from records, re-cooks by run-out time and weighs blame-free on paper, while Kadamba cooks a fixed ratio ([[fig:a9-comparison]]). A learning practice is therefore possible under current rules.

Decision: Treat the Kadamba and Yuktāhār comparison as a candidate reading, since five other differences separate the halls.

### Developing Design Principles {#sec-a9-principles}

#### The design objective and the case for the status quo

The objective is a breakfast system in which registration matches each student's intent, the kitchen cooks to expected eaters, and the cost of any gap reaches whoever sets the rules. It does not mean ending registration, resale or all waste: registration gives the planning count, resale recovers money, and some surplus is the price of never running out. Over-booking and over-cooking should become noticed exceptions. The status quo persists because it pays each actor, so the levers keep what each values and change what drives over-booking ([[tab:a9-status-quo]]).

Table: The case for the status quo: what each actor gains and what change would cost. {#a9-status-quo cols=16,26,26,32}
| Actor | Status quo gives | Cost of change | Cost of not changing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | No decision; seat; resale | Nightly decision; less resale | Paying for uneaten breakfasts (63 per cent of Kadamba's April registrations); reheated food |
| Prism (Kadamba) | Revenue possibly on registered plates; few run-outs | Possible revenue fall; daily record | Food no one eats; reheating |
| Bakul operator | No shortage, via floor and reserve (single-sourced) | Check on the manager's judgement | Food held hot for hours, softening aloo paratha |
| CDS | Low workload | Ledger; portal changes | A goal it cannot show it meets |
| Staff, outside diners | Food from surplus | Meals budgeted | Meals stay a by-product |
| CFS | Predictable budget | Budget varies with consumption | Spending on food nobody eats |

CDS and the Mess Office gain most from low workload and own the rules the fundamental fix touches, so LP1, LP11 and LP12 must reach them before any rule proposal.
