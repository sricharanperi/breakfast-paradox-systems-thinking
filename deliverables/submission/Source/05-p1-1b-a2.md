## Activity 2: Identifying and Mapping Stakeholders {#sec-a2}

### Purpose, Approach and Timeline {#sec-a2-approach}

The second activity asks who participates in the system, influences it, benefits from it or is affected by it; its deliverable is the Stakeholder Map (Deliverable 2). [[tab:a2-methods]] gives the key activities and methods.

The lenses were student, social, food production, governance, money, technology, academic, sleep and time use, substitutes, campus infrastructure, waste, health, policy, informal systems, absent or sleeping actors, temporal and intervention.

Two team members drew first maps independently on 5 September 2026 and consolidated them that evening, splitting the Mess Committee from the Mess Office. Parliament records (6 September) and the facilities head's interview (13 September) added the mess seats and the structure of CFS and CDS; the operator visits and records of 26 and 27 September overturned first-version findings on vendors, lead time and who orders food, and the passes of 27 and 28 September rebuilt the operator side.

### Use Case {#sec-a2-usecase}

The system registers, bills and serves resident students in four messes (Kadamba, Palash, Bakul and Yuktāhār) through one platform; registration counts, with no reference to attendance, drive billing and cooked quantity. Bakul's caterer cooks at the Hafizpet kitchen and trucks food in.

### Formal and Informal Roles {#sec-a2-roles}

Each operator runs an organisation the institution does not see; the two on-site ones are opposites, and Bakul splits across two sites.

#### Formal roles inside the operators

Kadamba sits at the end of a chain from CFS to CDS to Prism, and inside Prism from an operations manager to a manager, a supervisor (the in-service quantity decider) and service staff (single-sourced); a staff recording adds a director above (candidate). About ten chefs work in the kitchen and up to fifty staff at the mess (single-sourced).

Yuktāhār has no hierarchy: everyone does every job and peers cross-check. ABC runs it through two project managers and about three site staff (single-sourced; revised from five). The in-charge keeps the accounts and Wastage Book, a cross-verifier checks the grocery register, a coordinator keeps registrations, the attendance register and a wastage board with a falling waste-share target, and the store function is nobody's dedicated job. A tasting pair checks every breakfast, and sixteen kitchen staff start at 5:30.

Bakul splits two sites: Hafizpet cooks, and the mess holds, serves and decides.

#### Informal roles

Mess Cell, Ping and the tool developers have no official standing and each does a real job: resale market, public-pressure channel, alternative registration interface. Yuktāhār's twice-daily meeting is the real forecasting institution, named in no institutional document, and absences are raised there for lack of a leave policy (single-sourced).

#### Roles that are not separate stakeholders

Attending, skipping, having an early class, selling on Mess Cell and using an unofficial tool are states one student moves through in a week; peer influence is a relationship inside the Students node, so "friends" are not a stakeholder.

#### Dependencies

Students depend on operators for food quality and timing; operators depend on no individual student. Operators depend on the Mess Office for counts four days ahead and on the contract for payment, but procure on their own cadence; the count sets how much cooked food is prepared, since it lasts three to four hours. The Office's one confirmed policy change, motivated by workload (candidate), did not visibly come from the Committee.

Operators depend on suppliers for clean stock, on the garbage contractor and, at Bakul, on the road. The kitchen is about 8 km away, 20 minutes by road early and 40 in normal traffic (the first version had a 40-minute leg and 30-minute top-ups), and a top-up needs a call, cooking and the drive; the total lead time is unstated, so the 30-minute figure is a noisy-audio reading that transit alone makes doubtful (single-sourced). A late top-up would miss the close, a candidate reason for the reserve of about 50 at 9:20.

Vendors and students meet directly at the counter, which the first version denied (the sambar complaint, latecomers absorbed through doors operators cannot refuse, Yuktāhār weighing plate waste). No source links the Portal and IT owner to the Mess Committee.

One contract dependency matters most. Vijayalakshmi Caterers says it won a tender and is paid monthly on plates served, with deductions (single-sourced). If plates mean registrations, an operator cooking for well under all of them keeps the margin on every no-show and has no interest in closing the gap; if they mean meals served, Bakul bears the cost of over-cooking to avoid a shortage (candidate).

#### Influence

Operators hold high local influence over how much is cooked, when to top up and whether a second counter opens, responding within 10 to 30 minutes (each Bakul top-up adds a 20 to 40 minute drive), but none over the cut-off, billing rule or service window. The Mess Office holds the registration and billing rules and the only scan-level attendance data; the Committee holds menu authority and the final say on policy.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a2-decisions}

A long candidate list is hard to reason about; a short one may drop an actor who matters later. The first map treated the vendors as one actor; asking who decides how much food appears revealed three firms with three regimes.

Decision: Cut the stakeholder list from thirty-two to twenty-four on direct evidence, and keep the five minor and three excluded candidates on record with their reasons.

Decision: Treat the operators as their own cluster, keep the three firms separate, and keep every unconfirmed governance identity separate until someone with authority confirms it.

Decision: Hold each stakeholder's role, interest, need and expectation in one place, and model peer influence as a relationship inside the Students node.

Decision: Follow the course's stakeholder-map template for the deliverable's structure and add maps (relationship network, proximity map) only where they show something the template's map does not.

Decision: When operator visits contradicted a first-version finding about vendors, lead times or ordering, replace the finding everywhere and mark the revision where it appears.
