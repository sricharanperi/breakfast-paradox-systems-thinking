## Activity 8: Reframing the Problem {#sec-a8}

### Purpose, Approach and Timeline {#sec-a8-approach}

Activity 8 converts the operational problem into a systemic design opportunity. It produces the Reframed Systemic Problem Statement (Deliverable 4 of Phase 2) and the Design Opportunity Statement (Deliverable 5), which says where to intervene and stops before designing.

| Key activity | Method used | Reported in |
|---|---|---|

| Compare stakeholder perspectives | What each stakeholder feels and cannot see, side by side | [[sec:a8-perspectives]] |

The first version, written on 14 September, read as a consulting memo that named a fix and an owner. The team rebuilt it on the corrected iceberg, the root causes, the power asymmetries and the tensions of Activity 7, adding two findings: the system is stable and not broken, and no single actor experiences the paradox as their own problem.

### The Reframed Problem Statement {#sec-a8-statement}

> The Breakfast Paradox is a stable equilibrium. The system holds the gap between registration and attendance in place because every actor's behaviour inside it is individually reasonable, and every consequence it produces is absorbed before it reaches anyone who sets the rules.
>
> Three structures hold it. Breakfast is booked by default: every student is registered automatically, holidays included, and can leave only through five cancellations per meal type per month, each made at least four days ahead, so the default cannot follow the decision a student makes on the morning. Billing fires at the booking, not at consumption, so the inaccuracy costs the system nothing and, if operators are paid on booked plates, the operator nothing either. And the gap is measured twice, in the CDS scan record and in the operators' own books, but neither measurement is routed to the people who set registration, billing and menu rules, whose decision rights are split so that none holds both knowledge and authority. Lunch runs under the same rules and mostly works, which makes this a breakfast problem.
>
> What follows is quietly absorbed. Resale takes the student's loss. The operator's discount on the booking, live top-ups, leftover reuse, outside diners and staff who eat the same food, and late-door service take most of the physical surplus. Holding and reheating turn some of the rest into lower quality, and the remainder leaves in the bin. Outside venues take the dissatisfaction and informal workarounds take the cultural mismatch. Most of this is useful and should not be removed. Together it keeps the gap from producing a signal loud enough to reach a system that responds to complaint intensity.
>
> No single actor feels this as their whole problem. The student feels a charge. One operator measures the gap every morning and has nobody to tell, another cooks to the count as the obligation it is paid for, a third adjusts weekly but never below a floor held against shortage. The office holds a statistic. The paradox is seen in aggregate only by the actors with the least authority over the rules.

The original question (why does registration not match who eats?) sits at the wrong layer. Beneath it is one mechanism inside authority the operator already holds: each operator converts the booking into a cooked first batch above turnout that can be topped up and never shrunk (Kadamba by a fixed ratio, Bakul by weekly judgement with a floor, Yuktāhār from its books). Bakul keeps no records, so every Bakul figure is the operator's own statement.

### Challenging Initial Assumptions {#sec-a8-assumptions}

Student and CFS Chair interviews, three operator interviews, a first-hand account, Yuktāhār's books and 11,941 Kadamba scans tested five assumptions.

**Students not showing up is the problem.** It is a symptom, and part of it is no problem: one respondent fasts by choice and others never had a breakfast habit. At Kadamba 48.5 per cent of non-vegetarian bookings were eaten against 32.4 per cent of vegetarian, and Sunday was lowest there and among the lowest at Yuktāhār (confirmed). Billing shows a considered choice and a forgotten booking identically.

**Students are careless or irrational.** Most are not choosing a booking: "Even if you want to eat breakfast or not, it is automatically registered. We don't have any opportunity to nullify it." The exit is narrow and early (confirmed). Resale makes sense when Skip Meal pays nothing, and skipping at 9:10 makes sense when popular items may be gone.

**Nobody has solved this.** The institution ran attendance-tracked billing twice, in a gas shortage and in holidays such as Felicity ([[sec:a7-c4]]). Students paid only if they ate. It worked and was switched off both times when the trigger passed, with no sign anyone evaluated keeping it. The scan data it needs is still collected daily (confirmed).

**Nobody knows how big the gap is.** It is measured in at least three places and never reconciled: 37.1 per cent turnout at Kadamba and about 28 per cent at Yuktāhār against lunch at 56 to 89 per cent ([[sec:a5]]). The CFS Chair's recalled 35 to 40 per cent holds for Kadamba and his 70 per cent matches lunch; the earlier gap of about 30 per cent is a lunch figure. Beliefs sit above records: Yuktāhār's site head says up to about 50 per cent, Bakul's operator believes Kadamba fills 90 to 95 per cent, Bakul's manager estimates at most about 60 per cent (candidate upper bound).

**This is one problem.** It is three structural causes plus one thing that is no problem, and a single fix would aim at the average of four different things.

### Identifying Contradictions and Tensions {#sec-a8-tensions}

Each side behaves sensibly given the structure around it ([[fig:a8-tensions]]). Student-level and kitchen-level tensions are in [[sec:a7-tensions]]; these add evidence.

![Ten tensions in which each pair is rational on both sides.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08r-tensions.png){w=100 #a8-tensions}

**Billing certainty against demand accuracy.** The standing model gives a certain revenue base four days out; the attendance-tracked model gives that up, and the institution chose certainty each time. The lead does not protect the kitchen: Yuktāhār buys groceries fortnightly and vegetables two or three times a week, Kadamba buys perishables the day before. The binding constraint is the cooked first batch, fixed by the safety sequence before any turnout signal (candidate).

**Operator knowledge against operator authority.** Yuktāhār plans in a daily meeting from portal bookings, the Wastage Book entry for the matching week, a weekday register and events, and dropped commercial prediction software because the data is in its books (single-sourced). It cannot touch the registration close, billing or menu, and exams reach it only if mentioned.

**One billing basis against three operator stances.** The plate payment basis is unconfirmed ([[sec:a8-gates]]). Kadamba cooks to the count because "it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it." Yuktāhār names lower cost and higher profit as goals. Bakul's manager says there should be no shortage even if some wastage occurs, and keeps food for about 70 per cent of registrations on mornings he calls very low (all single-sourced). Whether an operator produces information that could correct the rules follows its culture (candidate).

**The operator's discount against the late crest.** Kadamba's first batch of 70 per cent of registrations meets 37.1 per cent turnout: on April's average day about 530 vegetarian portions against about 245 eaten and about 220 non-vegetarian against about 153 (candidate arithmetic). Yet late students meet run-outs, since the crowd crests at close and 9.2 per cent of scans come after it through a back door the mess cannot refuse (confirmed; back door single-sourced).

**One rule against real variation.** One registration and billing rule covers four halls on three kitchen systems. Yuktāhār's first-half-hour projection under-reads the crest (candidate); Kadamba cooks biryani lunch at 100 per cent of registrations, so its ratio follows the dish (candidate).

**Transparency against gaming.** Posting the menu two weeks ahead fixed menu fatigue and, by the same office's account, let students plan their skipping; the same cycle lets Yuktāhār look up the matching week.

**Loud complaints against quiet costs.** The path that fixed menu fatigue has never carried the larger, measured gap; the facility team inspects safety and taste and never asks about quantity (candidate).

**One model against real difference, and two systems with nobody between them.** Fasting, regional habits and observance fold into the turnout number. The 8:30 class start and the breakfast window have no confirmed link between their owners; a first class ending near 9:30 could push students into the minutes the kitchen can least serve (candidate).

### Why the System Holds Steady {#sec-a8-equilibrium}

The gap persists neither because nobody solved it nor because somebody benefits. Every consequence is absorbed by something that works before it reaches anyone who sets the rules ([[fig:a8-equilibrium]]).

![Why the gap holds steady: three forces create it, eight absorbers take up its cost, and only a loud signal reaches the rule setters.|Source: authors' analysis; the held and reheated food absorber is a candidate.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08r-equilibrium.png){w=100 #a8-equilibrium}

Resale absorbs the student's loss. The largest absorber is each operator's daily adjustment, which makes the gap a kitchen routine nobody upstream answers for. Yuktāhār reuses leftovers. At Kadamba about 30 portions are planned for 30 to 50 outside diners such as night security, and about half of the staff eat the same food (single-sourced), an uncounted sink (candidate); whether those portions sit inside the first batch or on top, and whether they are the 30 to 40 held for walk-ins, is unconfirmed. The reopened door absorbs part of the crest.

Most of this helps people, so any change to the first batch must plan their portions. Three absorbers protect no one (the over-cooked first batch, repeated reheating, the bin) and are legitimate targets.

One kitchen weighs waste item by item and records run-out times (confirmed), but this never enters a rule forum, and the system above the operator listens to complaint intensity, not records. Something already corrects locally: Yuktāhār cut waste between August and December by weighing every meal, discussing it daily and making weighing blame-free after staff feared blame (single-sourced, tracking confirmed). Kadamba has one recorded adjustment (ragi idli cut for plain idli) and no outcome record. Bakul adjusts weekly down to its floor. Nothing carries these fixes up to the rules.

### Alternative Problem Framings {#sec-a8-framings}

Six other framings were scored against four tests: why breakfast and lunch differ, why the problem persists unseen, whether it reaches the booking and whether it fits the evidence ([[fig:a8-framings]]).

![Six alternative framings against four tests; only the reframing passes all four.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08r-framings.png){w=100 #a8-framings}

"How do we get more registered students to attend?" (the original) aims at the least powerful. "How do we make registration a more accurate signal of intent?" fails because an automatic registration cannot express intent. "How do we help kitchens forecast better?" meets operators who already forecast: the best-instrumented believes up to 50 per cent where its register says 28. "How do we get every operator to run like Yuktāhār?" leaves the default, billing and the missing route untouched.

### Validating the Reframing with Evidence {#sec-a8-validation}

The statement was tested against every starting assumption, and most were revised or overturned ([[fig:a8-assumptions]]).

![Assumptions tested against evidence: what held, what changed and what is open.|Source: authors' analysis of records, interviews and field accounts.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08r-assumptions.png){w=100 #a8-assumptions}

Seven pieces of evidence support the framing. (1) A fix is possible: the attendance-tracked model ran twice and closed the gap, on scan data captured daily. (2) The booking is a default, confirmed three ways: the poster's rules, a student's account and Kadamba bookings flat across weekdays. (3) The gap is large and measured in two halls from records. (4) It is a breakfast problem: Yuktāhār's lunch runs at 56 to 89 per cent under the same rules on days breakfast runs at 18 to 40.

One question limits the framing: what a plate counts for in each operator's payment. Bakul's manager says plates served and Kadamba's remark points to booked plates (both single-sourced); no contract has been seen. The answer decides who bears a no-show; if the terms rule out an attendance basis, the fix moves from adoption to renegotiation. ### What Remained Missing {#sec-a8-missing}

Open items were stated and not merged: the plate basis of payment, whether Skip Meal changes what Kadamba cooks, who heads CDS, the registration cadence (operators say weekly blocks, students monthly), the base of Kadamba's waste figure, whether outside diners are registered, whether holding and reheating can be observed, why Skip Meal is used so little, where waste goes after collection, and the absence of lunch, dinner and other-hall records ([[app:open]]).

### Design Opportunity: Where the Leverage Is and Is Not {#sec-a8-leverage}

The second deliverable rests on operator interviews at three messes, Yuktāhār's photographed books, Kadamba's April 2026 scans and a first-hand account of two visits. The Bakul account added three secondary areas: food quality as eaten, demand by item and the menu's pairings. [[fig:a8-ladder]] reads five layers against the evidence.

![Where intervening pays: five layers of the iceberg read against what the evidence shows.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08d-leverage-ladder.png){w=100 #a8-ladder}

At the event layer, reminders and campaigns hit the most visible and least movable layer. At the parameter layer the cancellation cap has moved from five to ten and back (confirmed), leaving the default alone; lowering the first-batch ratio by decree moves cost from surplus into run-outs. At the structural layer Kadamba's registrations held at about 1,070 a day every day in April while turnout ran from 42 to 27 per cent; Yuktāhār's are not flat (228 to 404 a day in September, confirmed).

### The Design Opportunity Statement {#sec-a8-opportunity}

> The opportunity is to give this system a way to notice its own cost.
>
> The Breakfast Paradox persists because the gap goes unmeasured, although it is measured daily, and because the fix is unknown, although it has been run twice. The measurements, in scan records and operators' books, reach no one with authority to change the rules that produce the gap. Every consequence is absorbed before it becomes visible, and the institution's attention triggers on complaint intensity, and this problem is quiet.
>
> The opportunity is to build the missing routes: from the records that exist to the kitchen decision that fixes how much is cooked, and from the same records upward to a decision about registration and billing. Four intervention points follow. One sits in the kitchen, where a registration becomes a cooked quantity. Three sit in governance, in decreasing depth: the registration and billing rules, the ownership gap and the attention mechanism. The last is the cheapest and most overlooked, and would have surfaced the CFS Chair's own figures years ago.

### Validating the Opportunity against Evidence {#sec-a8-opp-evidence}

Three claims carry it. First, the gap is large and specific to breakfast: 37.1 per cent at Kadamba and about 28 per cent at Yuktāhār against lunch at 56 to 89 per cent on the same platform (confirmed from records).

Second, a private conversion step turns an inflated count into surplus. Kadamba's batch is roughly 1.9 times what is eaten, about 750 portions against about 400 (candidate). Bakul's manager sets 70 to 80 per cent by item each week and never below about 70, above the highest turnout he expects (candidate).

Third, the measurement exists and is stranded. The scan export holds one row per registration with a timestamp (confirmed). Yuktāhār's books record per item what was issued, cooked, left and wasted, with run-out time, and issued must match cooked or the mismatch goes to the daily meeting (confirmed; [[sec:a4]]).

Operators hold the most outcome data and the least rule authority, and items they see wasted repeatedly (uttapam, set dosa, upma at Kadamba) have no route to the menu. Under the same rules the gap is a cost Yuktāhār acts on, the institution's concern at Kadamba, and at Bakul the accepted price of avoiding a shortage (all single-sourced). The opportunity reads as a voice and a better input at Yuktāhār, may not be wanted at Kadamba, and at Bakul is a check on a judgement already made, acceptable only if run-out risk does not rise.

### The Four Intervention Points {#sec-a8-points}

The four points are opportunities; Activity 9 works out what each would look like ([[fig:a8-fourpoints]]). The first acts closest to the waste and the other three on the governance that sets its conditions. Three secondary areas rank below them.

![The four intervention points on the chain from booking to bin.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08d-four-points.png){w=100 #a8-fourpoints}

#### 4.1 On process: how a registration becomes a cooked quantity

The first batch is the point of no return, and the data to calibrate it exists. Yuktāhār shows the loop can run in-house under current rules: staff first resisted ("they are catching us"), managers made weighing blame-free, and by December staff reported waste had fallen (single-sourced). The opportunity is to feed recorded turnout by hall, weekday and line into the decision that sets the first batch, the evening before or at the planning meeting, keeping each kitchen's top-up path. At Kadamba this can build on the gram standard multiplied by expected turnout per line instead of 70 per cent of registrations; a first-half-hour projection will understate a breakfast that crests at close (candidate).

#### 4.2 On the rule: what a registration is and what triggers a bill

Registration is automatic, including over ordinary holidays (confirmed), so which holiday periods ran without compulsory registration is open. The opportunity is a standing, partial version of the attendance-tracked model, using scans already collected; whether they are retained per student is a candidate, since the export has no identifier. The rule has three parts: what a registration is (a default for every student), how a student leaves it (capped cancellations and a Skip Meal toggle that tells the kitchen not to prepare and removes no charge, confirmed) and what it costs (a charge fixed at the booking). Changing the default or widening the exit makes registration carry more of the morning's demand; changing the billing trigger alone moves who bears a no-show and does nothing for the count.

#### 4.3 On decision rights: who owns the outcome

Decision rights are split at least five ways: the Mess Office sets registration and cancellation rules (operators say "CDS team"; whether it is the same body is unconfirmed); the institution sets billing; students and the Mess Committee choose the menu; each operator decides privately how much to cook; the academic administration sets a calendar that reaches kitchens by word of mouth. No actor holds both knowledge and authority, the confirmed reason a proven fix was never picked up. At Kadamba the chain runs from CFS to CDS to Prism to a supervisor deciding within a meal how much more to cook (single-sourced; [[sec:a3]]).

The opportunity is one named owner accountable for the booked, cooked and eaten outcome end to end, with a standing route for operators' data. It is no new committee: existing escalation structures work when something reaches them, and what is missing is an actor for whom this is their problem. The third secondary area is the menu's pairings: Bakul's manager says the problem is pairings, not dishes (Raj Kachori with aloo curry, not chana; single-sourced), untested with residents.

#### 4.4 On information flow: what gets noticed

High-intensity feedback goes to a committee and low-intensity feedback to the CFS head; both respond to loudness and neither to cost. The opportunity is an escalation trigger that fires on measured cost alongside the complaint trigger. It is the cheapest of the four by a wide margin and would also catch the next quiet but large problem. The unlocking step is routing and a shared definition.

### What Any Intervention Must Respect {#sec-a8-guardrails}

The evidence imposes eight constraints ([[fig:a8-guardrails]]).

![Guardrails: what to keep, what to target and what to leave alone.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08d-guardrails.png){w=100 #a8-guardrails}

**Do not remove an absorption mechanism to manufacture a signal.** Making resale harder or ending access to leftovers makes the problem visible by making people worse off, and a smaller first batch takes food from the outside diners and staff unless it provides for them by name. The bin benefits no one, and holding and reheating, which the team judged Kadamba's main quality problem (candidate), absorbs surplus at the cost of what students eat.

**Act before the first batch is cooked.** Cooking starts at 5:30 at Kadamba and Yuktāhār and around 3 AM for Bakul's labour-heavy items. At Kadamba the batch passes a safety sequence before any diner arrives ([[sec:a4]]).

**Reduce waste without raising the chance of a shortage.** Run-outs are the failure students feel directly, and one operator states the rule: "There should not be a shortage of food, even if some wastage occurs." A cut that raises shortage risk fails students and will not be adopted by an operator holding that rule (candidate). At Kadamba the operator's rule of thumb is about 150 diners in the last ten minutes, and after 9:30 latecomers enter by the back door and cannot be refused.

**Do not treat turnout as the success metric.** Part of the gap is students eating the way they live. The measure is the gap between cooked and eaten, read with run-outs and food quality as eaten. Waste falling while run-outs rise is no success.

**Do not treat the 8:30 class start as the lever.** The timetable solves real problems and is a fixed boundary. Nearly 60 per cent of Kadamba's April scans fall at or after 8:45, so class timing is not the only driver of late arrival.

**Do not aim the fix at students or at operators.** Students hold the least power and the most defensible behaviour, and "register more accurately" asks them to solve a structure they did not build. Kadamba's manager mirrors the blame (students register "even if you are in the village", single-sourced).

**Keep measurement blame-free and work with the records operators already keep.** Routes that carry waste figures upward must not ground deductions on the staff who record them. Yuktāhār dropped a commercial system because the data was in its books and staff are not digitally inclined (single-sourced), so any route starts from paper books and the daily meeting, and at Bakul from the manager's weekly review.

**Preserve what already works.** The Student Council to Committee pathway produced the menu rotation, whose two-week cycle makes Yuktāhār's matching-week lookup possible (confirmed). Yuktāhār's books are a working calibration loop, each top-up is the only same-day balancing loop, and Bakul's weekly adjustment is a second calibration bounded by its floor. Whether Skip Meal declarations reach kitchen forecasting is unresolved: the CFS side says they do, Kadamba's operator says it cooks on registrations whether or not people want to eat (single-sourced), and no operator named Skip Meal as a planning input; both hold if Skip Meal lowers the count the operator reads and the operator treats it as its obligation (candidate).

Tensions travel with the design: Kadamba's stated waste does not square with its first batch, and surplus that looks like waste is also the meal of outside diners and staff; a late reserve protects diners from a run-out and is the food held longest.

### How the Four Interact {#sec-a8-interact}

The order matters ([[fig:a8-interaction]]).

![How the four intervention points fit together: cheap and enabling first, deep and gated last.|Source: authors' analysis.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08d-interaction.png){w=100 #a8-interaction}

Information flow (4.4) enables the rest and needs no new data. Process (4.1) shows a physical effect fastest and can move without governance where the operator has a reason, as at Yuktāhār; at Kadamba it is coupled to the plate-basis question and alone reduces surplus inside a system that still over-books. Ownership (4.3) turns a visible number into a decision.

### The Questions That Gate the Design {#sec-a8-gates}

Two questions decide the shape of the deepest point ([[fig:a8-gates]]).

![The two gating questions and what each answer would change.|Source: authors' analysis; both questions are open.](phase-2-rebuild/Assets/s08d-gates.png){w=100 #a8-gates}

The first is what counts as a plate for payment. Bakul's operator won a tender and is paid monthly as a lump sum on plates, with deductions, and its manager says plates served (single-sourced); Kadamba's operator cooks to registrations because it is paid for them, pointing to registered plates.

The second is who set the four-day lead and the five-cancellation cap, and what depends on them. Kitchens buy perishables one to three days ahead and Kadamba's team has challenged the four-day close. If nothing operational depends on it, the lead is habit or billing convenience, and moving it or widening the exit is the nearest-term version of 4.2, though Kadamba also finds last-minute registrations a pain (single-sourced).

### What Phase 3 Inherits {#sec-a8-inherits}

Activity 9 receives four intervention points ranked by depth, constraints that rule out whole categories before design (acting after the first batch is cooked, removing food from outside diners and staff, cutting waste in a way that raises shortage risk, penalising the people who record waste, depending on a new digital system, aiming the fix at students or operators), three secondary areas, a sequencing logic and two gating questions. The planning horizon of [[sec:a5]] is the timeline any intervention works inside.

The evidence limits travel with it. Record-level turnout exists for Kadamba breakfast in April 2026, Yuktāhār in September 2026 and two Yuktāhār Wastage Book sheets from June 2026 only. The Kadamba ratio and gram standards are the operator's own statements and carry self-presentation bias; Bakul's figures come from a team member's synthesis of its manager's account, with no records; each hall was visited once.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a8-decisions}

Decision: The team rejected its first reframe, which named a fix and an owner, and rebuilt it as a systems statement of a stable equilibrium, changing the driving question from why the gap has not been fixed to what would make anyone fix it.

Decision: Activity 8 was split into the Reframed Systemic Problem Statement and the Design Opportunity Statement, and the causal loop work was labelled as the second deliverable of Phase 2.

Decision: Six alternative framings were scored against four tests and only the reframing was kept; the weaker ones stay in the record so their rejection can be reviewed.

Decision: The opportunity stops before designing interventions and hands Phase 3 a shortlist filtered against the evidence, with the reasons obvious directions were ruled out.

Decision: The four intervention points are ranked by depth and sequenced by cost, and three secondary areas (food quality as eaten, demand by item, the menu's pairings) rank below them.

Decision: No absorption mechanism is removed to create a signal; the three absorbers that protect no one (the over-cooked first batch, repeated reheating, the bin) are the legitimate targets.

Decision: Measurement must be blame-free and start from paper books and the daily meeting, not a new digital system, and turnout alone is not the measure of success.

Decision: The 8:30 class start is not treated as a lever, and the fix is aimed neither at students nor at operators.

Decision: The plate basis of operator payment is the gating question and is held open, and conflicting accounts (Skip Meal, the head of CDS, the registration cadence) are stated side by side and not merged.
