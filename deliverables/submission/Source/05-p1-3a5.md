## Activity 5: Analysing System Behaviour over Time {#sec-a5}

### Purpose, Approach and Timeline {#sec-a5-approach}

The activity asks what pattern appears when the process repeats, across people and across time. It replaced the assumed typical morning with measurements wherever records existed.

### Examine Feedback Loops and Recurring Patterns {#sec-a5-loops}

#### R1: shifting the burden through Mess Cell

R1 is an open chain, held as a candidate reinforcing loop that sustains the dysfunction and does not grow it. None of the six respondents who addressed Skip Meal has used it (the one who explained why said it returns no money), and four of six have used Mess Cell in some form.

![R1: Mess Cell as a shifting-the-burden loop; dashed links are unstated inferences.|Source: student interviews; authors' analysis.](phase-1-rebuild/Assets/d10-loop-r1.png){w=72 #a5-r1}

#### B1: kitchen waste control

With no respondent using Skip Meal, the kitchen-side balancing loop might not close. It does, but the operator closes it, and each hall runs its own loop with a different delay ([[fig:a5-b1]]).

Yuktāhār sizes each item in a twice-daily meeting from registrations, the matching Wastage Book page two weeks earlier, the weekday register and the month's turnout. On seeing 18.7 per cent breakfast attendance on Sunday 6 September, the site head said the quantity would be cut "not next month, next week" (confirmed); the delay is two weeks and it acts on surplus and shortage. Within the meal, the first half hour projects the rest (single-sourced) and a written run-out-time rule triggers re-cooking (confirmed), with a delay of minutes, upward only. Kadamba cooks 70 per cent of registrations and tops up in ten to fifteen minutes (single-sourced); nothing recorded shrinks the next first batch.

![B1: balancing channels in the kitchen; the Skip Meal link is unresolved (dashed amber).|Source: operator interviews and Yuktāhār's books.](phase-1-rebuild/Assets/d11-loop-b1.png){w=76 #a5-b1}

Kadamba's loop closes against shortage only; Yuktāhār's first channel and Bakul's review close against surplus, Bakul's by one person's judgement with no known record. None is calibrated to actual turnout.

Whether Skip Meal reaches Kadamba's kitchen is an open conflict. An earlier finding was that declarations reach the kitchen for same-week preparation; the operator says it cooks for the number registered, "because it is a responsibility and they are getting paid for it" (single-sourced). The two fit only if Skip Meal lowers the registration count the kitchen sees, which is unconfirmed.

#### B2: capacity redistribution

B2 is weak or broken. One respondent registers for the whole month because "Kadamba is hard to get and it gets full": evidence of scarcity-driven concentration on the popular hall and none of redistribution to Bakul or Palash.

#### Same meal, two failures

The candidate that preparation is calibrated to the low attendance figure, leaving real attendees under-served, fails at the aggregate. Kadamba's first batch is 70 per cent of registrations, about 750 portions a day in April, while 37.1 per cent ate, about 398, so the first batch alone is roughly double what is eaten (70 per cent single-sourced, the doubling a candidate inference).

Kadamba's operator puts waste at about 5 to 10 per cent a day, with the base unstated (per meal or day, by weight, plate waste or kitchen leftover; single-sourced). That is in tension with a first batch about twice what is eaten. Either the surplus is absorbed before it is counted, by 30 to 50 outside diners, about half the staff, reheating and smaller top-ups, or the percentage uses a different base; this is not resolved.

#### Recurring patterns

Turnout falls every Sunday at both halls with records (Kadamba 25 to 33 per cent, Yuktāhār 18 to 24) while registrations stay put (confirmed). Bakul's manager describes very low weekend breakfast too, yet keeps food for about 70 per cent of registrations then (single-sourced).

The three kitchens use three forecasting regimes: a fixed ratio with counter reaction at Kadamba, learning from books across a two-week cycle with a within-meal projection at Yuktāhār, and a weekly judgement bounded by a floor at Bakul. They state three stances: Kadamba cooks for registrations because it is paid to, Yuktāhār names lower cost and higher profit alongside quality (candidate reading), and Bakul's manager says a shortage is the failure to avoid and some waste its price (single-sourced).

Operator beliefs drift from operator records. At Kadamba the belief about timing is close to the records and the gap lies in the level of turnout. Yuktāhār's site head believes breakfast turnout is at most about 50 per cent, beside about 28 on average (18 to 40) in his own register.

### Decisions, Trade-offs and Revisions {#sec-a5-decisions}

Decision: Freeze the evidence at a stated date, write up what exists and list what is missing (Bakul records, months other than April and September, student identity, exam windows), instead of waiting for completeness.

The record-level evidence arrived late, in batches, and two students had said only 30 per cent of students turn up, a figure the records contradict.

Decision: Trust the records over recall, and use lunch at the same hall as the control for breakfast, to test whether the cause is the meal or the system.

Decision: Treat aggregate claims and individual trajectories separately and claim no semester-level trend, since respondents move in different directions and no multi-month record exists.

Decision: Keep a loop as an open chain whenever one link is only inferred (R1, same meal two failures), so nothing is called a loop without evidence.

## Synthesis of Phase 1 {#sec-p1-synthesis}

The five activities describe one system from five sides. Activity 1 found that the stated purpose, minimising wastage through advance planning, and the mechanism that serves it are different things ([[sec:a1]]). Activity 2 found twenty-four stakeholders with an operator cluster at the centre ([[sec:a2]]), Activity 3 authority split into pieces that do not touch ([[sec:a3]]), and Activity 4 that no kitchen cooks to the count ([[sec:a4]]). This activity measured what the others could only describe. 

Nine findings follow.

1. Registration is a default and not a demand signal: automatic registration, a narrow early exit, billing at registration, a Skip Meal that refunds nothing and a walk-in premium reward staying booked ([[sec:a1]], [[sec:a5]]).
2. Every kitchen guesses its own conversion and every guess is too high; the three differ in method and none matches recorded turnout ([[sec:a4]], [[sec:a5]]).
3. The gap is measured twice and goes nowhere: scan records and operators' books both measure it, and neither reaches the owner of the registration and billing rules ([[sec:a3]]).
4. The binding constraint is the cooked first batch and not procurement; nothing shrinks a batch once it is cooked and approved ([[sec:a4]]).
5. Shortage and waste happen on the same morning, because a late crest meets a fixed first batch ([[sec:a5]]).
6. Academic administration is the clearest sleeping stakeholder: it holds the class start time, has no link to mess governance and reaches the kitchen only by word of mouth ([[sec:a2]], [[sec:a3]]).
7. Oversight and feedback change kitchens and not rules: institutional checks cover safety and taste and never quantity, and no confirmed instance exists of student input changing registration, billing or hours ([[sec:a3]]).
8. Surplus is absorbed out of sight: resale absorbs the student's loss; reuse, outside diners and staff, reheating and the bin absorb the food. Some absorbers feed real people, so any cut to the first batch must plan for them ([[sec:a4]]).
9. The most requested fix among respondents was a longer close, with a pay-as-you-go alternative to fixed registration proposed as a structural option ([[sec:a4]]).

Disposal after close remains unobserved and semester-level trends are unmeasured; these gaps and the corrections made during Phase 1 are collected in [[ch:synthesis]], and the next phase turns the findings into a model of the system ([[ch:phase2]]).
