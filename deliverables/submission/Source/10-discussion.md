# Discussion {#ch-discussion}

## Interpretation of the Main Findings {#sec-interpretation}

The central finding is that the gap between registration and attendance arises from a default that no one chose to keep, and that the gap is measured every day. This is the pattern that Meadows (2008) describes when she says that structure produces behaviour: sensible people acting on the signals they receive produce an outcome that none of them would design. The records strengthen the reading. The gap is about equally large at Kadamba and at Yuktāhār, which share the rules and differ in kitchen practice, so the registration structure explains it and the kitchen culture only decides how much of it becomes surplus. Yuktāhār, the one hall that uses its records to plan, shows the same gap. Learning in the kitchen therefore cannot close a gap that begins at the registration.

The finding that every working loop sits inside a kitchen is also a finding about information. The system corrects locally and has no correction at the centre, which is why a quick fix in the kitchen can make the central problem harder to see. This was the reason for fixing the Sunday skip to a date. The shifting-the-burden archetype (Senge, 1994) predicted the risk, and the gates of Activity 10 were written to guard against it. A related reading is that the system's absorbers, resale, reuse and outside diners, are individually kind. Each hides a cost, and the design rule that no absorber is removed to create a signal follows from that.

## Key Design Decisions and Trade-offs {#sec-tradeoffs}

The recommended design rests on trade-offs that were each a real choice between options with costs, and the principal ones are these: believing the opening description or doubting it (doubted and tested link by link); studying the whole mess or breakfast only (breakfast, with lunch and dinner as the control); one operator or three (three, because the differences were the finding); scoring the actor or the decision (the decision); many loops or few (few, each fully evidenced); one root cause or a structure (a structure of three parts and two conditions); the deepest lever or the one the team can move (rank by depth, then judge feasibility); testing only good ideas or including a foil (a foil in every test); letting the kitchen pilot decide the exit or fixing a date (a fixed date); and waiting for more records or writing with what exists (the evidence was frozen at a stated date). The reasons are those given in the activity sections.

## Implications for the Institution {#sec-implications}

The recommendation asks little of students and a good deal of the institution. The records that would calibrate every kitchen are already kept, so the first step costs a weekly sheet and a meeting. The second step is a decision for the rule owners, who today hold only fragments of the outcome: a Sunday skip that removes the charge. A third implication concerns ownership. No office is responsible for the whole chain from registration to bin, and the project holds back the proposal of a named owner until the payment basis is known, since that fact decides who has a reason to close the gap. The institution's own posters give its aim as minimising food wastage through advance meal planning. The analysis suggests that the planning the posters describe happens at the registration, which is not where the quantity of food is decided.

## Reflection on the Process {#sec-reflection}

The most useful steps of the project went to the kitchens and asked for records. For several weeks the team reasoned about the system from the student side and from the published rules, and the first honest look inside a kitchen changed more than any analysis had. With hindsight the team would go to the kitchens first, ask for the records sooner because they existed all along, and state how sure it was from the first document. That last habit allowed findings to be changed cleanly when evidence arrived. The project also showed that measurement and routing are different things. The system measures its problem daily and still cannot see it, because the measurement is not delivered.
