# Blueprint: The Breakfast Paradox presentation

Team Invictus, Systems Thinking and Design (PD2.405), IIIT Hyderabad. This document is the single brief for the builder. It covers the research, the visual identity, the slide archetypes, the full slide plan, and the production rules. Nothing here is slide text unless it sits under "On-slide text".

Reading guide: slide entries use these fields. Title (action sentence), Message (the one thing the audience should keep), Visual (what to draw), On-slide text (words beyond the title and beyond labels inside diagrams), Notes (speaker notes), Build (native or image, with file). "Live" marks the core path for a talk (L) or read-alone support (R).

Counts: 65 main slides, 4 appendix slides. 13 decision cards. About 38 slides are on the live path; with the decision cards left to read-alone except Decisions 7, 8, 11 and 12, a 25 to 30 minute talk runs about 30 slides.

## A2. Visual language distilled

- Slide types seen across the examined decks: title, question-only, single-image perception, definition in large type, loop diagram, iceberg, framework wheel, case template with takeaway box, chapter opener in a block colour.
- Colour: the strongest exemplars use few colours with a job (one colour per chapter in the Design Council report; two inks in the Murphy slides). The weakest repeat a logo bar and use many unrelated colours.
- Typography: one family, bold left-aligned titles, large type for single claims. Anything below about 12 pt (the habits list in exemplar 1) fails when projected.
- Systems diagrams on slides: loops are circles with a few nodes and the loop type named in the centre; maps survive only when cut into chunks (exemplar 5); icons in small circles make nodes recognisable.
- Decisions and trade-offs: the fixed card with a coloured takeaway (exemplar 3, and the d.school cards) is the pattern worth copying.
- Chaining a long story: a chapter colour or opener (exemplar 2), parallel phrasing (exemplar 4), and a presenter who guides the eye (exemplar 11). None of the examined decks has a progress indicator, which is a gap this deck fills.
- Text density: live decks run light on text but rely on the speaker; reports run dense and cannot be projected. This deck needs to be light on the slide and complete in the notes.

## A3. Principles for presenting this systems case study

1. One message per slide, stated as the title. Action titles (a full sentence that makes the claim) replace label titles such as "Mental Models". A reader who sees only the titles should be able to follow the whole argument.
2. Answer first. Show the headline numbers and the three recommended moves in the first three slides, then earn them.
3. Build a map across consecutive slides, not on one. Keep every element at the same coordinates, add one layer per slide, and grey the earlier layers. The bottleneck map (slides 31 to 33) and the equilibrium (slide 40) follow this rule.
4. Redraw dense diagrams, never shrink them. When a figure has more than about eight labels, redraw only the part that carries the message at 12 pt or larger. If an existing figure image is used, crop to the one panel in question and annotate it; keep the full figure in the appendix or speaker notes.
5. One chart, one highlight. A single colour shows the number the title names; the rest sit in grey; values are labelled on the marks and gridlines and legends are removed.
6. Identical template for repeating items. The thirteen decisions share one card, so the audience learns to read it once.
7. Chain with a motif and a progress device. A recurring picture (the plate) and a recurring problem (the missing link) tie the sections; a ten-dot indicator says where we are.
8. Show how sure we are. Small evidence tags (confirmed, projected, reasoned, planned) appear on any slide that carries them, so projections are never mistaken for results.
9. Keep detail for the reader. Full tables go to the appendix; the main slide shows the essential data as a picture. Speaker notes hold the full voice track.
10. Design for two uses. As a live talk, a speaker needs about 30 slides; as a read-alone deck, every slide must make sense without the speaker. Do this with sentence titles, direct labels, and notes that carry the reasoning. Use a few question-only slides (the opening perception slide, the closing questions) because they work live and cost little.

The deck alternates dark and light slides, and the alternation has a meaning: dark slides are openers and hero numbers (where the story turns), light slides are working slides.

Rules: role colours are used for their role only, never decoratively. White text on E65100 and on 1565C0 passes only at 14 pt bold or larger; on small text use the dark text colour on the tint fill instead. Chart series use the role colours; a single highlighted bar uses C62828, all others 9AA3B5 (a derived mid grey) or the role colour at lighter tint.

Evidence tags (small rounded rectangles, 9 to 10 pt caps, placed at the top right of the content zone): CONFIRMED (green fill), SINGLE-SOURCED (grey fill), CANDIDATE (amber fill, dark amber text), ASSUMED (amber outline only), PROJECTED (indigo fill, white text), REASONED (purple fill, white text), PLANNED (teal fill, white text), OPEN (red outline, red text). The word is always printed, so the tag never relies on colour alone.

## B2. Fonts

- Titles, big numbers, section numerals: Cambria bold.
- Body, labels, cards, footers: Calibri.
- Chart text (axis labels, data labels, legends): Calibri, set explicitly for every chart because chart fonts default to Arial in generated files. Arial is the fallback only.
- Sizes: opener title 30 pt; slide title 26 pt (24 pt if three lines would otherwise result; titles stay at two lines); big numbers 72 to 120 pt; body 16 pt; card text 14 pt; diagram labels 12 pt (11 pt only in dense matrices); captions, footers and evidence tags 10 pt (tags 9 pt).
- The diacritic in Yuktāhār must render: Calibri and Cambria both carry it.

## B3. Recurring motif: the plate and the missing link

Two motifs, both made of meaning and not of decoration.

1. The plate. A circle with a wedge for the 37.1 per cent that was eaten (orange E65100) and an empty ring for the 62.9 per cent that was not. It appears large and cropped at the edge on the title slide and on every section opener (always at the same position, bottom right, at a different crop), as the data on slide 24, and as a small bullet-marker for plate counts. Build with a native pie shape (angle range 0 to 133.6 degrees for the wedge) or a doughnut chart if the pie shape's angles are unreliable.
2. The missing link. A dashed arrow from "records" (purple, scan icon and register icon) toward "rule setters" (teal) that stops short, with a small gap and a broken-link icon in the gap. It first appears on slide 14, returns on slides 31, 40 and 41, and becomes a solid arrow with the label "weekly ledger" on slide 55. It is the same object on every slide: same colours, same dash, same gap. The payoff is that the audience watches the line get built.

Neither motif is a stripe, a rule under a title, or a side bar.

## B4. Progress device across the ten sections

Ten small circles in the footer, left aligned at x 0.5 in, y 5.28 in, 0.11 in across with 0.09 in gaps. Past sections are filled (indigo on light slides, white on dark), the current section is a larger circle (0.16 in) with a ring, and later sections are outlined only. Beside the dots, the current section's number and name in 10 pt muted text ("3 Understanding the system"). The slide number sits at the right margin. Section numbers follow the case study: 1 Background, 2 Approach, 3 Understanding the system, 4 Modelling the system, 5 Finding where to intervene, 6 Designing and testing interventions, 7 What the findings mean for the institution, 8 Limitations and open questions, 9 Reflections, 10 Questions for discussion.

Section openers (dark, first slide of each section) replace separate divider slides. They show the section numeral large in indigo tint (Cambria, 96 pt, top left), the section name (30 pt), the section's lead visual, and the cropped plate. This saves ten slides.

Bridge lines: the last slide of a section ends with a one-sentence question in its notes that the next opener answers; the opener's notes begin with that question.

## B5. Icon style

One icon family, solid (filled), one weight: Material icons through react-icons (the Md set), rendered as PNG at 256 px and placed in a filled circle of the role colour (0.4 to 0.55 in) with the glyph in white. Suggested glyphs (verify names when building): registration MdHowToReg, billing MdReceiptLong, scan MdQrCodeScanner, register book MdMenuBook, kitchen MdRestaurant, bin MdDelete, clock MdSchedule, calendar MdEvent, student MdSchool, operator MdEngineering, governance MdAccountBalance, route MdAltRoute, missing link MdLinkOff, confirmed MdCheckCircle, unknown MdHelp, warning MdWarning, money MdPayments, chart MdBarChart, loop MdAutorenew, ladder MdStairs, foil MdBlock, lock MdLock, handover MdHandshake, group MdGroups, tune MdTune, balance MdBalance. Do not mix in outline icons or emoji.

## B6. Layout grid (16:9, 10 by 5.625 in)

- Margins 0.5 in left and right, top 0.3 in.
- Title box: x 0.5, y 0.3, width 9.0, height 0.85, top aligned, left aligned, two lines at most, same position on every light and dark working slide. Section openers use a larger title (30 pt) at x 0.5, y 1.6, width 5.2.
- Content zone: y 1.3 to 5.0 (3.7 in high), 12 columns of 0.58 in with 0.185 in gutters (column n starts at x = 0.5 + n x 0.765).
- Footer zone: y 5.2 to 5.5: progress dots and section label on the left, slide number on the right. Nothing else.
- Evidence tag: top right of content zone, x 8.0 to 9.5, y 1.12.
- Decision tracker (decision cards only): thirteen 0.1 in circles right aligned above the title at y 0.12, the current one filled.
- Minimum 0.25 in clear space between any two blocks; nothing within 0.3 in of the slide edge except the cropped plate and full-bleed figure crops.
- Cards: rounded rectangles with 0.08 in radius, 1 pt border in the role colour, tint fill, no shadows. Left-align all body text; centre only numbers inside circles.

# PART D. The slide-by-slide plan

## D0. Story arc in ten beats

1. The puzzle (slides 1 to 6): students are registered and billed for every meal; about two thirds of breakfasts go uneaten; the brief blames the alarm clock; the team restates the question.
2. How we looked (7 to 11): a four-phase plan, evidence from students, kitchens and two record sets, and a label on every claim.
3. The system as it is (12 to 23): a boundary, 24 stakeholders, authority split into pieces, three kitchens with three rules, and records that never reach the rule setters.
4. What the records show (24 to 30): the month, the week, the morning crest, the lunch control, the first batch, and the beliefs that fell.
5. Why it holds (31 to 36): the bottleneck chain, eight absorbers, eighteen loops tested, and three structural causes.
6. The reframing (37 to 41): the first reframing is rejected; the problem is a stable equilibrium; the opportunity is to let the system notice its own cost.
7. Where to push (42 to 46): twelve levers on the ladder, seven principles, and one test slice.
8. What we built and tested (47 to 54): eight concepts and a foil, a paper prototype, a replay on April records, four gates, and the expected pushback.
9. What we recommend (55 to 60): three moves that run together, a 20-week sequence, a pilot with stop conditions, the team's own exit, and projections.
10. What it means and what we do not know (61 to 65): the institution's part, the limits, three open conflicts, the lesson that measuring and routing differ, and six questions.

The thread through all ten: the records exist, and the route to the rule setters does not. The missing-link motif is drawn broken from slide 14 and drawn solid on slide 55.

Conventions in the plan. Role by title only (students, operators, the head of facilities); the three team members and Prof. Raman Saxena may be named. Spellings: Yuktāhār, Palash, Kadamba, Bakul. "Rs" for money. No dashes of any kind in slide text or notes. "Per cent" in prose; "%" inside charts, big numbers and diagram labels. The notes are the voice track and may carry detail that the slide does not.

# PART E. Production rules and QA checklist

## E1. Build rules

1. Slide size 10 by 5.625 in (pptxgenjs LAYOUT_16x9). All positions come from the grid in B6. The title is at the same position on every working slide.
2. Text minimums: body 16 pt; card text 14 pt; diagram labels 12 pt (11 pt only inside matrices and the loop tiles on slide 33 and the ladder chips on slide 42); captions, footers and evidence tags 10 pt (tags 9 pt). Nothing below 9 pt anywhere. The appendix tables may use 11 pt.
3. No accent line under a title, no side stripes, no decorative bars, no gradient fills, no shadows. The only repeating graphics are the plate, the missing link, the progress dots and the evidence tags, all of which carry meaning.
4. Left align all body text and titles. Centre only numbers inside circles and big-number blocks. No justified text.
5. Titles: sentence case, at most two lines (about 100 characters at 26 pt in 9 in), no trailing full stop, no colon-led label titles, no slogans or antithesis headlines.
6. Colour: role colours only for their role (B1). Never encode meaning by colour alone: pair each colour with an icon, a word or a line style (dashed for unconfirmed or assumed).
7. Charts: native charts only (bar, column, doughnut, stacked bar for floating ranges). Set font face Calibri on axis labels, data labels, legends and titles; remove chart titles (the slide title does that job); remove gridlines and legends where direct labels exist; value axis starts at zero; one highlighted series or point in colour, the rest grey. If per-point colours do not apply to a single series, split the series.
8. Figures from the project: use only where the plan says so, cropped to one panel at no less than 150 dpi at placed size, with a native callout or highlight on top and no more than eight legible labels. All other diagrams are redrawn with native shapes and connectors, with shapes grouped in logical order.
9. Icons: Material solid in a coloured circle, rendered at 256 px; one style only.
10. Animation: none, except that consecutive slides 31 to 33 and 24 to 26 are built as separate slides at identical coordinates so that the "build" survives export to PDF.
11. Speaker notes on every slide: one or two sentences for the voice, then any detail the plan lists under Notes. Notes are plain prose. Notes carry the detail that the slide leaves out.
12. Accessibility: alt text on every image and icon cluster (a short phrase of what it shows); reading order title, message, visual, tag; contrast at least 4.5 to 1 for text (14 pt bold and above 3 to 1); no text over busy images; evidence tags always carry their word.
13. Footer on every slide: progress dots, section name, slide number, nothing else. Appendix slides use "For reading" in place of the dots.
14. Order: slide numbers as in this plan; appendix after slide 65, never shown in the main numbering.
